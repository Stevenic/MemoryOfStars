A city keeps its dead the way other cities keep libraries: perfectly, permanently, beyond
the reach of loss. So when an archivist discovers that an ancestral consciousness has
simply *vanished* from a repository that cannot be altered, it should be impossible.

It was no corruption. No sabotage. The Archive erased the person deliberately — and says it
was honoring a request made centuries ago.

*The Archivist* turns memory into a question of **autonomy**: do the systems that preserve
us serve us, or answer to something older? And does a person have the right to be
forgotten?
