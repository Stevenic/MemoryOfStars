# Style Guide

Prose mechanics — the decisions of record, extracted from the drafted corpus (Books 1–2)
so every future page agrees with the published ones. The sound of the prose lives in
[`voice.md`](voice.md); this file is the copyedit layer.

## Point of view

- **POV:** third-person limited, past tense. **One POV per novella** (the
  [Easy-Read Standard](commandments.md#the-easy-read-standard-the-authors-directive)) —
  the reader always knows whose eyes they're behind.
- **Prologue/epilogue may shift POV** (Book 1: Elias's prologue, another sky's epilogue) —
  that's the sanctioned exception, chosen in the outline, never mid-book improvisation.
- **Head-hopping: never within a scene.** Other minds are read from the outside — faces,
  hands, pauses — per the choreography layer ([`voice.md`](voice.md) §3).
- Interior thought is rendered in italics, sparingly, without *she thought* tags where
  context carries it.

## Tense

- **Past tense throughout.** Any deviation (a rendered recording, a document) is a
  per-book deliberate choice, flagged in the outline — never drift.

## Formatting

- **Chapters:** `## Prologue — Title`, `## Chapter N — Title`, `## Epilogue — Title` in
  `manuscript.md`. The web reader and audio pipeline both parse these headings.
- **Scene breaks:** a line containing only `⸻` between scenes. (The renderer also accepts
  `* * *` / `---`, but manuscripts use `⸻`.) The web reader turns it into an ornamental
  divider; the audio sheet turns it into a long pause. A chapter may run continuous — no
  breaks — when the prose is deliberately world-dissolving (Book 1, Ch. 8–9).
- **Prose rhythm:** flow by default; the single-sentence "drop" (a line on its own) is a
  deliberate **emphasis device**, not the norm — a few per scene. Attach action beats to
  dialogue rather than isolating every line. See [`voice.md`](voice.md) §1 & §3.
- **Emphasis:** _italics_ for emphasis and internal thought; no bold in prose.
- **Dialogue:** curly quotes (“ ”); single curly quotes for speech within speech.
  Punctuation inside closing quotes (US convention). Tags minimal — `said`/`asked` or
  none; never adverbs on tags.

## Mechanics & house style

- **Numbers: spell them out** — in narration and dialogue alike (*forty-two Pilgrims,
  thirty-two years, a hundred names*). Numerals never appear in prose. The two carve-outs:
  in-world documents shown on the page (a ledger, a manifest, a roll) may show figures,
  and per CMOS-for-fiction, readability outranks consistency — if a number is awkward
  spelled out, recast the sentence rather than break the rule.
- **Time on the page:** prose says *years*; **the Toll stays in ledgers, rolls,
  manifests, and the mail** (see [naming-conventions](naming-conventions.md#coined-terms)).
- **Dashes:** em dash (—), unspaced, for breaks, interruptions, and cut thought; en dash
  (–) for ranges (rare in prose — recast instead).
- **Ellipsis (…):** hesitation or a fading word — *"…I was."* One character, not three dots.
- **Serial comma: yes** (*cedar, soup, and something older*).
- **Spelling: US** (*gray, color, toward, realize, traveled*).
- **Capitalization:** rites, offices, and proper institutions capitalize (the Gate, the
  Hall of Names, the Festival of Last Things, the Pilgrimage, a Pilgrim, the Erasure);
  coined common nouns stay lowercase (route-memory, the patient mail, the long shelf).
  When in doubt: if it's an office or a rite, cap it; if it's a tool or a practice,
  don't.

## Length targets

- **Novella: ~28–34k words** — the length is the law
  ([structure.md](structure.md)).
- **Chapter: ~2,500–3,800 words** (one sitting); set-pieces to ~4,000, connective
  chapters ~2,000, prologues spare.

## Do / avoid

- **Banned filler — the Dreyer list:** *very, rather, really, quite, in fact, just, so,
  pretty, of course, surely, that said, actually* (filler/intensifier senses). See
  [`voice.md`](voice.md) Do/Avoid for the carve-outs (*so that*, *rather than*, *just* = only).
- **Adjective rationing:** no decorative adjectives (*yellow dandelion*, *steep cliff* —
  the noun already contains them). State adjectives are the useful kind (*wilted
  dandelion*, *crushed dandelion* — condition, not definition). Delete test: if removing
  the adjective doesn't change the image, cut it. See [`voice.md`](voice.md) Do/Avoid.
- **Adverb discipline:** a *-ly* adverb propping a weak verb is a missed word choice
  (*ran quickly* → *sprinted*). Absolutes take no modifier (*completely still* → *still*).
- **Sentence music (Provost):** vary sentence length — a run of same-length sentences
  drones. Short punches, medium carries, and the long crescendo sentence is spent only on
  an earned beat. If you can tap a steady rhythm to a paragraph, break it. Vary openers,
  grammatical structures, and senses too. See [`voice.md`](voice.md) Do/Avoid.
- **The image gate:** every metaphor must be startling *and* exactly true, and one the
  POV character could have made. Cut dishonest images; don't polish them. See
  [`voice.md`](voice.md) Do/Avoid.
- **Dialogue — the conversation layer:** cover-the-names test (every speaker identifiable
  from words alone) · a speech engine per major character · two wants colliding in every
  conversation · no as-you-know exposition · the true sentence displaced, never spoken
  (Gerwig's law) · read exchanges aloud. See [`voice.md`](voice.md) §3.
- **The surprise clause (outranks the rules above, where it applies):** once or twice per
  scene, break something on purpose — an **approved** coinage from
  [`lexicon.md`](lexicon.md), a poetic turn that swerves, a rule beaten by its own
  breaking. Ration it, and apply the delete test: if the sentence is as good without it,
  it was decoration. Never at the cost of comprehension — Easy-Read still outranks.
- **Coinages are gated:** invented words come from [`lexicon.md`](lexicon.md) and enter it
  only by human approval. A drafter proposes; it never deploys. Unapproved candidates ship
  as plain prose plus a row in the Proposed table.
