# The Lexicon — approved coinages and turns

The series' invented English: words and phrases that exist nowhere but this prose. They
are what keeps the voice from reading as assembled — a signature a reader recognizes
across thirty books without being able to name why.

> **Nothing on this page enters a manuscript until a human writer has approved it.**
> A drafter may *propose* a coinage; it may not *deploy* one. Unapproved candidates go to
> [Proposed](#proposed--awaiting-approval) and the prose ships plain in the meantime. A
> plain sentence costs nothing; a bad coinage costs the voice.

**This is not the glossary.** [`series-bible/glossary.md`](../series-bible/glossary.md)
holds *in-world* vocabulary — things the characters would say (route-memory, the patient
mail, the Toll), governed by
[naming-conventions.md](naming-conventions.md). This page holds *craft-level* invented
English — things the narrator does to the language, which no character would call a term.
If a candidate is a name, a place, an office, or a piece of in-world jargon, it belongs in
the glossary and the don't-reuse list, not here.

## The workflow

1. **Propose.** While drafting, when a coinage would earn its place, write the plain
   version into the prose and add the candidate to [Proposed](#proposed--awaiting-approval)
   with the sentence it was meant for. Never leave an unapproved coinage in a manuscript.
2. **Approve.** The author promotes, rewords, or kills. Promoted entries move to
   [Approved](#approved) with a usage budget.
3. **Use.** Approved coinages are reusable across books — that recurrence is the point.
   Respect the budget: a signature spent too often becomes a tic, the same law that
   governs gesture and adjectives ([voice.md](voice.md) §3, Do/Avoid).
4. **Retire.** Anything tried and found wanting goes to [Retired](#retired) so it is never
   re-proposed. Retired words are banned, like retired names in the gazetteer.

## What makes a candidate worth approving

- **Self-glossing.** The reader understands it without stopping. A coinage that needs
  explaining fails the [Easy-Read Standard](commandments.md#the-easy-read-standard-the-authors-directive)
  and is not a close call.
- **It does work no existing word does.** Apply the delete test: swap in the plain phrase.
  If the sentence is as good, the coinage was decoration.
- **It sounds like this universe** — worn, plain-rooted, tactile. Compounds of common
  words; a noun used as a verb; a verb used as a noun. Never ornate, never Latinate.
- **It surprises once and rewards twice** — startling on first read, obvious in hindsight.

**Auto-reject, no review needed:** anything with an apostrophe · anything needing a gloss ·
proper nouns and in-world terms (those are the glossary's) · modern or anachronistic
register · fantasy-generic compounds · anything that reads as clever rather than true.
