# Voice

**Version:** 1.0 · **Status:** Canonical (craft) · **Derived from:** the finished text of
*The First Pilgrim* (Book 01).

This is the concrete specification of how Memory of Stars *sounds* on the page — distilled
from real prose, with examples. Where [`voice-and-craft.md`](voice-and-craft.md) covers
broad craft principles, this file is the measurable sound of the series. It is what the
[critic](../.claude/skills/critique/SKILL.md) checks prose against.

> Governing spirit (from [foundation.md](../series-bible/foundation.md)): *ancient and
> optimistic, wonder before spectacle, curiosity before action.* The voice below is how
> that spirit is executed sentence by sentence.

---

## The voice in one breath

Spare, vertical, and quietly devastating. Short declaratives stacked one per line, plain
diction, tactile detail, deadpan wit in dialogue, and the uncanny rendered flatly — so the
emotion lands through restraint and accumulation rather than intensity.

---

## 1. The vertical line (rhythm & paragraphing)

The default unit is **one sentence per paragraph**, separated by a blank line. Each beat
gets air. This is the signature rhythm — a litany, a held breath.

> There had been six.
>
> There were always six.
>
> For a moment, she had thought she heard a seventh.

**Break the pattern deliberately, for two jobs only:**
- **Sensory/setting sweeps** — one longer, comma-rich sentence or a compact paragraph to
  ground a place, then return to the vertical line. *("The city slept below, scattered
  across the valley like fallen stars…")*
- **Lists rendered as single-word lines** for weight or ritual: *"Fold. / Press. / Turn."*
  · *"Food. / Blanket. / Rain cloak. / Cup. / Knife."* · *"One. / Two. / Three."*

Rule of thumb: if three consecutive paragraphs are all long, something is wrong. If nothing
is ever long, the prose goes airless — let one sensory sweep breathe per scene.

## 2. Definition by negation (the signature move)

The most identifiable device: state what a thing is **not**, then what it is. Keep the
turn on its own line.

> Mist rolled across the path.
>
> Not weather.
>
> Memory.

More: *"Not a touch. / The memory of one."* · *"Not in protest. / In disbelief."* ·
*"Not recognition. / The pain just before recognition."* · *"Not from the fire. / Warm as
though someone had only just stood up."*

Use it for the uncanny, for correction, and for emotional precision. Don't overuse it —
roughly once or twice per scene, never twice in a row.

## 3. Dialogue — deadpan and stichomythic

Fast, clipped exchanges, often one line each, frequently **untagged**. Wit comes from
literalism and understatement, not jokes.

> "You started without me," Lyra said.
> "I was starving."
> "You always say families should eat together."
> "I also say people should come downstairs when called."

- **Comic understatement:** *"It was a large apple."* · *"You returned two."* ·
  *"I appreciate skill."*
- **Characters answer the precise question, slightly off-axis** — especially Tomas
  (*"That is adjacent to the truth."*) and Seren (*answers a question with a flat "No."*).
- **Tags:** minimal — `said` / `asked`, or none. Never adverbs on tags. Let the line carry
  the tone.
- **Voice by character:** Lyra — curious, dry. Tomas — over-precise, deadpan, defended by
  data. Mara — warm, teasing, guarded. Elias — gentle riddles, *"I keep meaning it."*
  Seren — spare, careful, withholding. (Full cast notes live per book in `audio.yml`.)

## 4. Rendering the uncanny — flatly

Supernatural and cosmic beats are **underplayed**. No exclamation, no melodrama, no
narrator alarm. Dread is built by **repetition and counting**, then a plain statement.

> Voices overlapped.
>
> Memories separated.
>
> Facts dissolved.

The bells, the traveler-counts (forty-two / forty-nine / forty-three), the bowls — the
horror is arithmetic that won't reconcile. State the impossible in the same calm register
as the ordinary. Let the reader supply the fear.

## 5. Motifs & the canonical litany

Recurring phrases and gestures accrue meaning across the book (and series). Reproduce them
**verbatim** — drift breaks the spell. Track new ones in the book's `canon-ledger.md` and
[glossary](../series-bible/glossary.md).

- **"Come home carrying more people than yourself."** — the farewell. Exact wording.
- **Two fingers to the heart** — the farewell gesture; describe consistently.
- **The bells that don't stop at six** — a seventh/fourth note where none belongs.
- **"I don't know." / "You keep saying that." / "I keep meaning it."** — Elias's refrain.
- **"Memory asks what remains. Love decides what you carry."** — the thesis, spoken.
- **Counting as dread** (Tomas) and **the red bird / silver & red thread** as objects.

## 6. Emotional register — restraint

Grief and love are rendered **obliquely**. Don't name the feeling and don't linger;
stage an action or a plain line and move on.

> Tomas did not tell her the memory might return.
>
> He did not promise the answer no longer mattered.
>
> He simply stood beside her while she grieved it.

Earn sentiment, then undercut or exit fast. The biggest emotions get the *shortest*
sentences.

## 7. Punctuation & typography

- **Em dash (—)** for interrupted or trailing-off speech and cut thought: *"For now—"* ·
  *"I think you'll find—"* · *"Beyond everything else—"*.
- **Ellipsis (…)** for hesitation or a fading word: *"…I was."* · *"…Perhaps."*
- **Curly quotes** for dialogue; italics only for genuine emphasis/interior thought (rare);
  never bold in prose.
- **Scene break:** a line containing only `⸻` (see [style-guide.md](style-guide.md)). Some
  chapters run continuous with none (Ch. 8–9) — that's a deliberate choice for immersive,
  world-dissolving passages, not an omission.

## 8. Chapter landings

End on a short, resonant declarative — usually eerie or bittersweet, often reframing what
came before. The gut-punch is a *plain* sentence, not a flourish.

> He could not remember whether he had climbed it alone.

> something remembered her name.

## Do / Avoid

**Do:** one beat per line · plain, concrete diction · tactile nouns (winterleaf, resin,
wet spoon) · define by negation · underplay the impossible · minimal dialogue tags ·
land chapters on a quiet blow.

**Avoid:** purple/ornate description · adverb-laden tags (*"she said nervously"*) ·
explaining the mystery or the emotion · melodramatic reactions to the supernatural ·
paragraph walls · rhetorical questions from the narrator · modern/anachronistic idiom ·
drifting the canonical litany phrases.

## 30-second self-check

- [ ] Does the passage read as stacked single-sentence beats, with longer lines used only for sensory grounding?
- [ ] Is there at least one "define by negation" turn, and not more than two?
- [ ] Is dialogue clipped, mostly untagged, dry rather than jokey?
- [ ] Are supernatural beats stated flatly, with dread built by repetition/counting?
- [ ] Are recurring phrases/gestures reproduced verbatim?
- [ ] Is the emotion shown obliquely, not named?
- [ ] Does the section end on a short, resonant line?
- [ ] Does it earn one moment of wonder ([Law 7](../series-bible/seven-laws.md#law-7--wonder-comes-first))?
