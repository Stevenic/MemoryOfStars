# Novella Structure — the length is the law

**Version:** 2.0 · **Status:** Canonical (craft) · **v1.0 established by** *The First
Pilgrim* (Book 01) · **Amended 2026-08 (author directive):** the **length** is
constitutional; the chapter grid is a per-book choice.

What readers must be able to count on is **time, not architecture**: every Memory of
Stars novella is a 2–3 evening read, and every chapter is one sitting that earns the
next. That — plus the voice — is the series' real cadence. The 3×3 grid was Book 1's
shape, and for a while we treated it as a signature; the Cycle 1 re-draft showed the
cost: books with identical grids rhyme mechanically (three books peaking in the same
numbered chapters), and small stories get padded toward a shape they don't want. A
reader should recognize this series by how a chapter *feels*, not by counting sections.

## The contract (constitutional — never varies)

- **Total: ~28–34k words.** A 2–3 evening read — the
  [Easy-Read Standard](commandments.md#the-easy-read-standard-the-authors-directive).
  No book outgrows its slot; a story that wants more belongs to a later cycle, not a
  longer book.
- **A chapter is one sitting:** ~2,500–3,800 words. Set-pieces may run to ~4,000;
  connective chapters ~2,000; prologues run **spare** (Book 1's was ~420).
- **Chapter 2 detonates** — whatever the shape
  (the [Chapter-Two Rule](commandments.md#the-chapter-two-rule-where-the-hook-lives)).
- **Prologue and epilogue only if the story wants them.** The prologue is an oblique
  cold-open that plants mystery and tone; the epilogue is aftermath plus an *echo*
  forward — never a cliffhanger. (So far, every book has wanted both.)
- Every book passes **[The Writer's Test](../series-bible/seven-laws.md#the-writers-test)**.

## The grid (chosen per book, at outline time)

Chapter count and act grouping are the outline's to choose — and the outline header
**defends the choice in one line**. At one-sitting chapter sizes, the arithmetic yields
roughly **8–11 chapters**; that's an expectation, not a target. Shapes in use:

| Shape | Books | When it fits |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| **3 acts × 3 chapters** (the founding shape) | 01, 02, 05 | stories with three real movements — setup / deepening / cost |
| **3×3, re-pointed** | 04 | the peak moved to the midpoint; the back half is consequence, not contest |
| **2 acts × 4** | 03 | the held-breath single-hander — two movements, no room to wander |

The founding shape is always available and never owed. A finale may echo it
deliberately (Book 5 bookends Book 1) — a rhyme, not a rule.

## What each part does (when present)

- **Prologue** — oblique cold-open, often a different POV or time; plants the central
  mystery and the tone. *(Bk 1: Elias at the Gate. Bk 2: the Observation of the Lens.)*
- **Early chapters** — the ordinary world and the first cracks; the reader falls in
  love before anything detonates. Chapter 1 makes them care; **chapter 2 hooks them**.
- **The middle** — investigation deepens; the uncanny escalates; somewhere near the
  center the book turns (a reversal, a re-reading, or — Book 4 — the decision itself).
- **The late chapters** — the climax and its **permanent cost**, both budgeted
  generously (Book 1's outline starved the resolution and the draft corrected it —
  don't repeat the mistake in either direction).
- **Epilogue** — aftermath and the hook forward, as echo.

## Non-negotiables regardless of shape

- Scene breaks are a lone `⸻`; a chapter may run continuous (no breaks) when the prose
  is deliberately world-dissolving (Book 1, Ch. 8–9). See [`voice.md`](voice.md).
- Chapter headings: `## Prologue — Title`, `## Chapter N — Title`, `## Epilogue — Title`.

See also the [novella-outline template](templates/novella-outline.md); the five Cycle 1
outlines are worked examples of grids chosen to fit their stories.
