# A Hundred Empty Years — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #7** · **Target length:** ~29,500 words · **Structure:**
Prologue + 3 acts × 3 chapters + Epilogue — chosen: three real movements (the practice,
the secret, the night), with the full secret landing at dead center so the back half can
ask the harder question: *does forgetting count if you could take it back?*

- **Facet:** memory as **deliberate forgetting**.
- **Central question:** *Does forgetting count if it's reversible?*
- **The book's job:** Cycle 1 asked whether a people may delete its past (Book 4). This
  book lives inside the institution that answered *yes* and made it a calendar. It is
  the cycle's closed-room book — one small colony, one approaching date — and its
  tragedy is gentler and stranger than Meret's: not whether to forget, but whether
  their forgetting has ever been real.
- **POV:** Tilde — twenty-three, apprentice **Keeper of the Manual** in the colony of
  Sowen. Her private vice, since childhood: she keeps lists. Names of dogs, storms,
  bread prices. In a town that empties itself every hundred years, she is a rememberer
  the way other people are left-handed.
- **The spine in one line:** weeks before her colony's first great Emptying, its
  apprentice archivist finds proof that nothing was ever destroyed — the past is
  crated, sold, and *addressed* — and the office she is about to inherit is the one
  that must decide what a hundred honest years are worth.

## The threads
- **New truth:** deliberate civilizational forgetting exists as *practice*, not crisis —
  institutionalized, calendared, survivable. And its engineering detail: Sowen doesn't
  burn its past. It **exports** it. The Emptying is a shipping manifest wearing a
  liturgy. *(The universe now contains both kinds of forgetting: Meret's irreversible
  and Sowen's reversible — the series' corrective argued from the other side.)*
- **The founding, at midpoint:** Sowen was settled by Meret's children — the diaspora
  of the Mercy (Book 4, ~CR 180, never named beyond what the scene needs). The
  founders' one law, cut into the first crate: **never again unrecoverable.** They had
  seen what mercy costs when it cannot be unsaid. The colony's cover story ("we forget
  to prevent tyranny") is true — and it is the *second* reason.
- **The opposition at full strength:** Vorn, the colony's best teacher, discovers the
  export too — and his case is the book's engine: *we have never once forgotten; we
  have only looked away. We are not free of the past; we are its absentee landlords.*
  He wants this Emptying to be the first honest one: total, ash, unrecoverable. He is
  not wrong. He is Meret's argument, come back a century later wearing Sowen's face.
- **The office decides, not a vote:** Sowen's custom — the Emptying proceeds unless the
  Keeper refuses; one office holds the whole no. (Book 4 owns the referendum; this book
  deliberately does the opposite: the loneliest possible version of the same weight.)
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the contraband page *and* the worse discovery
  under it — her master already knew. The floor of the holy practice is false, and the
  people she trusts are standing on it comfortably.

## Characters in play
- **Tilde** — the apprentice Keeper; the list-maker. Her arc is from *keeping secretly*
  to *keeping honestly* — and out the door.
- **Ansel** — the Keeper, her master, old and kind and complicit; every Keeper finds
  the truth, he says. The office *is* the finding. What you do next is the test.
- **Vorn** — the reformer who wants real fire. Full dignity, real following, the
  book's second-best claim to being right.
- **Petra** — Tilde's mother, a wheelwright; the colony's ordinary life embodied — the
  freedom the practice actually bought, walking around in an apron.
- **Joss** — the guild courier who comes for the crates (a minor figure here; Book 8's
  eyes). One scene, one handshake, one manifest.

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — civilizational forgetting as sustainable *practice*; reversible
      deletion exists, with an address. *(Ch 1, 5, 8.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — the envelope dated a century out; what the founders knew that
      made them write the one law (never said); whether honesty will hold for the next
      hundred years. *(Epilogue, Ch 5, 8.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Tilde: secret rememberer → honest Keeper →
      emigrant; the girl who kept lists becomes the woman the lists are for. *(Ch 8–9.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the practice survives its own truth; Sowen becomes the
      universe's proof that forgetting can be *chosen honestly, on schedule*. *(Ch 8.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — the address implies the archive; the archive implies everyone
      else's crates. Where does a galaxy put what it cannot bear to hold? *(Ch 5 → Book 8.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Law 4** is the floor: the Emptying built Sowen's whole culture — no surnames, no
  statues, law without precedent — and the book shows the freedom as real before it
  questions the price.
- **Antagonist rule:** Vorn is the series' argument (Meret's) given full dignity;
  Tilde's win must cost — she keeps the practice by ending its innocence, and pays
  with exile.
- **Intentionally undefined, preserved:** zero canon spend. The Mercy is referenced
  only as the founders' history (one scene, standalone-safe: "a city that chose to
  forget, and what that cost"); no undefined question is touched.
- **No chosen one:** the office chooses, not the woman; anyone's apprentice year could
  have been the Hundredth. That it was hers is calendar, not destiny.
- **Easy-Read note:** the reversible/irreversible distinction is the book's one
  concept; teach it entirely through crates, fires, and manifests. No terminology.

## Continuity
- **Timeline:** **~CR 300** (founded ~CR 200 by the Mercy's emigrant children; the
  events of Book 4 are ~120 years past). Contemporaneous with Book 6, different world.
- **Echoes carried:** the Mercy and its diaspora (Book 4, one scene); "we keep it so
  no one must" inverted — *we send it away so we can be kind*; the sealed-thing-
  with-a-custodian shape (Book 4's room, now a fleet of crates); Tilde's lists (the
  plural record, domestic scale).
- **Hooks backward:** Book 6's one-line refused contract — the guild would not hold
  Sowen's route under a forget-clause; the courier line that *did* take the work is
  Joss's employer.
- **Hooks outward:** the Librarian (named, unvisited → Book 8); the envelope dated a
  century out (the patient mail, second appearance); Joss (Book 8's POV, met in one
  scene).
- **New names** (→ glossary via `canonize` when drafted): Tilde, Ansel, Vorn, Petra,
  Sowen, the Manual, the Emptying, Keeper (Sowen sense). *(Weight of names: Sowen
  keeps no surnames — the practice's quietest gift, never remarked.)*
