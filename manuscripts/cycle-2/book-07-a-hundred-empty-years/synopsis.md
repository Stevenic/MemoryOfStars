# A Hundred Empty Years — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The colony of Sowen empties itself every hundred years: no statues, no surnames older
than a generation, law without precedent, and a Manual of skills that is all any
generation inherits. The practice is taught as freedom — forgetting to prevent tyranny —
and the freedom is real. Tilde, apprentice Keeper of the Manual, is the town's quiet
anomaly: a reciter, keeping names of dogs and storms and bread prices nowhere but in her
head. Weeks before the colony's first Hundredth, she finds a page in the bindery that
predates the founding — and her master Ansel's face does the wrong thing: not surprise.
Recognition. Every Keeper finds the thread; the office *is* the finding.

She pulls it herself: freight manifests, a courier line under permanent contract, crates
that leave every Emptying and never come back. Reciting the manifests until they become
one pattern, she hears the address in it. In the Keepers' sealed annex the whole secret
lands: nothing was ever destroyed. The crates go to a buyer of last resort — an archive
called the Librarian — because Sowen's founders were Meret's children, emigrants of the
Mercy, whose one law was cut into the first crate: **never again unrecoverable.** The
Emptying is not a fire. It is a mercy with a return address.

Vorn, the colony's best teacher, finds it too, and his case splits the town: a hundred
empty years were a performance; the colony is not free of its past, only its absentee
landlord. He wants the Hundredth to be the first honest one — total, ash. Sowen's custom
gives no vote: the Emptying proceeds unless the Keeper refuses, and Ansel steps down on
the eve. The whole no lands on Tilde.

At the Hundredth she performs the rite in full and reforms it mid-liturgy by telling the
truth — *we do not burn; we send away; forgetting is our work, not our lie* — and Vorn
burns one crate: a decade of the colony, ash before the town's eyes, unrecoverable
against the founders' law, and no one will ever know what it held. It is his monument,
and it breaks his following; the town, having now tasted real forgetting, chooses the
practice with open eyes. A century — ten crates and an urn — rides light into orbit.
Then the practice's harsh hygiene, planted in the absence Tilde somehow never recited
(there are no old former Keepers in Sowen): whoever holds the address leaves with it.
She hands the Manual to her successor, makes the colony's first honest farewell, and
rides the crates out — writing her recitations down on the ship, for the first time in
her life. They are allowed now. At the port, the courier Joss signs for the consignment:
ten crates, one urn, one passenger. Among the papers, an envelope stamped with the next
Hundredth's date, a century away, is filed with the cargo. Patient.
