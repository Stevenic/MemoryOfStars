## Premise

Sowen empties itself every hundred years — no statues, no surnames, law without
precedent — and teaches that the practice prevents tyranny. It does. It is also not the
whole truth: the colony's past leaves by freighter, not fire. The book lives in the gap
between forgetting and looking away, and in the one office (the Keeper's) that must
decide what a hundred honest years are worth.
