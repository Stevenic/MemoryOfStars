# The Severed Reach — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The war between Corvane and Dresk is fought by moving beacons: treaty range is capped —
total lit hops are zero-sum — so the powers annex by silence, dragging chains until a
rival's systems fall out of hearing. Nothing is destroyed and nothing is provable, since
the provenance registry vanished into the Librarian's dispersal. Lio, a guild
route-broker, has done well off the dead routes and claims to deplore the war he
arbitrages — until he watches a severance complete, a settled system dropping out of the
audible sky mid-transmission, with Ru of the Selt beside him putting three settlements'
faces on the next one. The Selt's last hop is scheduled to be cut in sixty days. Lio
takes the job as a brokerage, fee on delivery, and pretends that's the whole reason.

The crew: Onna, a keeper of the Skerry's order who can raise a dead route from residue
and calls the job blasphemy while packing; Madge, a tug-pilot who treats a lighthouse
like any other barge; Quill, a forger with a philosopher's calm about the fact that
provenance is now a matter of opinion. They win a war-surplus beacon more or less
legally at the graveyard auction — then the war moves early, sixty days become nine, and
the only unseen road in is a dead one, which Onna recovers far outside her license while
the guild formally disavows them mid-transmission. The crossing is Law 1 with a
lighthouse on your back; halfway, the beacon's old station-voice wakes and starts
courteously logging them as *crew*.

The relight is the set-piece: the war's vacated placement going dark over empty space as
the Selt's sky comes back on — three settlements resolving out of silence mid-sentence,
a year of undelivered mail starting to move, a people reappearing in the audible galaxy
like a shoreline at dawn. Then the bill: the treaty cap makes the new hop payable only
by an equal surrender of range, and the sole clean tender is Lio's brokerage rights over
the crown route — the richest book of business in the reach — valid only with the
guild's countersign. The guild signs, disavowal notwithstanding: it legalizes the Selt's
hop, keeps its hands clean, and prices the favor at Lio's permanent delisting. He pays
with a joke, and the joke doesn't survive the signing.

The crisis forces the conference the war never had, and the Selt speak at it — because
now they can be heard. In the treaty's annex, one line of small print promotes a quiet
frontier world named Carath to relay-hub. The crew scatters lighter and poorer; Onna's
order writes the residue-craft into its liturgy at last; and the epilogue is the Selt's
first outbound transmission on the new hop, in their own plural, untranslated name —
a year of mail moving, a wedding heard by cousins three systems out, and somewhere in
the traffic, next year's almanac quietly updating itself: *answering*.
