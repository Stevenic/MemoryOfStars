# The Severed Reach — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #9** · **Target length:** ~31,500 words · **Structure:**
**No prologue — cold open.** 10 chapters in four movements (The Mark · The Crew · The
Move · The Price) + Epilogue — chosen: a heist wants to open in motion and land its
bill late; the chapters run short and punchy, and the epilogue is the invoice.

- **Facet:** memory as **reconciliation**.
- **Central question:** *What is a people worth if no one can hear them?*
- **Register — the cycle's romp (FIRM):** propulsive, witty, adventurous — the series'
  one caper. The cost still lands, all of it, in the fourth movement. (Book 2 was the
  detective romp; this is the heist. Same law: the wit buys the reader's guard down;
  the ending collects it.)
- **The book's job:** show the cycle's war — fought not with fleets but by **moving
  beacons**: territory measured in reach, alliances in hops, annexation by silence.
  Cutting a people's last transmitter makes them unhearable — the human-scale mirror
  of the thing the cycle is circling, done by *us*, on purpose, for lines on a map.
- **POV:** Lio — forty, a Wayfinders' Guild **route-broker**: the fixer who prices
  reach, trades hops, and has done well out of a war he claims to deplore. Prices
  everything; is about to meet the thing that doesn't price.
- **The spine in one line:** a war of moved beacons is about to sever a small people's
  last link to the audible galaxy, and the broker who profited from the silence
  assembles a crew to steal a lighthouse — and finds out what buying it back costs.

## The threads
- **New truth:** reach *is* territory. The powers annex by re-rigging the mesh —
  drag a beacon chain and a system falls out of hearing; no shot fired, nothing
  destroyed, a people subtracted from the conversation. The war's paperwork engine:
  the provenance registry is gone (released in the Librarian's dispersal — Book 8,
  never explained here beyond one line), so *no placement can be proven original* —
  every move is legal because nothing is provable.
- **The guild as casualty and broker:** its routes die when beacons move; its brokers
  get rich arbitraging the corpses. Lio is both halves in one coat, and knows it.
- **The heist inverted:** they aren't stealing a beacon *from* someone — they're
  stealing it *to* somewhere: the job is an illegal act of **placement**. The crime
  is giving a people back their voice.
- **The bill:** treaty math caps total lit hops; a new hop must be paid for with an
  equal surrender of range. The romp's last trick is that the trick was never free —
  peace, like reach, is bought with what you loved having.
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the sixty days — the severance scheduled, the
  auction announced, and Lio taking the job for money while the reader watches him
  pretend that's the whole reason.

## Characters in play
- **Lio** — the broker. Jokes when it matters most; his ledger-mind is the romp's
  engine and his arc is one unpriced entry.
- **Onna** — a keeper of the Skerry's order (Book 3's rite, institutional descendant),
  dour, magnificent, guild-illegal: she can recover a dead route from residue and
  considers it liturgy, not navigation.
- **Madge** — tug-pilot; flies mass for a living and treats a beacon like any other
  barge, which is the funniest and then most terrifying thing in the book.
- **Quill** — the forger; papers, provenance, and a philosopher's calm about the fact
  that since Book 8's dispersal, *everyone's* provenance is a matter of opinion.
- **Ru** — the Selt liaison; young, formal, learning mid-book that dignity and
  desperation can share a sentence.
- **The Selt** — the small people about to go unhearable: three settlements, one
  aging relay, no fleet, no vote at anyone's table. *(Weight of names: "Selt" is
  what the powers call them; their own name for themselves is plural and untranslated
  — kept off the page, deliberately.)*
- **Corvane & Vasse** — the two powers. Neither villainous on-page; both bureaucratic,
  both certain, both moving lighthouses like chess pieces because the other one did.

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — reach is territory; war by beacon-moving; unhearability as
      annexation. *(Ch 1, 2, 8.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — who moved first and why the powers *really* fear silence
      (never stated; the reader, holding Books 6–8, suspects the war is a symptom);
      the station-voice that logged a crew. *(Ch 7, 9.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Lio: the man who priced reach pays with his
      own; broker → the reach's first honest middleman. *(Ch 9–10.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the Selt precedent: placement-as-right enters the
      treaty language; the keepers' rite becomes an order's acknowledged duty. *(Ch 10.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — the almanac's small print; a war this size was a *border
      skirmish* by the reach's scale. *(Epilogue.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Law 1** is the floor: moving mass between stars is the whole war *and* the whole
  heist; nothing about the move is free, fast, or safe.
- **Law 4:** beacon-war has already reshaped culture — treaty math, hop-dowries,
  silence insurance; show it in furniture, not lecture.
- **Romp discipline:** the wit never mocks the stakes; the Selt are never comic; the
  cost is real and stays paid (the crown route does not come back).
- **Intentionally undefined, preserved:** zero canon spend. Why the mesh allows
  re-rigging, who built the beacons, what the war is a symptom of — all held open.
  Comms ≠ travel throughout; FTL mechanics unstated.
- **No chosen one:** the Selt are saved by a *contractor with a crew*, and it almost
  doesn't work. History from choices, priced.

## Continuity
- **Timeline:** **~CR 312.** The registry released ~CR 305 (Book 8); the war is the
  paperwork's echo, seven years on.
- **Echoes carried:** the keepers' rite (Book 3), employed and then honored; plural
  holding (Book 6's heresy, now the crown route's fate); the dispersal's long shadow
  (Book 8, one line); the series' farewell — *not* spoken; the Selt's untranslated
  plural self-name (weight of names, working).
- **Hooks outward:** Carath's promotion (→ Book 10's premise, cold plant); the
  station-voice that logs crews (toolbox — a future book may want it); the treaty's
  new placement-right (citable canon for later cycles).
- **New names** (→ glossary via `canonize` when drafted): Lio, Onna, Madge, Quill,
  Ru, the Selt, Corvane, Vasse, reach (formal sense), hop, the crown route.
