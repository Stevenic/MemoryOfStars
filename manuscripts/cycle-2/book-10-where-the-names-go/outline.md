# Where the Names Go — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #10** · **Target length:** ~31,000 words · **Structure:**
Prologue + 3 acts × 3 chapters + Epilogue — chosen: the cycle finale deliberately
echoes the founding shape (a rhyme, not a rule), and this story has three true
movements: the misprints, the taking, the holding.

- **Facet:** memory as **survival — made active: memory as defense**.
- **Central question:** *What can be saved while it is being taken?*
- **The book's job:** the cycle's capstone. Books 6–9 built a dread out of deniable
  pieces — a missing mile, a pattern in an archive, a war over hearability. This book
  stops denying: **the first Erasure event witnessed directly, in real time, on a
  thriving world** — and, in the same hard week, the first direct sight of the other
  side: the old defense, flickering awake. It also resolves the cycle's mystery: the letters
  from tomorrow, opened at last.
- **POV:** Ivet — twenty-nine, junior **registrar** of Carath's Hall of Rolls: the
  clerk who writes the names down — births, marriages, arrivals, the ten thousand
  small acts by which a city agrees it exists. She knows everyone's name and almost
  no one's face; ledgers are how she loves people from a safe distance.
- **The spine in one line:** on the world the war's re-routing made loud, the names
  begin to fail — off stones, out of records, out of mouths, in real time — and a
  registrar, a guild rite-book assembled from letters nobody sent yet, and a crowd of
  strangers must learn, in one hard week and the night at its crest, the only armor
  that has ever worked.

## The threads
- **New truth (the cycle's fragment, landed):** the Erasure is *active now* — seen
  happening, not inferred. And the mesh's scope pays off as horror's mechanism: the
  re-routing (Book 9) made Carath a hub — bright, singular, *loud* — and what is
  whole and loud in one place gets taken (Book 8's pattern, confirmed the hard way).
  Nothing about the Erasure's nature is explained. It is weather with a preference.
- **Names first:** it takes the *handles* before the things — signage, stones,
  records, then mouths mid-sentence. People remain; what fails is the ability to be
  referred to. (The book's precise, teachable dread — and the reason the registrar is
  the POV.)
- **The letters, resolved (scoped):** the guild responders' rite-book was assembled
  over decades from the patient mail — letters opened on their dates, each a plain
  warning or a page of practice (what held somewhere else, once), converging on Carath. The senders: **the network's own
  watchers** — the waking defense, using the mesh's one strange margin to post
  warnings *ahead of need*. Who and what the watchers are: **not answered** (→ Cycle
  3). They cannot steer; they can only mail. Most letters were misfiled. That, too,
  is the theme.
- **The defense flickers:** at the event's crest, Carath's oldest substructure wakes
  and *fights* — briefly, at visible cost, buying one night. First direct sight of
  both sides of a war older than every name in the book. What woke, why now, what it
  spent: enlarged, not explained.
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the counts disagreeing and then a name failing
  *mid-sentence, in a living mouth*. The cycle's whole dread, cashed in one scene at
  a kitchen-table scale.

## Characters in play
- **Ivet** — the registrar. Order is her tenderness; the book breaks her method and
  gives her a better one (names held in people, not paper).
- **Noor** — chief of the guild responders: calm, tired, carrying a rite-book she
  only half believes until the night it works. *(The guild as first responders —
  the institution's fifth face this cycle.)*
- **Marek** — Ivet's superior, Keeper of the Rolls; a man whose faith is the
  archive, watching the archive fail — Hale's argument (Book 8) living through its
  refutation with terrible grace.
- **The child** — brings the book's first wound: a grandmother's stone gone blank.
  Unnamed on the page until the Night of Strangers, when Ivet takes her name —
  and the reader learns it as Ivet does.
- **Seren** — among the volunteers: warm, minor, works three shifts and never
  sleeps; two survivors later describe him as two different men. No one notices.
  *(The continuity witness — powerless to intervene; watching is not keeping.)*
- **Carath** — the world, practically a character: young, proud, loud, promoted by
  the war to hub — one famous name, everywhere. *(Weight of names: its singularity
  is its exposure. The book never says so; the shape says it.)*

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — the Erasure is active now, witnessed; names fail before things;
      plural custody works (demonstrated, not preached). *(Ch 2, 5, 8.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — what the Erasure is (untouched); what woke beneath the Hall
      and what it spent; who the watchers are; where the names go — the title's
      question, pointedly never answered. *(Ch 6, 4, 8.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Ivet: from paper to people; the registrar
      becomes the first keeper of the plural rolls. *(Ch 7–9.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the plural-name custom is born; the responders'
      protocol is proven and will spread; Carath persists under new names. *(Ch 8–9.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — both sides of a war older than settlement, glimpsed;
      the watchers implied everywhere the mesh reaches. *(Ch 6, 4.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Canon spend: ZERO — verified.** The event is witnessed, never explained; "the
  erasure" appears only as the folk word (lowercase, Book 1's precedent). What it
  is, why storage is finite, where taken things go: untouched. The watchers are
  the *defense's* face, not the Erasure's, and stay unidentified.
- **Law 6:** no chosen one — Ivet is on shift; the armor is a crowd's, not a
  hero's; the defense wakes for its own reasons, not for anybody's destiny.
- **No cheap resurrection:** nothing taken returns — not the grandmother's name,
  not the famous name, not the flicker's spent heat. The eucatastrophe is what
  *held*, never what came back.
- **No prophecy / no puppeteer:** the letters are warnings that mostly failed to
  be read in time; the watchers cannot steer. Seren watches; nobody steers.
- **Temporal scoping:** the letters are a witnessed anomaly, never explained.
  Whether time is linear stays on the intentionally-undefined list — this book
  (and any later one) may *use* the mesh's margin, never explain it.
- **Counting discipline:** the counts-disagree device (Books 1, 5) is used once,
  at the detonation, then retired for the book — the horror hands off to *names*,
  which is this book's own instrument.
- **Easy-Read note:** one concept — names fail before things — carries the whole
  book; teach it in a kitchen, a masonry yard, and a registry counter before
  letting it touch the sky.
