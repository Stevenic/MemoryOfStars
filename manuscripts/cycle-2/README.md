# Cycle 2 — *The Scattered Light*

Books 06–10. The wide cycle: **worlds and the roads between**. Cycle 1 was rooms — a
town, a city, a station; this cycle is the space between them: the interstellar mesh as
everyday, engineered infrastructure, and the people who keep ten thousand scattered
settlements one civilization.

Its connective tissue is an institution rather than a place: the **Wayfinders' Guild** —
couriers, route-holders, brokers, and responders. Each book stands alone with its own
protagonist; the guild appears in a different role in every one (a holder's story, a
colony's contract, a courier's run, a broker's war, the responders' night). And through
all five, quietly, recurs the cycle's strangeness: **the letters from tomorrow** — mail
that arrives before it is sent.

## Central questions

1. **The Cartographer of Silence** — What happens to a world no one remembers the way to?
2. **A Hundred Empty Years** — Does forgetting count if it's reversible?
3. **The Last Librarian** — When is deletion an act of care?
4. **The Severed Reach** — What is a people worth if no one can hear them?
5. **Where the Names Go** — What can be saved while it is being taken?

## Why this sequence works

The cycle widens and then converges:

> **a road → a colony → the archive → a war → the night it all pays off**

Books 6–8 each raise one deniable wrongness (a missing mile; a practice with a false
floor; a library pruning itself); Book 9 shows what people do to *each other* with
silence; Book 10 collects every thread on a single world in a single night. By the end,
the reader should hold the cycle's argument:

> **Reach is existence — and what is kept whole in one place is not safe there.**

## Shared-canon discipline

As in Cycle 1: each book returns four artifacts to the trunk via its `canon-ledger.md`
(confirmed canon · cultural beliefs · open mysteries · continuity hooks). Books connect
through **echoes, recurrences, and handed-off objects** (a refused contract, a crate, a
registry, a promoted world) — never through cliffhanger continuations. Every book reads
cold, from page one.
