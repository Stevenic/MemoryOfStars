# The Falling Node — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Three centuries after the Mercy, Meret's Wells run warm, its rolls plural, its bridge
rebuilt and walked. Yune, ninth Warden of the Wells — keeper of the unopened room —
speaks her nightly rite through the door, the day's news told to a room that has never
answered. Tonight the door is cold; the institute's advisory arrives stamped:
*pattern consistent.*

Mid-rite, the southern Well-mouth dies — the first node-death of a working Well in
recorded history — and its district begins to thin. Yune performs the decrement herself
while the city watches her write. Relief comes — and with it Breda, old now, sack first.
Nodes have never been defended. They will be the precedent.

The defense deploys everything the series has built — relay-held rolls, incompleteness
doctrine cut into the Wells' own strata, names rehearsed to strangers nightly — and it
slows, but does not stop, the cooling. Doss, Yune's deputy, finally asks: the **casket**
— the Mercy's sealed copy, the most coherent object in the node — is a lure; open it,
read it plural, dissolve it into a thousand heads. The office's law says never.

Through the northern Well-mouth, the Custodian is witnessed: patient, exact, tending —
culling strata the way a keeper culls a flooding archive. And failing: it re-culls its
own work; its triage stutters.
The city coins the only name that fits — **the keeper's keeper** — and the institute's
hunger-frame dies. Then the finding: the plural holds, the singular is taken clean — and
the node itself, old and perfectly coherent, cannot be made plural in time.

The office ends correctly. Yune neither opens the casket, nor runs with it, nor burns
it: she carries it to the northern mouth and gives it, still sealed,
to the keeper's keeper — a warden's mercy to a failing caretaker: *"We keep it so no one
must,"* completed at last. The mercy buys nothing; survival was bought by the plural
armor. The Wells fall. The casket goes with them. The city does not.

Meret stands, the first city to survive its node, grieving infrastructure the way you
grieve a grandmother — and the keeper who keeps, gives: from forty years of contraband,
Breda surrenders her sole-source founding deed of the city's bridge to Meret's new
rolls, without ceremony. It is small. It is enough to build on.

The ledger's new edition reads Meret — *answering (relay-held)* — and Grier's preface
strikes out the hunger: *it maintains; it is failing; fighting it has failed.* The cycle
hands Cycle 5 its mandate: understand it. Yune speaks her last night rite to open air
where a door used to be — then, for the first time in three centuries, says the news to
the city instead. The wardens' rite goes plural.
