# The Memory War — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #17** · **Target length:** ~31,500 words · **Structure:**
**No prologue — cold open.** 10 chapters in four movements (The Strike · The Pattern ·
The Conspiracy · The Accords) + Epilogue — chosen: romps open cold in this series, and
this one is a thriller: the propulsion is dread with a deadline.

- **Facet:** memory as **weapon**.
- **Central question:** *What do you feed when you fire forgetting?*
- **Register — the cycle's romp (FIRM, confirmed at charter — scoped):** war-thriller
  propulsion. In the dark cycle, **momentum itself is the contrast** — not wit. The
  bill lands in the fourth movement and stays paid.
- **The book's job:** test the second faction's answer. Corvane and Dresk — the old
  beacon-war rivals, a century on and no wiser — have weaponized erasure itself:
  targeted forgetting as ordnance. The answer **fails informatively**: every strike
  feeds the real thing. The war is chumming the water.
- **POV:** Kest — thirty-one, Dresk's finest **strike-surveyor**: the officer who
  maps what a forgetting-strike will take before it is fired. She aims the war. Her
  competence is the book's engine and her guilt is its fuel — the series' first POV
  who has done real harm on purpose, and knows it.
- **The spine in one line:** the war's best targeting officer watches the true
  Erasure follow her strike-pattern in — and must conspire with her enemy
  counterpart to miss, falsify, and finally shoot her own maps, because the only way
  to stop feeding the thing is to end the war that feeds it.

## The threads
- **New truth:** erasure can be **weaponized** — bounded forgetting-strikes (rally
  names, command reputations, treaty texts unmade in enemy heads and archives; the
  Mercy's technology, militarized). And the cycle's second data point: **strikes
  attract the blanks.** Weaponized forgetting is bait. The war is feeding what it
  cannot see.
- **The old rivals:** Corvane and Dresk, round two (← Book 9) — a century of peace
  spent re-arming, and the new territory is memory itself. Neither is villainous;
  both are terrified; the arms race has its own momentum (Law 4: the weapon rebuilt
  the war).
- **The counterpart:** Ivo — Corvane's strike-surveyor, Kest's opposite number and
  mirror: two targeting officers who have spent five years studying each other's
  work and are the only two people alive who can read the pattern. The thriller's
  spine: enemies conspiring to miss.
- **The institute at the edges:** the Cartographers' war-observers log every strike
  and every blank; the ledger's overlay is the proof nobody wants — and the neutral
  channel through which two enemies can talk.
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the masterpiece strike and its echo — within
  days, a true blank arrives along the exact profile Kest drew. She recognizes her
  own signature in the weather.

## Characters in play
- **Kest** — the strike-surveyor. Precise, decorated, quietly rotting; her maps are
  works of art and she has begun to dream in them. Deflection: she narrates
  everything as targeting language — until the book breaks the habit at the worst
  possible moment.
- **Ivo** — Corvane's surveyor. Older, drier, further down the same road; he
  noticed first and said nothing for a year. His first message to Kest is a
  correction to one of her maps — professional courtesy as treason.
- **Brom** — Kest's commander. Decent, exhausted, institutionally incapable of
  hearing "our weapon feeds the enemy nobody declared." The arithmetic in uniform.
- **Observer Sette** — the institute's war-observer; keeper of the overlay; the
  neutral human channel. Counts strikes by day and blanks by night and has stopped
  sleeping between.
- **The Blackout Accords** — the treaty the book builds toward; a peace signed not
  from wisdom but from terror. The cycle's institutions on one page.

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — erasure weaponized; strikes attract the blanks; the ledger's
      overlay as proof-of-pattern. *(Ch 1–2, 8.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — why feeding *quickens* it (mechanism untouched); what the
      unfed base rate portends; several passes nobody can remember mattering.
      *(Epilogue.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Kest: from aiming to watching; the
      surveyor's oath re-sworn to the ledger. *(Ch 9–10.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the Blackout Accords: erasure-weapons banned by the
      first treaty written in the Erasure's shadow. *(Ch 9.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — both powers' signatures in the weather; the blanks' patient
      base rate under everything. *(Ch 8, Epilogue.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Romp discipline (scoped):** propulsion, not wit — the thriller's momentum is
  the dark cycle's contrast; nobody's grief is set dressing; the cost (the
  archive, the passes, the peace-of-fear) stays paid.
- **Law 4:** the weapon rebuilt the war — strike liturgies, targeting corps,
  audit culture; shown in furniture.
- **Canon-spend guard:** zero. *Why* strikes feed it is never explained; the
  selection rule stays unnamed (Book 18's); the blanks are tracked, never sourced.
- **No cheap resurrection:** nothing the war unmade returns — not the rally-names,
  not the passes, not Kest's archive. The Accords ban; they do not restore.
- **No chosen one:** Kest sees the pattern because she drew it. Authorship, not
  election.
