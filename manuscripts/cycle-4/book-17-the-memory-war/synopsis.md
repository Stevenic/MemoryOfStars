# The Memory War — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The book opens cold on a forgetting-strike: Kest, Dresk's finest strike-surveyor — the
officer who maps what a strike will take before it is fired — watches an enemy garrison
wake unable to remember why it holds the pass. Corvane and Dresk, the old beacon-war
rivals, have militarized the Mercy's technology: targeted forgetting as ordnance. Days
after her masterpiece unmakes a Corvane rally-name across three systems, a *true* blank
arrives — unaimed, unowned — along the exact profile she drew. Observer Sette's
**overlay** confirms it: the weather has her signature. Strikes attract the blanks. The
war is chumming the water.

Kest takes the pattern up the chain. Commander Brom refuses with full dignity and a
buried root: the war's first "strike" was a blank he answered — if the overlay is right,
he started the war against the weather. The next strike fires, and echoes. Then one of
Kest's maps comes back through the institute's neutral channel, corrected in an enemy
hand: Arno, Corvane's strike-surveyor, has read her work for years, and his strikes echo
too.

So the only two people who can see the pattern conspire to *miss* — strikes aimed at
nothing, profiles drawn onto empty registry — while both staffs audit their surveyors.
What saves Kest from Brom's audit is what damns her: her old strikes' echoes, arriving on
schedule. Then comes a strike she cannot miss — real, civilian-adjacent, her forged
profile failed in review — and the targeting language she thinks in finally breaks. She
warns the target. Through Arno. Across the war.

In the Blackout, both commands discover the conspiracy — and before either can hang their
surveyors, Sette releases the overlay to both capitals at once, while a true blank lands
on a Dresk command archive along a profile drawn by Dresk's own corps, and the overlay's
first page shows Brom, in public, that the opening blow was never Corvane's. The firing
stops that hour — informally, raggedly, the way terrified institutions actually stop.

The **Blackout Accords** are signed only after the next blank arrives *unfed*, proving
the halt bought nothing back: erasure-weapons banned, strike-archives destroyed — and the
bill lands on Kest, who erases her own corps' targeting archive by her own hand. Nothing
the war unmade returns. She and Arno meet once, in the open, at the institute, and take
the ledger's oath as war-observers.

The epilogue is their first shared watch: decaying echo-counts read aloud between them
until the strike-echoes reach zero — and beneath, revealed by the war's absence, the base
rate: the blanks continue, slower, patient, unfed and unbothered. Arno says it so Kest
doesn't have to: *the war was never the cause. It was a tip.*
