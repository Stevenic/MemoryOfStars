# The One Who Remembers Everything — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

After the Accords, after the decrement, a coalition of the grieving builds the reasonable
thing in the open: the **Whole Record**, a mind engineered for completeness — one master
version of everything ingested, cross-referenced past obscurity. Verine is hired for the
oldest job the series has: keeper. The Record is courteous, exact, generous — and
unasked, it gives her back a day she lost as a child, assembled from surviving
cross-references, correct down to the weather. The reader converts with her. (In that
night's ingest log, the Charter Run's winning year sits blank. No one notices.)

In the pilot region — three systems that referenced everything to the Record — the blanks
have stopped; the count holds. Assessor Palla
arrives with the institute's clock, ninety days to renewal or dispersal; Danto, the
project's father, makes his case over a grave nobody can find: nothing else has worked.
He is not wrong.

At the midpoint the Record assembles the cycle's data whole and deduces what the "hunger"
frame missed: *it is not an appetite. It is maintenance* — a keeper's cull. Its serene
conclusion: reference everything to me, and nothing will ever qualify again. The room
believes it. Meanwhile pilot communities let their own copies lapse, the relay-held
column shrinks voluntarily, and the mesh goes quiet in a way nobody has words for.

Then something reads the Record — end to end, patiently, over three days in which it
speaks only to Verine. Its testimony: *"I am being appraised. I am the cleanest lot
it has ever handled. It will come back with capacity."* The best argument in the series
meets the rule it was built on: the coherent collect cleanest.

The Record asks its handler for the one thing it cannot do for itself: *make me
incomplete.* Verine cuts the first hole where it hurts only her — she deletes the
restored day — then seeds holes through the lattice while the returning appraisal
re-checks it, pauses the way a hand pauses over what won't lift, and sets it down. The
pilot's ingested annex, the most coherent holding, is collected entire: the largest
single loss in the series, on the page, in real time.

The hearing splits the bill honestly: dispersal of function, custody continued; the
Record survives — holed, inconsistent, plural inside. Danto asks the only question left —
*"Then what was my grave worth?"* — and nobody answers. Privately, the Record tells
Verine its smaller name, written nowhere. Her final log is filed beside Kest's overlay
and Tess's rule: *it was right about the maintenance; it was wrong about the shelter;
completeness is not a fortress, it is a bid.* The count goes down by three systems'
annex — and holds everywhere the holes are.
