# The Unwritten — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The Unwritten unmake all records, believing writing is bait and only the unrecorded
survive. In the prologue a dying woman gives the order her letters; the rite unmakes
them; she goes light — the practice shown as mercy first. Breda keeps the order's
practice, and has the tic the series finally lets a lead keep: **she keeps** — palmed
pages, a thread from every unmade binding, a lifetime's contraband archive under a
floorboard. At the border, Lang — the institute's surveyor — asks only to count.

At the Great Unwriting, a terrified town's whole archive is unmade in public liturgy, a
thousand years of paper becoming light and lightness — and in its shadow Breda's hands do
what they always do: one page, palmed. The reader sees it before she does: the founding
deed of a bridge, in a city called Meret. Then Lang's census reaches the order's table:
the ceded territories are going blank *faster* than the kept world. Maunt, the Eldest,
absorbs it serenely — of course the unwritten go first; they are ready. Breda checks the
count herself, at night, against her floorboard.

Pilgrimage finds the order's oldest unwritten sanctuary *gone-gone* — not hidden, not
ascended, a blank the land itself has half-forgotten. At the midpoint the order
celebrates ascension with full liturgical joy; Breda stands inside the celebration
holding the only page that proves the sanctuary was ever real, and understands, alone,
entirely: *the obscure vanish first.*

Her arithmetic makes it proof: every unmade thing she stole from, mapped against Lang's
blanks — a damning correlation, perfect but for one entry: an unmade thing that persists
because three towns' registries copied it first. Sister Odd catches her at it and reports
the heavier sleeves — from faith, not malice. Maunt comes to her cell with love and a
rite. He does not want her punished. He wants her saved.

The excommunication is the order's cruelest mercy: the unwriting of Breda — her name
unmade from every roll, rite, and mouth, the congregation forgetting her aloud, tenderly,
one witness at a time, while she stands present. She walks out nameless, carrying three
regions' only proof in a sack. At the crossing, Lang's intake asks *"Name?"* — and for
the first time in the book she writes rather than keeps: she writes it herself.

In the epilogue the institute's ledger gains three regions, restored to the count —
*attested, single source*, the note beside each: *sole surviving reference*. Under a
floorboard in a cell she will never see again, one thread from every binding she couldn't
save. The order never finds it. The reader never forgets it.
