# The City That Forgave — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

In the city of Meret, two peoples divided by the river Sil have preserved the **Bridge
Night** — a centuries-old atrocity — as perfect experiential memory. Every year the
**Bearers** of each bank relive it through their ancestors in the **Wells**, and so it has
never become the past. Juna, twenty, is a Bearer of Southbank. Her mother Vessa — a former
Bearer who scandalously set her inheritance down — brings the motion the city comes to
call **the Mercy**: bounded deletion of the Bridge Night from both Wells and every Bearer
line.

The argument sharpens on the streets: an old Bearer's Reliving bleeds into the present
and he attacks a boy he "remembers" from the Night. Garrel, Northbank's chief archivist,
opposes the Mercy with full dignity — deletion, he says, is a second Bridge Night, aimed
at the dead. He is right. Vessa is right. That is the tragedy. Then Juna and Tammo, her
Northbank counterpart, do the forbidden thing: descending into the Southbank Well, they
run both Relivings side by side. The records contradict at the pivotal instant; both are
true; in both, one figure is helping people off the burning bridge whom neither bank's
line claims and neither record can hold — and beneath everything, the two Wells share a
single root. Surfacing, Juna reaches for the Bearer's oath — *I carry what happened* —
and cannot finish it. She no longer knows what happened.

The referendum lands at the book's midpoint, not its climax: the city's best arguer casts
her vote in silence — for the Mercy, against her own office — and it passes narrowly on
both banks. The rest of the book is what it costs. The Forgetting is staged as the city's
largest rite and rendered as loss: the Mercy's law requires one sealed copy, kept unread
against need, and a Bearer must volunteer to keep it. Juna does — and then, holding the
casket, undergoes the Forgetting like everyone else. **The custodian keeps the door, not
the memory.**

After, the peace works. Children play on the rebuilt bridge and still sing the river
lullaby — a verse gone that no one alive can miss; the old man and the boy meet without
history; a city mourns without an object. Then a petition is filed to open the sealed
room. The petitioner is Garrel — who spent his life keeping something sacred and now
cannot say what, and asks only to know again what he kept. Juna refuses the man who was
right, unable to tell him or herself why — only that the Juna who sealed it knew, and she
keeps faith with that stranger. Vessa and Juna love each other across an absence neither
can explain. Juna trains the next custodian on the rule — *"We keep it so no one must."*
On the casket's seal, scratched long before the Mercy, is a circle crossed by a single
line.
