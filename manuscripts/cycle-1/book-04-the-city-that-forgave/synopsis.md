# The City That Forgave — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

In the city of Meret, two peoples divided by the river Sil have preserved the **Bridge
Night** — a centuries-old atrocity — as perfect experiential memory. Every year the
**Bearers** of each bank relive it through their ancestors in the **Wells**, and so it has
never become the past. Juna, twenty, is a Bearer of Southbank. Her mother Vessa — a former
Bearer who scandalously set her inheritance down — brings the motion the city comes to
call **the Mercy**: bounded deletion of the Bridge Night from both Wells and every Bearer
line.

Juna crosses the river and, with Tammo, her Northbank counterpart, does the forbidden
thing: they run both Relivings side by side. The records contradict at the pivotal
instant; both are true; and in both, one figure is helping people off the burning bridge —
a figure neither bank's line claims and neither record can hold. Meanwhile the argument
sharpens on the streets: an old Bearer's Reliving bleeds into the present and he attacks a
boy he "remembers" from the Night. Garrel, Northbank's chief archivist, opposes the Mercy
with full dignity — deletion, he says, is a second Bridge Night, aimed at the dead. He is
right. Vessa is right. That is the tragedy.

The referendum passes narrowly on both banks. Juna votes for it — against her own office.
The Forgetting is staged as the city's largest rite and rendered as loss: Bearers weep for
what they can no longer name. And the Mercy's own law requires one sealed copy, kept
unread against need, by a volunteer custodian. Juna volunteers.

After, the peace works. Children play on the rebuilt bridge; the old man and the boy meet
without history; people mourn and cannot say what. Vessa and Juna love each other across
an absence neither can explain. Juna keeps the unopened room and teaches her successor the
rule — *"We keep it so no one must."* On the casket's seal, scratched long before the
Mercy, is a circle crossed by a single line.
