# The City That Forgave — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #4** · **Target length:** ~30,300 words · **Structure:**
Prologue + 3 acts × 3 chapters + Epilogue, re-pointed — chosen: **the referendum lands
at the midpoint**, not the climax. The back half of the book is not *will they?* — it is *what did it cost?* A
political tragedy structured as a consequence, not a contest. *(Re-drafted 2026-08.)*

- **Facet:** memory as **burden**.
- **Central question:** *Can peace exist while everyone remembers the truth?*
- **The book's job:** the cycle's anti-thesis. Books 1–3 assume carrying is a virtue;
  this book makes preservation the wound. It must never let the reader settle on a side —
  every position is held by someone decent, and the tragedy is that they are all right.
- **POV:** Juna — twenty, a Bearer of Southbank in the city of Meret. Each year she
  relives the Bridge Night through an ancestor's preserved experience. It is her office,
  her inheritance, and — until her mother's motion — her identity.
- **The spine in one line:** a reformer proposes deleting both peoples' inherited memory
  of an atrocity; the reformer is Juna's mother, the memory is Juna's office, and the
  book is the space between those two facts.

## The threads
- **New truth:** inherited *experiential* memory can be institutionalized at civic scale
  (the Bearer lines, the Wells) — and it can be **deleted**, deliberately, by consent,
  from a whole people. The universe now contains a city that chose to forget.
  Preservation is not always virtuous (the series' stated corrective).
- **The tragedy engine:** the Relivings keep the Bridge Night present tense. Grief that
  never becomes past cannot become peace; but deletion means the dead die twice. Vessa is
  right. Garrel is right. Juna's vote is the book.
- **The two records:** Northbank and Southbank each preserve the Bridge Night completely —
  and the records contradict at the pivotal instant. Contradiction-as-information is the
  Book 2 echo, never named: two true accounts that cannot be reconciled *are* the city.
- **The reader becomes the sealed copy:** the prologue's Reliving is rendered once, for
  the reader alone. After the Forgetting, every character has let the Night go — the
  reader is the only one still inside it. The series' thesis (the reader as last
  rememberer) executed at book scale, invisible on the page.
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the Mercy going public — the commandments' cited
  exemplar — sharpened by the kitchen table: the city's crisis and the family's are the
  same scene.

## Characters in play
- **Juna** — Bearer of Southbank. Raised at a councilwoman's table, she *argues* — out
  loud, well, at everyone; the city knows her as the girl who defends the office better
  than its elders. The book takes the arguer to a vote she casts in silence, and then to
  an office she can no longer explain even to herself.
- **Vessa** — Juna's mother; council reformer; *former Bearer who set it down* (her old
  scandal). Author of the Mercy. Believes she is freeing her daughter; learns too late
  what she is also taking.
- **Tammo** — Bearer of Northbank, Juna's counterpart. The only person in the city who
  knows the Night the way she does — from the other side. Not a romance; a mirror.
- **Garrel** — Northbank's chief archivist, the opposition. No villain (per the
  foundation): a man preserving something sacred, given full dignity — and, after the
  Mercy, the book's final wound (see Ch 9).
- **The Wells** — two memory-wells, one per bank, older than the city; the circle crossed
  by a single line worn into their copings. No one alive built them. (Network echo,
  unstated.)

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — inherited experiential memory at civic scale; deletion of a people's
      memory is possible, survivable, and priced. *(Ch 1, 7, 8.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — the truth of the pivotal instant (never adjudicated); the unheld
      figure on the bridge; who built the Wells; what the sealed room holds (the *reader*
      knows — the city never will). *(Ch 5, Epilogue.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Juna: the arguer who can no longer argue her own
      office; Bearer of the Night → keeper of a door. *(Ch 6–7.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the Mercy becomes precedent: the universe now holds a city
      that forgave by forgetting, citable by every later book. *(Ch 7–8.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — the Wells share a root and bear the symbol; the network is under
      the tragedy, unexplained. *(Ch 5, Epilogue.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Law 4** is the floor: the Relivings built this culture — calendars, tram lines,
  marriage law, the shape of grief. The Mercy is a technology too, and it will build the
  next culture; Ch 8 shows the first bricks.
- **Law 5 / Law 6:** both civilizations edited their past *by preserving it perfectly
  from one side*. No chosen one: Juna holds an office anyone's daughter could hold; her
  only distinction is what she chooses.
- **Antagonist rule:** Garrel preserves; Vessa frees; the Mercy is a good intention
  pursued *with* humility, and it still costs. No side is the author's.
- **Intentionally undefined, preserved:** whether memories are objective is *not*
  answered — both records are "true" and the book never rules. The unheld figure is
  never identified (a series motif rhyming with Sael, not a reappearance). The Wells'
  builders stay unknown.
- **POV discipline:** the Prologue is the only full Reliving of the Night. The atrocity
  is never re-staged for spectacle; afterward it exists as weight, not scene.
- **Coherence of the deletion (keep it simple, per the Easy-Read Standard):** the city
  remembers *that* it chose to forget; the content is gone. Empty rites persist. No
  paradox-play on the page.

## Continuity
- **Timeline:** **~CR 180**; the Bridge Night ~CR −120 (three centuries of Relivings —
  the wound older than anyone, kept present tense by the office).
- **Echoes carried:** the circle-crossed-line (Wells' copings, casket seal); the unheld
  figure at the rim of catastrophe (Sael-rhyme, unnamed); "carrying" as the Bearer's word
  for the office — Book 1's farewell inverted: here, carrying more people than yourself
  is the wound.
- **The river lullaby (device 8 debuts — private note):** planted whole in Ch 1, sung
  truncated in Ch 8 — the Mercy took a verse with it, and only the reader can know.
  Book 5 pays it (its eucatastrophe). Log the canonical text in the roadmap when drafted.
- **Hook backward:** a fragment-ship out of the Skerry's chain was logged bound for Meret
  (Book 3, Ch 8) — a Bearer family keeps an *Anthem* fragment as an heirloom, one line,
  unexplained.
- **Hook forward:** the Meret diaspora carries the silence outward — Book 5's POV grows
  up in it.
- **New names** (→ glossary via `canonize` when drafted): Meret, the river Sil,
  Northbank, Southbank, the Bridge Night, the Reliving, Bearer, the Wells, the Mercy,
  Juna, Vessa, Tammo, Garrel. *(Weight of names: Meret's world stays unnamed — a
  deliberate debt.)*
