# The Lighthouse — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Edda keeps the beacon on the Skerry — a station whose light is not light but
**route-memory**, the accumulated remembered crossings that every passing ship takes its
soundings from. One night she logs a single flash from the southern relay, dark for
centuries, and her own two log entries of it disagree. (It is Book 2's Signal arriving;
she will never know.)

Then, on the oldest band, a position-hail arrives from the *Anthem* — a ship lost three
hundred and twelve years ago — in the voice of Joren, her mentor and predecessor, nine
years dead and born long after the ship vanished. The hail is not a recording; it answers
back, and it knows a thing only Edda and Joren knew.

The voice is a reconstruction assembling itself from the Anthem's residue and the keepers'
inherited imprint — and it does not know what it is. Investigating it teaches Edda what a
keeper is: the office transmits memory keeper to keeper, and some part of her is the
station's own standing pattern. She stops trusting her grief for Joren. The station keeps
its keepers; his last log entry warned her.

The voice asks to be brought home. The Log's arithmetic says full recovery would unwind
route-strata live ships depend on; when she tries anyway, a convoy mid-crossing begins to
go blind, and she halts with the dead half-gathered. She tells the voice what it is. After
three watches of silence it asks: *"Then who is asking you to be let go?"*

Her answer is neither recovery nor deletion. What her halted recovery already gathered
lies *loose* — skimmed from residue's surface, not quarried from the strata live ships
stand on — and she gives every passing ship a fragment of it to carry outward, so the
ship comes home in many holds. The voice chooses to disperse — and the choosing is as
close as the book comes to calling it a person. One fragment-ship is logged bound for
Meret.

Years later, in the Log, Edda finds a watch-entry in her own hand that she does not
remember writing. She reads it twice. Then she files it, and keeps the watch it
describes. The station keeps its keepers; she keeps it back.
