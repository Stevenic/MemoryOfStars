# The Lighthouse — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #3** · **Target length:** ~28,100 words · **Structure:**
Prologue + **8 chapters (2 acts × 4)** + Epilogue — chosen: one keeper, one station, one
voice, and a story with exactly two movements (the impossible thing arrives; answering it
costs). A held breath, structured like one. *(Re-drafted 2026-08.)*

- **Facet:** memory as **identity**.
- **Central question:** *When does a reconstruction become a person?*
- **The book's job:** take the reader off-world for the first time — distance,
  navigation, isolation, and the residue travelers leave — while staying one person's
  story (Edda's). Book 2 proved the network is engineered; Book 3 shows what living
  inside its infrastructure does to a self.
- **POV:** Edda — Keeper of the beacon on the Skerry, about sixty. Keepers surrender
  their surnames to the station when they take the light; she no longer remembers hers.
- **The spine in one line:** a voice that cannot be alive asks Edda to bring a lost ship
  home, and recovering it would cost the route-memories thousands of living ships depend
  on — while the search teaches her how much of "Edda" is the station's own standing
  pattern.

## The threads
- **New truth:** interstellar navigation runs on **route-memory** — the accumulated
  remembered crossings held by beacon-stations. Ships "take soundings" before a crossing;
  every passage leaves **residue** the beacon keeps. (Law 1 made mechanical: travel
  spends memory as well as time.) And: a reconstruction assembled from residue can
  converse, want, and *choose*.
- **The dramatic irony:** the single flash Edda logs in the prologue is Book 2's Signal
  arriving — the reader knows the network is waking; Edda never learns. Her mystery stays
  local and personal; the reader's grows cosmic.
- **The psychological screw:** the keeper's office is itself inherited memory. Parts of
  what Edda knows — perhaps parts of what she *is* — are the station's pattern, passed
  keeper to keeper. How much is never confirmed. The book refuses to answer its own title
  question twice: not for the voice, not for her.
- **The dilemma:** full recovery of the lost ship's final hours requires unwinding
  route-strata that live ships are using *now*. The dead's completeness against the
  living's way home.
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the impossible hail — the commandments' own cited
  exemplar. Chapter 1 earns love for the station; chapter 2 breaks its physics.

## Characters in play
- **Edda** — the Keeper. A lifetime of talking to things: she narrates her rounds to the
  station, teases the Log, wishes the kettle good morning — sociability with no one to
  spend it on. *(Not procedure-as-armor; that was Book 2's move. Her habit is
  companionship with the inanimate — so when something finally answers back, in a beloved
  voice, the book's whole trap closes in one sentence.)*
- **The Log** — the station's old intelligence. Slow, literal, fallible (Law 3). Answers
  in hours, sometimes days. Not a companion; a ledger that occasionally has opinions —
  and the nearest thing to conversation the station has offered in nine years.
- **The voice / Joren** — Edda's mentor and predecessor, nine years dead — speaking from
  the *Anthem*, a ship lost three hundred and twelve years ago, before Joren was born.
  The impossibility is the hook; the explanation (a reconstruction assembling itself from
  residue and the keepers' inherited imprint) is the identity engine.
- **Pell** — the quarterly courier. The one living visitor; warm, unbothered, carries
  words between stations the way Nio carried letters (echo, not reprise).

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — navigation runs on route-memory; beacons keep the residue of every
      crossing; a reconstruction can converse and choose. *(Ch 1, 4, 8.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — how much of a keeper is the station's pattern; the origin of the
      flash (never learned by Edda); what the Anthem met, 312 years ago. *(Ch 6, Prologue, 7.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Edda: from a self she trusted to a self she keeps
      anyway. *(Ch 6 → Epilogue: she keeps even the watch she cannot remember standing.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the fragment-carriage of the lost becomes a keepers' rite
      and spreads station to station. *(Ch 8; its reach shown by Book 4's heirloom.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — the dark relays are waking; the reader holds the cosmic half of a
      mystery the protagonist never sees. *(Prologue, irony throughout.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Law 1** is the book's floor: every crossing costs; the dilemma is *made of* the cost.
- **Law 3:** the Log is modest, slow, and wrong sometimes. The voice is never a ghost
  cliché; it is residue that learned to want.
- **Intentionally undefined, preserved:** whether consciousness survives death is *not*
  answered — what the beacon holds is memory, and whether the voice is Joren, the
  Anthem's crew, or neither is left as the title question. Do not resolve it. The
  network's builders stay unexplained; the flash is never traced on the page; **what the
  Anthem met is never hinted at** (no later book may answer it either — logged as a kept
  mystery).
- **Weight of names:** keepers surrender surnames — the doctrine's first on-page
  institution. The Skerry has a name; its world does not.
- **No chosen one:** Edda is on watch. That is the whole reason it is her.

## Continuity
- **Timeline:** **~CR 60** — decades after Book 2, long enough for the Signal to have
  crawled the relays (distance is real; never state the mechanism). Anthem lost ~CR −252.
- **Echoes carried:** the circle crossed by a single line, worn into the beacon's oldest
  seal (never remarked on); plural carriage as the resolution shape (Book 1's farewell,
  *unspoken* here — the spoken line stays reserved for Book 5); an office assembled from
  fragments (Amarin-rhyme, unnamed).
- **Hook outward:** the Meret-bound fragment-ship (→ Book 4's heirloom); the keepers'
  rite of fragment-carriage (→ Book 5's carriage, at species scale).
- **New names** (→ glossary via `canonize` when drafted): Edda, the Skerry, the *Anthem*,
  Joren, Pell, the Log, route-memory, soundings, residue.
