# The First Pilgrim — Outline v2 (the revision plan)

**Memory of Stars — Novella #1** · **Status: FORWARD PLAN** — unlike `outline.md` (the
record of the published draft), this is a plan for the 91+ revision. It changes the
minimum that reaches the target: five chapters carry real work, three carry line-scale
work, and four are untouched. Every chapter left alone is a chapter that cannot regress.

- **Logline:** A girl climbs a mountain to receive her city's memories and comes home
  carrying more people than herself — having briefly been Amarin, and having met the
  woman no record can hold.
- **POV:** Lyra throughout; the Prologue is Elias's (as drafted — say so and keep it).
- **Memory-facet:** memory as **inheritance** — dramatized as objects, sentences, and
  duties passed hand to hand, none of them arriving whole.
- **Central question:** *What must we inherit before we can call ourselves adults?*
- **Timeline placement:** CR 0 — this Pilgrimage defines the series' dating frame.
- **Word-count target:** ~31,300 (inside the 28–34k law; drafted actual 29,016 by the
  2026-08 re-measure this plan baselines against).
- **Shape:** Prologue + 3 acts × 3 + Epilogue — the founding shape, kept: this is the
  book that founded it, and the revision re-budgets weight *inside* the grid (Act I's
  detonation, Act III's peak) rather than changing the grid.

> **Why a v2 exists:** three independent judges scored the draft 79.1 (77.8 / 78.8 /
> 80.6) against a 91+ bar, converging on the same faults: Chapter 2 is the shortest
> chapter in the book where the law wants the strongest; Chapter 7 summarizes the
> book's best premise in a list; Chapters 4–6 run one loop three times; Chapter 8
> hands an oracle the answers; Chapter 9 resolves on a rule never planted; the
> machinery keeps selecting Lyra while the dialogue denies chosen ones (Law 6); and
> Lyra has no speech engine in her own book. Every fix below is aimed at one of those.

## Premise

Orin, a city under a mountain, sends its seventeen-year-olds up to the Gate every
generation to receive the memories of the dead. In the last ordinary week before the
climb, the city's arithmetic breaks — publicly, in the Hall of Names, by exactly one —
and Lyra carries an irreplaceable thing past a rule that forbids it. The book asks what
the young must inherit, and answers: not records — people, carried between people.

## Characters in play — speech engines (the conversation layer)

Every line in a revised chapter runs through its speaker's engine until it could belong
to no one else. The cover-the-names test is the gate.

- **Lyra** — **NEW ENGINE (this revision's core character work):** *everything Lyra
  says is a version of testing the told against the touched* — she answers stories
  with questions about the thing itself, and will not accept a secondhand sentence
  where a firsthand object can be reached. She has built her whole life from other
  people's sentences about her father; she trusts what her hands can hold and probes
  everything else for the seam. **Deflection style:** when a feeling is named at her,
  she goes concrete — asks about the object, the hand, the smell; when offered comfort,
  she asks one more specific question than is polite. **Said-instead:** the sentence
  she cannot say is *"I don't remember my father"* — what she says instead is questions
  about objects. **Gesture repertoire** (already latent in the drafted prose — formalize
  it): thumb over the chipped wing; picking a thing up while someone else describes it;
  leaning in; a flat palm pressed on wood or stone to steady herself.
- **Tomas** — everything he says is a version of an inventory. His not-knowing is never
  the bare phrase: it is *"Unestablished."* / *"Insufficient data."* / a count restarted.
  Gestures: aligning and counting things; squaring objects with table edges.
- **Mara** — everything she says is a version of tending something on the stove while
  watching you. Answers sideways; hands busy, eyes not.
- **Elias** — everything he says is a version of an apology for knowing more than he
  will tell. **"I don't know." / "You keep saying that." / "I keep meaning it." is his
  refrain and his alone** (see the sweep, below). Gesture: going still.
- **Seren** — everything he says is a version of a door held almost shut. Refusals are
  flat ("No."); when the door opens a crack it opens on *doctrine*, not on facts.
- **Sael** — beyond the Gate; speaks in what survives: precise, unapologetic, incomplete.
- Minor cast (Cor, Anet, Dain, Ressa, Beren, Nalia, the guides): held to walk-on
  functions; **Beren is named at his refusal in Chapter 7**, not first-named at the
  climax (fixes the cast complaint in `critiques/story.md`).

### The "I don't know" sweep
The bare refrain appears 30 times across six speakers; a phrase that belongs to
everyone characterizes no one. In every revised chapter (2, 4–9), re-voice all
non-Elias instances through the speaker's engine. Permitted survivors: Elias's refrain
(canonical litany, verbatim); Mara's single instance in untouched Ch 1; and **one**
deliberate inherited echo in Lyra's mouth late in Act III (drafter picks the single
strongest site — the summit "I don't know" in Ch 8's close or Ch 9's "Someone came
with us" beat — and re-voices the other). The echo is the point: she inherits his
sentence the way she inherits his burden.

## Chapter 2 — the full design (settled; converged three times)

Target ~3,000. Title kept: *Tomas Knows Everything* — now ironic and load-bearing:
the boy who knows everything counts a name the world then denies him.

**Movement 1 — the Hall (kept, tightened).** The drafted opening survives: the three
mysteries on Tomas's fingers, Elias's apology, the compass in Elias's hands. Engine
sweep: Tomas's "I don't know" becomes *"Unestablished,"* and the drafted joke inverts
to fit — "I don't know" is the rarest sentence in the Tomas language *because he will
not say it.*

**Movement 2 — registration, and the lie (B).** The drafted scene, weaponized. The
clerk recites the rules the text already contains — *"No family heirlooms that cannot
be replaced."* Lyra declares nothing, with the red bird in her coat pocket (Ch 1 put
it there: "though she did not remember putting it there"). Then, in the same breath,
she answers the voluntary question honestly — "And you choose to make the ascent of
your own free will?" / "I do." — and the honest answer makes the lie worse, not
lighter: it proves she knows exactly what a true answer costs. **Tomas sees.** He
counts her declared belongings; the count is short by the one thing his eyes are on;
he says nothing — his first omission from a record, and it costs him (his engine is
the inventory; he has just falsified one by silence). The drafted wondering — what
would happen if a Pilgrim said no; the question asked and never once needed — stays
verbatim in spirit: Chapter 7 pays it with Beren.

**Movement 3 — the count (A, THE DETONATION).** Tomas and Lyra arrived before everyone
to see the list (Ch 1 planted this). At the posted roll, before the crowd, Tomas
tallies the year's Pilgrims: **forty-three names.** He writes the number down. The
morning proceeds; forty-two Pilgrims register. At the closing reading, the clerk reads
the roll: forty-two names, and every name answers. Tomas objects with his morning
count. They re-count — aloud, together, clerks and Pilgrims and Elias, the whole hall
counting as one voice — **forty-two.** No one can say which name was there a moment
ago. Tomas opens his notebook: the count in his own handwriting reads *forty-two* —
and he remembers, with nothing to hold it, that it didn't. **Elias — caretaker of the
names, the man who knows everyone in Orin — is asked whose the forty-third name was,
and cannot say: and for the first time in the book he is frightened rather than sad.**
Rendering per voice.md §4: flat, arithmetic, no narrator alarm; the horror is a sum
that won't reconcile. This converts Ch 1's planted *feeling* (one name missing) into
public, verifiable, unignorable *evidence* — witnessed by everyone, singling out no
one (Law 6 clean). On-canon: the Hall holds every citizen — living, dead, departed,
returned; the failure is the erasure's fingerprint rendered as folk dread and never
explained; it pays off directly at the Chapter 9 plaque, where the Hall finally keeps
a name by a different rule.

**Movement 4 — the western wall (kept), the compass penultimate, the count lands.**
The drafted wall scene and the compass-across-the-evening-bells beat are excellent and
stay — but the compass becomes the penultimate movement. The landing is the count.
Direction (not a line to copy): *forty-two Pilgrims would climb in seven days — and
all the way home, Lyra counted forty-three.* Ends on the question the reader would pay
to have answered.

## Lyra — the lie-and-cost arc, chapter by chapter

Planted Ch 2; costs across Acts II–III; paid at the chair. She is never selected —
she chooses, and pays (Law 6).

- **Ch 2 — the lie:** declares nothing with the bird in her pocket; answers the
  voluntary question honestly in the same breath. Tomas's silence makes him
  accessory — the lie is now *between two friends*, and it is the first active,
  costly choice in her book.
- **Ch 3 — cost as texture (no text change):** the slate — *I am afraid I will forget
  what was mine* — is the smuggler's confession, hidden in plain sight.
- **Ch 4 — the lie widens:** past Mara's three inspections; coat, not pack. Choosing
  it twice is worse than choosing it once.
- **Ch 5 — the lie compounds:** she withholds the bird's connections from the group
  while the group drowns in unowned memories; Tomas keeps the hole in his own record
  for her — his cost accrues alongside hers.
- **Ch 6 — payment one, public:** the compass exposes the bird before forty-nine
  witnesses; she produces what she declared not carrying; Tomas's "Yes" prices his
  omission in front of Seren. The friend who kept her secret is the instrument of its
  exposure.
- **Ch 7 — the choice named:** Seren's re-lined beat converts the machinery's
  apparent election into her accountable choice: she carried it; carrying it anyway
  was the choice.
- **Ch 8 — the meaning inverts, unabsolved:** the workshop reveals the bird was
  designed to move outside every rule; her breach carried the contradiction to the
  Gate. No one thanks her. The reader does the arithmetic.
- **Ch 9 — payment in full:** she gives the smuggled thing away — the heirloom that
  "cannot be replaced" is placed on the chair to belong to no one. And the debt
  between the friends squares: Tomas starts a book that admits its holes (entries
  without dates).
- **Epilogue — the long tail:** the surrendered bird comes back changed, wet with
  black grass — the cost keeps compounding into the series.

## The Law 6 fix (the machinery stops selecting her)

The draft denies chosen-ones in dialogue while the machinery selects Lyra. The fix is
distributed, event-level, and mostly re-staging:

1. **Ch 2 adds nothing to it:** the count is witnessed by the whole hall; the
   wrongness singles out no one.
2. **Ch 4:** the whole cohort counts; the dread is communal, not hers.
3. **Ch 6:** the compass tracks the *object*, demonstrated on the page (drafted) —
   and the object is where it is because of her *choice* (Ch 2), not her nature.
4. **Ch 7:** Beren refuses the crossing (choice is real, and refusable); **Anet is
   offered the carrying and refuses it** — a genuine claim on Amarin, declined in
   public. The role is offered by encounter to more than one person; Lyra is simply
   the one who answers. **Seren's "It chose you" is cut** and re-lined as the
   carrying-was-the-choice beat.
5. **Ch 8 (already drafted, kept verbatim):** "No one is. The Gate does not choose
   people. It encounters them." / "Because you answered." — now the machinery finally
   agrees with the dialogue.

## The Acts II loop, broken (Chs 4–6)

Drafted loop, three times: impossible thing → Tomas counts → Lyra asks Elias → "I
don't know" → Lyra asks Seren → refusal on principle. The v2 rotation:

- **Ch 4:** everyone counts; the written record fails instead of the asking; no

- **Ch 5:** the impossibilities arrive through the minor cast; **Elias interrogates

- **Ch 6:** Seren **volunteers** the doctrine unprompted (the anti-refusal — the door
  opening on its own is stranger than the door held shut); the compass scene is Seren
  interrogating Lyra; Elias answers a question nobody asked.
