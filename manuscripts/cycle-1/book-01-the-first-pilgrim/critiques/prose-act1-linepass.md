# Critique — prose — Book 1, Act I (Prologue + Chapters 1–3)
_run: 2026-08-03 · standards: voice.md (Do/Avoid rules cluster, §1/§2/§7/§8), style-guide.md_

**Verdict:** Act I is the book's healthiest stretch at the line level — narration means
7.5–8.0 words/sentence with real variance (the drone lives in Acts II–III), zero
decorative-adjective violations, and the act's fragment runs are almost all earned
devices. Six surgical touches; the deliberate devices are logged below so later passes
don't re-litigate them.

## Findings

### F1 · Craft · Prologue
- **Where:** "For just an instant— Another chair stood opposite his own."
- **What:** Dreyer-list *just* in narration; the em-dash cut already carries the brevity.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, the Dreyer list (filler sense).
- **Fix:** "For an instant—"
- **Disposition:** applied — filler cut; the dash does the work.

### F2 · Craft · Chapter 1
- **Where:** "He served as caretaker of the names… He repaired the carvings when weather
  wore them down. He recorded births and deaths. He remembered family lines stretching
  back farther than written histories."
- **What:** Four consecutive sentences opening *He* + same subject-verb shape — opener
  and structure monotony in an exposition block.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, sentence music (openers/structures axes).
- **Fix:** Fold the last three into one flowing sentence rising to its longest clause.
- **Disposition:** applied — merged to a single sentence ending on the family-lines
  clause; the paragraph now runs long-flow instead of drumbeat.
- **Post-pass review (independent judge, different model, 2026-08-03):** contested.
  The merge buries *"He remembered…"* — the series' thesis verb — as the third item in
  a list, and the same act protects two other triples as intentional devices. Proposed
  middle path: merge only the first two (*"He repaired the carvings when weather wore
  them down and recorded births and deaths."*) and restore *"He remembered family lines
  stretching back farther than written histories."* as its own sentence — 4 He-openers
  drop to 2, and the landing survives. **Author-call.**

### F3 · Craft · Chapter 1
- **Where:** "She had the strong, unreasonable feeling that one name was missing."
- **What:** Doubled adjective; *unreasonable* carries it, *strong* is decoration.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, adjective rationing (delete test).
- **Fix:** "She had the unreasonable feeling…"
- **Disposition:** applied.

### F4 · Craft · Chapter 2
- **Where:** "He wrote as he spoke, in remarkably tiny handwriting."
- **What:** Adverb propping an adjective; *tiny* needs no crutch.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, adverb discipline.
- **Fix:** "…in tiny handwriting."
- **Disposition:** applied.

### F5 · Nit · Chapter 3
- **Where:** "The current caught them immediately."
- **What:** *-ly* adverb propping the verb; *caught* plus the next sentence's vanishing
  already carry the speed.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, adverb discipline.
- **Fix:** "The current caught them."
- **Disposition:** applied.

### F6 · Nit · Chapter 3
- **Where:** "…children wearing stolen hats, ribbons, aprons, and one extremely large boot."
- **What:** *extremely large* — intensifier propping a weak adjective; one strong word
  keeps the joke.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, adverb discipline.
- **Fix:** "…and one enormous boot."
- **Disposition:** applied.

### F7 · Craft · Chapter 2
- **Where:** "What if they just can't explain it?" (Lyra, dialogue)
- **What:** Hedge-*just* in dialogue.
- **Why:** Dreyer list — but dialogue is a near-ban with a voice-choice carve-out.
- **Fix:** Cut only if the plea shouldn't soften.
- **Disposition:** skipped — deliberate voice choice; the *just* carries Lyra's plea,
  and cutting it turns the question confrontational.

### F8 · Craft · Chapter 2
- **Where:** "He said it immediately. Without hesitation. Without the strange pause
  every adult seemed to have."
- **What:** Adjacent to the banned narrator frame ("the answer came too quickly") —
  response-speed named rather than shown.
- **Why:** voice.md §6 banned templates — but this is the scene pass's rubric, not the
  line pass's, and the speed is thematically load-bearing (Tomas lacks the adults'
  pause).
- **Disposition:** author-call — flagged for the next scene pass: show it (his answer
  overlapping her sentence) or keep the naming as the point.

### F9 · Nit · Act-wide (deferred)
- **What:** Duration formula *"For several seconds…"* (Ch 2, the compass beat) — one
  instance in Act I of a book-wide tic (×11 total).
- **Disposition:** skipped — single instance is fine here; the tic is an Acts II–III
  finding for the music pass on those chapters.

## Devices considered and KEPT (do not re-flag)

- Prologue's spareness (mean 6.3 w/s, no crescendo sentence) — spare by design (~430
  words); the drops (*Not weather. / Memory.*) are the wonder beat.
- Ch 1: "The same streets. The same bells. The same people…" + "Last week. Last market
  morning. Last time…" — mirrored triples, ordinary-vs-marked; intentional parallelism.
- Ch 1: the six-bells counting ("There had been six. There were always six.") —
  counting-dread device.
- Ch 2: the clerk's rule litany ("No weapons… No written histories…") — verbatim ritual.
- Ch 2: the compass-and-bells paragraph ("Through the first bell… Through the second.
  Through the third.") — counting device rising to an earned long sentence; exemplary.
- Ch 3: the stolen-inventory opener ("A wooden spoon from the baker…") — comic
  inventory.
- Ch 3: "There was only breath. Stone. Laughter. The slap of boots…" — race-blur device.
- Dialogue pro-forms and beats ("I hope so," "So did you," "Of course you are") —
  content senses and voice choices, all protected by the Dreyer carve-outs.

## What's working (keep doing this)

- "Mara's eyes traveled her face — hairline, brow, mouth — slowly and in order, like
  someone packing a bag for a long journey." — the image gate at full strength.
- "Whenever a conversation approached the summit, it drifted elsewhere. Not abruptly.
  Almost unconsciously. Like rivers flowing around a stone." — startling and exactly true.
- "This year it felt smaller. Lyra could see the edges of everything." — the act's
  thesis in nine words.
- The Ch 3 closing crescendo ("…thousands of shining lines connecting every person in
  Orin to something waiting in the dark.") — the long sentence spent exactly where it's
  earned.
- "The current caught them. Dark stone vanished beneath dark water." (post-edit) — the
  landing the ceremony deserves.
