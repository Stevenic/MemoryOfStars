# Critique — prose — The First Pilgrim (Book 01, full manuscript: Prologue + Ch 1–9 + Epilogue, 28,550w)
_run: 2026-08-03 · standards: writing-guide/voice.md (§1 rhythm, §2 negation, §3 dialogue + choreography + conversation layers, §5 litany, §6 register, §7 punctuation, §8 landings, and the Do/Avoid rules cluster adopted 2026-08) · writing-guide/style-guide.md (mechanics of record) · scorecard: .claude/skills/score/SKILL.md_

**Verdict:** Word-level craft is already at series standard — adjectives rationed, the
Dreyer list clean, mechanics of record near-flawless — but the book has one systemic
failure that costs it more scorecard points than everything else combined: **the sentence
music dies at the Act I/II boundary and never recovers.** Narration drops from a 7.8–8.3
word mean with 9–12% subordination to a 4.8–5.8 word mean with 4–5% subordination and
zero semicolons or colons, and every other defect below (shared monosyllable dialogue,
the overspent negation turn, sight monoculture, anaphora as ambient default) is a
downstream symptom of the same collapse. Fix the music and four other findings resolve
with it.

## Findings

### F1 · Blocker · Chapters 4–9 + Epilogue
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "The bird blazed. The Gate widened."
- **What:** Acts II–III narration flatlines into a machine-gun drone: mean 4.79–5.81 words
  per sentence, subordination collapsed to 4–5%, comma-bearing sentences down to 4–9%,
  zero semicolons or colons across six chapters, and 20+ word sentences functionally
  extinct (1–3 per chapter against Chapter 1's 15). Chapter 7 contains one unbroken run of
  **49 consecutive narration sentences of eight words or fewer**; Chapter 8 runs 34, Ch 6
  runs 32, the Epilogue 28. You can tap a steady beat to whole pages.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, *Sentence music — vary the length* (Provost): "never let
  sentence length settle into a pattern… whether they're all five words (machine-gun
  monotony) or all twenty." Named there as Book 1's own failure mode. Also §1: "when every
  line stands alone, nothing stands out, and the voice becomes a single sustained note."
- **Fix:** A length-restoration pass over Ch 4–9 + Epilogue, working paragraph by
  paragraph, not sentence by sentence: fold adjacent short declaratives back into flowing
  sentences with subordination (`because`, `while`, `until`, `as though`) wherever the
  sentences belong to one thought, and keep the drop only where §1's five earning
  conditions apply. Target Act I's profile — mean ≈7, subordination ≈9–10%, 8–12
  sentences of 20+ words per chapter. Do not add words for length; recombine existing ones.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14) — this is the single item capping that
  category; also drags Emotional Resonance (10) and Wonder (10), since the register that
  should widen at the book's biggest beats instead narrows. Worth ~3–4 points of the
  overall /100 on its own.

### F2 · Blocker · Chapters 6–9
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "Circles. Stars. Paths."
- **What:** The wonder beats — the Gate opening, the crossings, the bird unfolding, the
  chain of hands, the threads — are rendered entirely in one- to four-word fragments. The
  chapter has exactly **one** crescendo sentence in 287 ("A pale band of light arced
  across the darkness, and a broken moon hung low over a plain of black grass that bent in
  a wind no one on the mountain could feel," 33 words) and it works — but a crescendo
  needs a rested reader, and after 280 five-word sentences the reader is not rested,
  only numb. The long sentence reads as an anomaly rather than a peak.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, sentence music: "the long sentence — the one that builds and
  rolls and lands like a crescendo — is spent *only* when the reader is rested and the
  moment is worth it (a wonder beat, a chapter turn)." Also the *scope lift* clause:
  "at a wonder beat, the narration may briefly widen past the character… rare, rested,
  and structurally earned." Book 1 currently earns lifts it never spends.
- **Fix:** Name the four or five beats that get a crescendo (Gate opens · Lyra crosses ·
  the bird unfolds · the chain of hands · the plaque or the final star) and build one
  rolling sentence into each, with the fragments *immediately before* it clipped shorter
  still so the contrast is felt. This is the same pass as F1 but with the peaks placed
  first and the valleys shaped to serve them.
- **Points at stake:** Wonder & Mystery (wt 10) — currently the biggest unforced loss
  there; Voice & Prose (14); Emotional Resonance (10).

### F3 · Blocker · whole book
- **Where:** Epilogue — "“I saw another world.”"
- **What:** Cover-the-names failure. The book's default reply for every character is a
  bare monosyllable: `"No."` ×64, `"Yes."` ×43, `"I know."` ×30, `"I don't know."` ×24 —
  161 flat one-to-three-word answers shared by Lyra, Tomas, Mara, Elias, Seren, Sael and
  Dain alike. The Epilogue volley cited above runs eleven untagged lines — *"I saw another
  world." / "I know." / "It was dying." / "Perhaps." / "The towers were going dark." /
  "Yes." / … / "I don't know." / "I don't know." / "Is it still there?"* — and could be
  reassigned between any two of the cast without loss.
- **Why:** voice.md §3, *the cover-the-names test*: "Strip every attribution and beat from
  the exchange — can you still tell who is talking, from diction, syntax, and deflection
  alone?" And, naming this exact defect: "Book 1 hands *'I don't know'* to nearly every
  speaker 29 times; a phrase that belongs to everyone characterizes no one." Also *every
  major character gets a speech engine* (Tomas = an inventory, Mara = tending something on
  the stove while watching you, Elias = an apology for knowing more than he will tell,
  Seren = a door held almost shut).
- **Fix:** Protect the canonical litany verbatim — Elias's *"I don't know." / "You keep
  saying that." / "I keep meaning it."* is his and must survive untouched (Ch 4, Ch 5).
  Re-voice every other instance through the speaker's engine: Tomas's "I don't know"
  becomes an inventory of what he *has* counted; Seren's becomes a door held shut (a
  redirect, a question returned); Mara's becomes her hands answering while her mouth
  doesn't; Sael's becomes the difference she keeps drawing ("I remember ___ing it"). The
  Epilogue volley needs at most two of its eleven lines to remain monosyllabic.
- **Points at stake:** Character (wt 14) — distinct voices is half that category's
  definition; Voice & Prose (14). Second-largest lever in the book.

### F4 · Craft · whole book, worst in Ch 5–9
- **Where:** Chapter 5 — "He looked at her."
- **What:** Sight monoculture. `looked` ×176 (37 in Ch 5 alone), plus `saw` ×36, `stared`
  ×30, `watched` ×23, `glanced` ×14, `eyes` ×81. Whole sentences repeat verbatim: *"He
  looked at her."* ×4 (six including variants), *"Lyra looked toward Elias."* ×3, *"Seren
  looked at him."* ×3, *"Lyra looked at him."* ×3, *"She looked at him."* ×3. Meanwhile
  smell appears **zero** times in Chapters 6, 7 and 9 — a storm, a snow plateau, and a
  climax in which nothing is ever smelled, weighed, or felt against the skin. Sight
  outweighs all other senses combined by 3–6× in every chapter.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, sentence music (senses axis): "a scene rendered only through
  the eyes goes flat — Book 1 says *looked* 176 times; rotate in sound, weight,
  temperature, smell, and let a gaze become *watched*, *studied*, *followed* when it must
  stay a gaze." Compounded by §3's ban on beats that only attribute.
- **Fix:** Two moves, not one. (a) Where the look must stay a look, give it the vector §3
  asks for — where the eyes travel and in what order — and vary the verb. (b) Where the
  look is only attribution, replace it with a non-visual carrier the scene already owns:
  cold in the fingers, the weight of a pack strap, wet wool, resin, winterleaf, the rope's
  tension, the absence of echo off the Gate. Chapters 6, 7 and 9 need at least one smell
  and one temperature each. Kill all verbatim-repeated look sentences.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14); Emotional Resonance (10) — the body is where
  §6 says the feeling lives, and a sight-only body carries none; Wonder (10).

### F5 · Craft · Chapters 5, 7, 8, 9
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "Not blurred."
- **What:** The signature move is spent to devaluation. ~80 definition-by-negation turns
  book-wide; Chapter 7 alone has thirteen in 1,844 words (one every 142 words) — *Not
  brighter. / Not because he wished to. / Not because of fear. / Not violently. / Not
  blurred. / Not similarly. / Not her own. / Not for Elias. / Not loudly. / Not speaking. /
  Not learned. / Not breaking. / Not to Lyra.* — and several land within two or three
  paragraphs of each other (*"Not for Elias. / For her."* then *"Not loudly. / Clearly."*).
  Chapters 8 and 9 carry ten each. When the turn is the default sentence shape it stops
  being a turn.
- **Why:** voice.md §2: "Don't overuse it — roughly once or twice per scene, never twice in
  a row." Continuous chapters (8, 9) are exempted from ⸻ breaks by §7, not from the ration.
- **Fix:** Keep two per scene-beat — the ones where saying what a thing is *not* is the
  only way to say what it is (*"Not blurred. / Absent."* earns it; *"Not loudly. /
  Clearly."* does not). Convert the rest to plain statement; several will recombine into
  the longer sentences F1 wants anyway, so run this pass with F1.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14) — this is the series' most identifiable
  device and its overspend reads as tic rather than voice.

### F6 · Craft · whole book
- **Where:** Chapter 5 — "“What are you writing?”"
- **What:** 82–92% of dialogue paragraphs in every chapter carry no tag and no beat.
  Longest unbroken bodiless volleys: eleven lines (Epilogue), eight (Ch 5), seven (Ch 1,
  2, 3, 4, 6, 9). At that length the reader tracks speakers by counting lines, which is
  precisely the failure §3 names — and it is also the second engine of the F1 drone, since
  a page of bodiless one-line exchanges has no sentence length at all.
- **Why:** voice.md §3: "**Never run more than ~3 clipped exchanges without a body.** A
  long volley with no hands, no objects, no attribution reads as a list, not a
  conversation." Do/Avoid: "isolating every dialogue utterance."
- **Fix:** Every volley over three lines gets one grounded body — and per §3, one that
  *changes the meaning of the line it rides*, drawn from the established repertoire
  (Tomas aligns and counts; Mara's hands stay busy while her eyes don't; Elias goes still;
  Seren's knife). The Epilogue's eleven-line volley is the priority: it is the book's last
  conversation and currently has no room in it.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14); Character (14). Partly shared with the
  scene critic's presence pass — coordinate so the two don't double-fix.

### F7 · Craft · whole book, densest Ch 5–9
- **Where:** Chapter 6 — "For several breaths, he did not move."
- **What:** Duration formulas ×44: *for a moment* ×20, *for an instant* ×12, *for several
  seconds/moments/breaths/minutes* ×11, plus *for one impossible instant* ×3. Worse, most
  ride the same three-part template — fronted duration phrase, short clause, then *Then*:
  *"For several breaths, he did not move. Then he stood." / "For an instant, pale lines
  appeared beneath it… Then the light changed." / "For a long moment, nothing happened.
  Then the world remembered another place."* With `Then` opening 100 narration sentences
  book-wide, this template is the only grammatical variation the drone permits.
- **Why:** critique-prose rubric, formula tics: "duration formulas (*for several
  seconds/moments*)… repeated distinctive phrases beyond the canonical litany." voice.md
  Do/Avoid, sentence music (structures axis): "the same grammatical shape repeated —
  fronted phrase after fronted phrase — drones even when lengths differ."
- **Fix:** Cut roughly two-thirds. Most are measuring a pause the surrounding beat already
  shows; delete the phrase and the sentence is unchanged, which is the delete test. Where
  a duration genuinely matters, show it in the body (a breath held, a cup stopped halfway,
  three steps in silence — the book already does this well at *"They walked three steps in
  silence. He sighed. '…Perhaps.'"*).
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14).

### F8 · Craft · Chapters 4–9
- **Where:** Chapter 6 — "The mountain closed behind them."
- **What:** 52 runs of three-or-more consecutive narration sentences sharing an opening
  word. Perhaps twenty are earned ritual litanies the house owns (*"No hinges. / No seam.
  / No inscription."* · *"Sael laughing beside a river. / Sael asleep over a table…"* ·
  *"A preference for too much salt. / A scar earned falling through river ice…"*). The
  rest are unintended drumbeats on ordinary connective narration — *"The mountain closed
  behind them. / The first hour passed in darkness. / The lanterns moved in a wavering
  line across wet stone. / The trail rose steeply…"* (Ch 6) and *"The climb resumed before
  sunrise reached the slope. / The trail became steeper. / The air thinned…"* (Ch 6) and
  *"He produced a folded notebook… / He wrote as he spoke… / He looked up and waited…"*
  (Ch 2). Book-wide, 68% of narration sentences open with one of twenty tokens; `The`
  alone opens 15.2%.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, sentence music (openers axis): "three sentences in a row
  starting with the same word is a device or a defect — decide which."
- **Fix:** Decide, run by run. Protect the litanies verbatim; break the accidental runs by
  folding two of the three into one sentence (which F1 wants anyway) or fronting a
  different element. Do not introduce ornamental inversions — the fix is combination, not
  decoration.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14).

### F9 · Craft · Chapters 7–9, Epilogue
- **Where:** Chapter 9 — "He lifted two fingers to his heart. She mirrored him."
- **What:** The farewell gesture is spent seventeen times, ten of them in Act III, five
  within Chapter 9's single continuous scene (Elias→Sael at the threshold, Lyra→her
  father, Elias→Sael again at the parting, Elias→Seren's exchange, Elias and Lyra at the
  closed Gate). It is also the book's *only* nonverbal move at the climax. Where it should
  detonate — Elias's *"I loved her"*, Lyra's *"I love you"* to a father who isn't one —
  it has been pre-devalued by the four uses before it. Compounding: *"Lyra lost feeling in
  two fingers"* (Ch 6, ~30 lines before the Gate opens) collides the motif token with
  ordinary frostbite.
- **Why:** voice.md §3 choreography layer: "**Ration it like salt.** One choreographed
  exchange per *beat* of a scene, not per line… **Never the same gesture twice in a
  scene.**" And §5: motifs "accrue meaning across the book — drift breaks the spell."
- **Fix:** Cut Chapter 9's five to two — Elias's at the parting and Lyra's at the closed
  Gate — and replace the others with gestures from each character's own repertoire.
  Recast *"lost feeling in two fingers"* so the motif's token isn't spent on a symptom.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14); Emotional Resonance (10) — this is the beat
  the book's whole emotional argument lands on.

### F10 · Craft · Prologue, Chapter 2
- **Where:** Prologue — "scattered across the valley like fallen stars"
- **What:** Two images fail gate one (cliché) at the two places most likely to set the
  reader's expectation for the book's figurative register — the Prologue's second sentence
  and Elias's first close-up: *"The city slept below, scattered across the valley like
  fallen stars"* and *"Lines crossed his face like rivers on an old map."* A third is
  borderline: *"Like rivers flowing around a stone"* (Ch 2). The book's *good* images
  prove the standard is reachable — *"like someone packing a bag for a long journey"*,
  *"the way you watch a locked door swing open"*, *"Its wooden arms faced Elias like open
  hands"*, *"a sound that was half laugh, half wound"* — which makes the three lazy ones
  more conspicuous, not less.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, *the image gate*: "**Startling** — the reader has never heard
  it put that way; and **accurate**… Cliché fails the first gate… An image that fails
  either gate is **cut, not polished** — plain statement beats a dishonest figure every
  time."
- **Fix:** Cut all three to plain statement. Do not replace with new similes; the Prologue
  in particular is stronger with the city simply below and asleep.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14); Wonder (10) — the Prologue is where the
  reader decides how much to trust the book's eye.

### F11 · Craft · Chapters 4, 7
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "The mountain became utterly still."
- **What:** Three modifiers on the same absolute: *"He went entirely still"* (Ch 4),
  *"The mountain became utterly still."* (Ch 7), *"Seren remained perfectly still."*
  (Ch 7). All three sit at high-tension beats where the bare word would land hardest.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, adverb discipline corollary: "**absolutes take no
  modifier**: *still*, *silent*, *empty*, *dead* are binary states; *completely still* and
  *utterly silent* prop what cannot be propped, and the bare word lands harder (*The room
  was silent.*)."
- **Fix:** Delete the modifier in all three. *The mountain became still.*
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14). Small but free.

### F12 · Nit · Chapters 3, 4, 7
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "“No,” Seren said quietly."
- **What:** Adverbs on dialogue tags: *"she said slowly"* (Ch 3), *"she said softly"*
  (Ch 4), *"Seren said quietly"* (Ch 7), *"he said quietly"* (Ch 7), plus *"Mara said it
  lightly"* (Ch 1). Two of the five prop the tag where the line already carries the tone.
- **Why:** voice.md §3: "**Tags:** minimal — `said` / `asked`, or none. **Never adverbs on
  tags.** Let the line carry the tone." style-guide.md repeats it. *(Carve-out honored
  elsewhere: "Sael answered carefully" is the sanctioned survivor case — the care is the
  point. "Mara said it lightly, the way you set down something heavy" survives because the
  adverb sets up the image that contradicts it.)*
- **Fix:** Strip the adverb; where the tone genuinely needs marking, give it a body per
  §3 instead.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14).

### F13 · Craft · Chapters 3, 5
- **Where:** Chapter 5 — "Lyra could not escape the feeling that someone had just left."
- **What:** Two of eleven chapter landings miss §8, and both miss the same way — a flabby
  narrator frame that *names* the feeling instead of staging it: *"And all through the
  night, Lyra had the strange feeling that someone was dancing with them whom no one else
  could see."* (Ch 3, 23 words, "the strange feeling" + an awkward *whom* clause) and the
  Ch 5 line above. Set against the nine that land — *"He could not remember whether he had
  climbed it alone."*, *"By morning, no one agreed on how many bedrolls there had been."*,
  *"something remembered her name."* — the two weak ones are conspicuous, and both sit at
  the end of the book's two longest chapters, where the reader most needs the blow.
- **Why:** voice.md §8: "End on a short, resonant declarative… The gut-punch is a *plain*
  sentence, not a flourish." §6: "Don't name the feeling."
- **Fix:** Cut the "feeling that" frame in both; state the impossible flatly per §4 and
  let the reader supply the dread. Both lines shorten by roughly half.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14); Emotional Resonance (10) — chapter landings
  are the highest-leverage sentences in the book.

### F14 · Craft · Chapters 6→7→8→9
- **Where:** Chapter 6 — "Beyond the Gate waited another sky."
- **What:** The chapter-seam phrase repeat is used at *every* Act III boundary in a row:
  Ch 6 closes "Beyond the Gate waited another sky." → Ch 7 line 3 "Beyond it waited
  another sky."; Ch 7 closes "Lyra fell forward into another life." → Ch 8 opens with that
  sentence verbatim; Ch 8 closes "It came from the empty chair." → Ch 9 opens "The seventh
  bell rang from the empty chair." Once it is a device; three consecutive times it is a
  template, and it drains the surprise from each chapter's opening line.
- **Why:** critique-prose rubric, formula tics: "repeated distinctive phrases beyond the
  canonical litany"; voice.md §8 wants the landing to *reframe*, which a line the next
  chapter immediately restates cannot do.
- **Fix:** Keep one — the 7→8 verbatim carry is the strongest, since Lyra's fall *is* the
  chapter break. Recast the 6→7 and 8→9 openings to start somewhere new.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14); Story & Structure (14) marginally, since the
  Act III chapters currently open on borrowed momentum.

### F15 · Nit · whole book
- **Where:** Chapter 4 — "the way you watch a locked door swing open"
- **What:** The *"the way you [verb]"* comparative frame appears eight times: *set down
  something heavy* (Ch 1), *a man looks at a face he has spent years grieving* (Ch 1),
  *take something that is still warm* (Ch 2), *glance at someone to share a thought too
  small for words* (Ch 2), *watch a locked door swing open* (Ch 4), *hold something that
  belongs to someone you love* (Ch 5), *correct a stranger* (Ch 7), *he counted things*
  (Ch 8). Individually excellent; eight times it becomes the narrator's only way of
  comparing anything.
- **Why:** critique-prose rubric, formula tics. *(Two are protected: "the way you set down
  something heavy" and "the way you correct a stranger" are quoted as exemplars in
  voice.md §6.)*
- **Fix:** Keep the two exemplars and the two strongest others; convert the rest to direct
  statement or a different figure.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14).

### F16 · Nit · whole manuscript
- **Where:** header — "Draft complete. Paste the finished text under each heading below"
- **What:** Three mechanics-of-record items. (a) A stale authoring scaffold comment sits at
  the top of the published manuscript, and it contradicts the finished book on two facts:
  *"Scene breaks: * * *"* (the manuscript correctly uses ⸻ ×43) and *"Target:
  30,000–32,000 words"* (actual 28,550). (b) Em dashes in narration are **spaced** 26 times
  against 2 unspaced, where style-guide.md specifies "em dash (—), **unspaced**" —
  e.g. *"Something made him look back — to the chair"*. (c) Orin is *"the city"* thirteen
  times and *"the village"* twice, both in the Epilogue: *"Families waited near the village

- **Why:** style-guide.md, Formatting (scene breaks), Dashes, and Length targets; and
  diction consistency for a proper place with a market road, a clock tower, an eastern
  tower and a Hall of Names.
- **Fix:** Delete the scaffold comment. Decide the dash question (see Open questions) and
  apply once, globally. Change both Epilogue *village* → *city* / *town*. *(Note: line
  3486's "the village where he had been born" refers to another Pilgrim's birthplace and

- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14), small — but the scaffold comment is visible
  in the repo copy and costs nothing to remove.

### F17 · Nit · whole book
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "Darkness simply acknowledged that it had always contained an opening."
- **What:** *simply* ×6 in narration as a recurring understatement crutch: *"They simply
  began walking"*, *"Some simply watched"*, *"She simply could not remember Mara saying
  it"*, *"Darkness simply acknowledged…"*, *"For a long moment Seren simply looked at the
  opening"*. Related soft-hedge cluster: *almost* ×24, *nearly* ×12, *immediately* ×8.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid, adverb discipline: hunt every *-ly* and ask what verb it is
  compensating for. *simply* here is doing the same throat-clearing work the Dreyer list
  bans, without being on the list. *(Protected: "He simply stood beside her while she
  grieved it" is a voice.md §6 exemplar.)*
- **Fix:** Delete four of the six; the deadpan is stronger unmodified — *Darkness
  acknowledged that it had always contained an opening.* Thin the *almost*/*nearly* hedges
  by roughly half, especially where they precede an absolute.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (wt 14).

## What's working

- **Adjective rationing is exemplary and book-wide.** I could not find a single decorative
  adjective. The state-adjective doctrine is honored everywhere it matters — *chipped
  wing*, *cracked glass*, *faded thread*, *worn red paint*, *broken ledge*, *a warm
  cellar*-style expectation breaks like *"a warm blanket… warm as though someone had only
  just stood up."* This is the hardest of the 2026-08 rules and the book passes it cold.
- **The Dreyer list is clean in narration.** Every *just* is content-sense (*just before
  noon*, *just beyond the firelight*), every *so* is a consequence conjunction (*so fine
  they looked like threads*, *so steep that*). No *very*, *really*, *quite*, *actually*,
  *of course* in narration.
- **Mechanics of record are near-flawless.** Curly quotes throughout (zero straight quotes
  or apostrophes in 28.5k words), single-character ellipses (29, zero three-dot), correct
  nested single quotes (*"I say 'I don't know' all the time"*), every number spelled out
  including *nine hundred eighty-six* and *one hundred and twelve*, US spelling, serial
  commas, and the caps doctrine held exactly — the Gate / the Hall of Names / a Pilgrim
  capitalized, *the erasure* and *route-memory* lowercase.
- **Act I is the model the rest of the book should be measured against.** Chapters 1–2
  carry a 7.8–8.3 word mean, 25 sentences of 20+ words, 9–12% subordination, and real
  crescendos: *"Mara's eyes traveled her face — hairline, brow, mouth — slowly and in
  order, like someone packing a bag for a long journey."* Nine of eleven chapter landings
  are exemplary, and the two best (*"He could not remember whether he had climbed it
  alone."*, *"something remembered her name."*) are already the guide's own §8 exemplars.

## Open questions

- **Em dash spacing.** style-guide.md specifies unspaced em dashes; the manuscript uses
  spaced ones 26:2 in narration and unspaced ones for cut speech (*"For now—"*). But
  voice.md v1.0 declares itself "derived from the finished text of *The First Pilgrim*."
  Which is the rule of record for Book 1 — the guide, or the text the guide was drawn
  from? A global change would touch 26 sentences and Book 2's matching habit. Flagged, not
  fixed.
- **Rations in continuous chapters.** §2 (negation) and §3 (gesture) both ration
  *per scene*, but §7 sanctions Chapters 8–9 running with no ⸻ breaks at all. Does the
  ration then apply per beat, per chapter, or not at all? F5 and F9 assume *per beat*;
  confirm before the editor applies them.
- **Cross-chapter phrase stitching.** The guide is silent on repeating a chapter's landing
  line as the next chapter's opening. F14 treats three consecutive uses as a worn template,
  but if the Act III seam repeats are an intended structural device, say so and I'll stop
  flagging them.
- **audio.yml drift (not prose, but adjacent).** The Chapter 2 production note directs the
  reader to "Close quiet and unresolved on 'disappointed her or brought relief'" — a line
  that no longer exists in the revised manuscript, which now closes on *"She wasn't sure
  which she had wanted."* The audio sheet should be re-synced after any line pass.
- **Gnomic present in similes.** *"the way a man looks at a face he has spent years
  grieving"*, *"the way you watch a locked door swing open"* etc. are present tense inside
  a past-tense narration that style-guide.md says is "past tense throughout, any deviation
  flagged in the outline." This is conventional English and two instances are voice.md
  exemplars, so I have treated it as an unwritten carve-out rather than a violation —
  worth writing down.
