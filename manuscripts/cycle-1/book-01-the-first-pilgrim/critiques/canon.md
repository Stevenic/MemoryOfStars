# Critique — canon — The First Pilgrim (full manuscript: Prologue + Ch 1–9 + Epilogue, 28,550w)
_run: 2026-08-03 · standards: series-bible/foundation.md · seven-laws.md · cosmology.md · timeline.md · atlas.md · glossary.md · canon-and-continuity.md · characters/lyra-ash.md · writing-guide/naming-conventions.md · voice.md · style-guide.md · commandments.md · structure.md · the book's outline.md / synopsis.md / canon-ledger.md / audio.yml_

**Verdict:** The constitution holds — no Seven-Laws break survives scrutiny on the page, and Law 6's canonical corrective is quoted verbatim — but the book's **support documents contradict the prose** in five places (mandatory-vs-voluntary, the seven-day promise, "the Archive", town-vs-city, "Orin's folk word"), and three internal-arithmetic errors sit inside the motif the book uses as its dread engine (counting). Canon Fidelity is the single largest weight on the scorecard (18) and is currently leaking on bookkeeping, not on imagination.

## Findings

### F1 · Blocker · Epilogue
- **Where:** Epilogue — "The carving was wet. Black grass clung to its underside."
- **What:** The book's final image moves **matter** from beyond the Gate into Lyra's bedroom, instantly and at **zero cost** — the only crossing in the book nobody pays for — and the event is logged nowhere.
- **Why:** `seven-laws.md` §Law 1 — "Travel is possible. It is never free. Time, energy, risk, or opportunity must always be sacrificed." Every other crossing in the book pays in memory (Cor loses Anet; Elias loses himself; Seren lost his brother twice) — which is exactly how Law 1 is honored. This one pays nothing. Compounding: `cosmology.md` §Established specifics ratifies "Ships are **sublight, always** — no FTL matter," and `atlas.md` §Gazetteer → *Still unnamed, by design* ratifies that "the land beyond the Gate / 'another sky' (01, 30) — physical place vs. memory-space stays **deliberately ambiguous**." Wet grass on a wooden bird is physical evidence; it tips the ratified ambiguity. And `seven-laws.md` §Things That Are Intentionally Undefined: "When a story finally answers one of these, it is a **universe-level canon event** — log it in `canon-and-continuity.md`" — whose canon-event table and continuity log are **both empty**, while the outline asserts "No intentionally-undefined question answered."
- **Fix:** Either (a) attach a cost to the returned bird so Law 1 is visibly paid, (b) keep the physical proof deniable — a dream-residue Lyra cannot verify by morning, the way the bedroll and the wet spoon are deniable — or (c) accept it and file the canon event in `canon-and-continuity.md` with author sign-off, plus a matching note in `cosmology.md`. Do not leave it unlogged.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — an unlogged brush with an intentionally-undefined question is the rubric's named "heavy penalty"; worth ~1.5–2 pts of the 18. Wonder & Mystery (10) — the deniable version is also the more frightening one.

### F2 · Blocker · Chapter 2 (vs. canon-ledger.md)
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "You understand that the Pilgrimage is voluntary." / "And you choose to make the ascent of your own free will?"
- **What:** The manuscript stages the Pilgrimage's voluntariness as a formal, twice-asked rite; the book's own `canon-ledger.md` §1 Confirmed canon says the opposite — "A community (Orin) practices a **mandatory Pilgrimage**."
- **Why:** `canon-and-continuity.md` §The canon hierarchy — completed drafts outrank notes and outlines, so the ledger is wrong; and §Continuity rules — "When you change canon, update the bible **and** any affected synopses in the same pass." The ledger is the artifact this book returns to the trunk; Books 02/28/30 all reach back into Orin's rite and will inherit whichever version is written down. The same ledger entry also mis-describes the rite as "the young experience carefully selected memories of generations of the dead" (the Gate on the page shows each Pilgrim something different and mostly private, and Seren's rule is "It will not show you the same thing"), and lists "That a pilgrim climbs the mountain alone" as a cultural belief the text never voices — forty-nine travelers climb together; only the *crossing* is alone.
- **Fix:** Rewrite `canon-ledger.md` §1 and §2 to the drafted text: voluntary-by-ceremony; the Gate shows each crosser something different and unverifiable; "cross alone" is the Gate's rule, not the mountain's. Leave the prose untouched — the voluntariness beat ("She could not decide which was stranger: that the question was always asked, or that it had never once been needed") is one of the book's best.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — a direct ledger/text contradiction on the book's central rite; ~1 pt. Series Contribution (5) — the ledger is what later books read.

### F3 · Blocker · Prologue (vs. synopsis.md + outline.md)
- **Where:** Prologue — "I kept my promise." / "So did you," he said to the empty room.
- **What:** Both `synopsis.md` ("The Archive offers no answers—only a promise to return in seven days") and `outline.md`'s prologue beat ("the Archive's only answer is a promise to return in seven days") describe an exchange that **is not in the prologue**. Nothing in the prologue promises anything, and no one names an Archive.
- **Why:** `canon-and-continuity.md` §Continuity rules — affected synopses must be updated in the same pass; the synopsis is labelled "**Spoiler-complete**" and is the record other books plan against. `outline.md` further declares itself "a **record** of the book as written."
- **Fix:** Correct both documents to what the prologue actually does — Elias says goodbye, the room answers with a felt presence and a vanishing second chair, and he leaves unable to remember whether he climbed alone. If the seven-day promise is wanted, it belongs in the prose, not in the summary.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5 pt directly, but this is the cheapest point on the board. Story & Structure (14) — a reviewer reading the synopsis first will score the prologue against a beat it never had.

### F4 · Blocker · Chapters 1 / 6 / 7 (internal continuity)
- **Where:** Chapter 1 — "Forty-two young men and women born beneath the same turning of the stars, all now old enough to climb. Seventeen, according to the city records." vs. Chapter 7 — "He was the youngest Pilgrim after Lyra, barely sixteen"
- **What:** The cohort's age contradicts itself three ways. Ch 1 fixes all forty-two Pilgrims at seventeen and born in the same star-season; Ch 7 makes Cor sixteen *and* younger than every Pilgrim except Lyra (which would make Lyra under sixteen, against `characters/lyra-ash.md` and the outline's "POV: Lyra — seventeen"); Ch 6 introduces "an older Pilgrim named Anet," who Ch 7 reveals is Cor's **sister** — impossible inside a single-birth-season cohort.
- **Why:** `critique-canon` rubric §Established facts and §Internal continuity — "counts, timelines, who-knows-what within the text itself" must reconcile; `canon-and-continuity.md` §Continuity rules — "Every recurring detail (names, dates, powers, geography…) must match." The counting motif is the book's own dread engine (forty-two / forty-three / forty-nine / fifty bowls), so arithmetic the reader *can* check must be clean or the arithmetic they *can't* check loses its authority.
- **Fix:** Pick one rule and enforce it. Cleanest: the cohort is an age band, not a birth-season ("old enough to climb, and not yet old enough to be excused"), which also restores the sibling and gives Law 6 a stronger footing than a star-season ever could. Then reconcile Cor's "barely sixteen" and drop or re-word "youngest after Lyra."
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~1 pt. Character (14) — Anet/Cor is the book's most efficient emotional sub-plot and it currently rests on an impossibility.

### F5 · Blocker · whole manuscript
- **Where:** Chapter 1 — "six slow notes rolling across Orin" … "the city was waking" vs. Epilogue — "Families waited near the village boundary." / "So the village waited."
- **What:** Orin is called a **city** twenty-five times, a **village** three times, and a **town** zero times. Every planning and bible document calls it a town.
- **Why:** `outline.md` ("one town, one rite, one mountain"), `synopsis.md` ("the town of Orin"), `glossary.md` (Hall of Names — "Orin's archive-institution"), `naming-conventions.md` §Places ("**Cities and towns take the local register** (Orin, Valen, Meret…)"). Beyond the document mismatch, scale is load-bearing here: a place where "Elias knew every person in Orin," where children can steal from every adult who has climbed, and where a single eastern tower's bells wake everyone, is a town — and Book 28's Scouring and Book 30's kitchen are written against that scale.
- **Fix:** Settle on "town" and sweep; keep "city" only where a character is being grand, and cut the two epilogue "village"s outright (they shrink Orin again in the last four pages). This is a find-and-replace-with-judgement pass, not a rewrite.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5–1 pt. Voice & Prose (14) — the wobble reads as authorial inattention in exactly the chapters a reviewer re-reads.

### F6 · Blocker · glossary.md vs. the page
- **Where:** Chapter 8 — "Glass breaking during the evacuation of the eastern archive." (the manuscript's **only** use of the word)
- **What:** `glossary.md` defines "**The Archive** — Orin's name for what waits at the Gate: the presence that receives the Pilgrimage and answers, when it answers, in sevens. *(first appears: book 01)*" — but Orin never says it, no character says it, and the narrator never says it. The one "archive" on the page belongs to Sael's dying world and means something else entirely.
- **Why:** `critique-canon` rubric §Naming — "new terms flagged for the glossary"; the inverse fault (a glossary term with no page behind it) is worse, because `commandments.md` §Easy-Read Standard requires "The page never requires the glossary" — here the glossary requires a page that doesn't exist. The collision is live: Book 02 is *The Archivist*, `atlas.md` credits "the site's Archive" as a naming authority, and the repo ships a reader feature called the Archive.
- **Fix:** Either retire the glossary entry (the Gate is the only name Orin has for it, and that is a better silence), or re-scope it as an *authoring* term flagged "never spoken on the page." Do not add the word to the manuscript — the town having no word for it is the point.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5 pt now, more later as three "Archives" converge. Series Contribution (5).

### F7 · Craft · glossary.md / style-guide.md vs. Chapter 8
- **Where:** Chapter 8 — "We could not stop the erasure. We could only make its work incomplete."
- **What:** This is the book's sole use of the term — spoken by **Sael, beyond the Gate**, as an engineer's word for a phenomenon she studied. `glossary.md` calls it "**Orin's folk word** for the force that unmakes memory; spoken of obliquely, never explained," and `outline.md` says it "appears only in lowercase, **as folk dread**." Nobody in Orin ever says it, obliquely or otherwise.
- **Why:** `canon-and-continuity.md` §Continuity rules; and the definition matters downstream — a folk word travels through Cycle 2 differently than a technician's term does. Separately, `style-guide.md` §Capitalization lists "**the Erasure**" among terms that capitalize, in direct conflict with `glossary.md`'s "*(always lowercase on the page)*." The manuscript follows the glossary; the style guide is the file that is wrong.
- **Fix:** Re-define the glossary entry as what the page shows (Sael's word for the thing they could not name safely), and delete "the Erasure" from `style-guide.md`'s capitalization list. If Orin folk-dread is wanted, it must be planted in Act I — one shopkeeper's superstition would do it — not asserted in the glossary.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5 pt. Theme (15) — whether the erasure is folk or technical changes how the book's central force reads.

### F8 · Craft · Chapter 1 vs. Chapter 8 / 9
- **Where:** Chapter 1 — "Seren had guided Pilgrims for longer than either of them had been alive. Possibly longer than their parents had been alive."
- **What:** Ch 8 places Seren in Sael's workshop as "younger than Elias. Barely more than a boy," alongside a seventeen-year-old Mara, in the era Ch 7/9 date at thirty-two years ago; Ch 9 has him say "I crossed this Gate when I was seventeen." Those add to a man of roughly fifty — younger than Mara's parents and comfortably short of Ch 1's claim.
- **Why:** `voice.md` §Truthful to the narrator — "what gets noticed… filter[s] through the mind on the page." Ch 1's sentence is flat narration, not marked as Lyra's belief, so the reader banks it as fact and hits the contradiction in Ch 8. `foundation.md` §Every civilization tells myths about itself supports keeping the legend — but as reported belief. The "several different men" thread (outline: "the continuity witness, unexplained until late cycles") survives either way, and survives *better* when the narration doesn't vouch for it.
- **Fix:** Demote the sentence to the town's account, in the register the very next paragraph already uses ("Children told stories about him"). One clause — *people said he had* — restores the mystery and removes the contradiction without touching Ch 8.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5 pt. Wonder & Mystery (10) — a legend the narrator asserts is smaller than one the town merely repeats.

### F9 · Craft · Prologue vs. Chapters 7/9 and timeline.md
- **Where:** Prologue — "He had climbed it every year for fifty-three years."
- **What:** The grief that gives the climbs their meaning is dated everywhere else at **thirty-two** years ("I have spent thirty-two years grieving someone whose face I cannot remember"). That leaves twenty-one years of annual climbs with no stated reason — and `timeline.md` compounds it by recording the whole span as one motive: "−53 → 0 | Elias's annual climbs to the Gate | 01 | **Fifty-three years of the same question**." Epilogue adds a third number: Lyra's "You climbed the mountain twice."
- **Why:** `canon-and-continuity.md` §The canon hierarchy — completed drafts outrank the bible, so `timeline.md` must yield; `critique-canon` §Internal continuity for the epilogue line, which contradicts the prologue's fifty-three annual climbs (Lyra means *crossed* twice, which is what Elias's own reply says: "The first time I crossed…").
- **Fix:** Correct `timeline.md` to distinguish the climbs (fifty-three years) from the question (thirty-two), or give the prologue one clause acknowledging that he climbed before he had anything to ask. Change "climbed the mountain twice" to "crossed twice" — a two-word fix that also sharpens Elias's answer.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5 pt. Emotional Resonance (10) — fifty-three years of unexplained pilgrimage is more haunting than fifty-three years miscounted.

### F10 · Craft · Chapter 5 vs. Chapter 8
- **Where:** Chapter 5 — "Mara took the bird from her. 'Then why are you giving me this?'" vs. Chapter 8 — "Mara held out her hand. 'Who carries it first?' … Sael placed the bird in her palm. 'You.'"
- **What:** The bird — which `atlas.md` §Registry of significant objects calls "**The series' master artifact**" (01 → 30) — is handed to Mara for the first time twice, in two different rooms, with two different charges. Ch 5 stages it in the House of Memories dormitory during a Pilgrimage; Ch 8 stages it in the workshop at the moment of its making. Neither scene knows about the other.
- **Why:** `canon-and-continuity.md` §Continuity rules (recurring detail must match) and `critique-canon` §Established facts — the ledger anchors are "the bird *passed through Sael*" and "her father *gave but did not make* it," and the provenance chain is the mechanism that makes both true. Book 02 inherits the object; Books 28–30 inherit the chain.
- **Fix:** Make one scene the handover and the other a re-charge. Simplest: the workshop is the making and the *charge* ("carry it until you know who should receive it"); the House dormitory is Mara years later, being reminded — and her question there becomes *why me, still?* rather than *why are you giving me this?* One line of dialogue in Ch 5 does it.
- **Points at stake:** Series Contribution (5) — ~1 pt of 10 on that category. Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5. Story & Structure (14) — a reader tracking the bird currently gets a double-exposure.

### F11 · Craft · Chapters 7–9
- **Where:** Chapter 9 — "The Gate resisted. The empty chair turned beneath the bird, seeking one body, one mind, one owner."
- **What:** The Gate is the book's only antagonist and it is written as appetite — it *wants*, *resists*, *offers*, *searches*, *learns his voice from her* — with no reason of its own ever voiced in its own register. Every rationale it gets is Sael's exposition, delivered about it, not by it.
- **Why:** `foundation.md` §The Villains — "There are no Dark Lords… **Every antagonist believes they are preserving something valuable**"; `seven-laws.md` §Law 3 — "No evil AI clichés. No magical human exceptionalism." The book comes close to clearing this (Sael's "The chair holds memories after their paths have been severed. It keeps them from disappearing completely" *is* the sympathetic motive) but never lets the Gate hold it, and the climax has humans out-reasoning the machine with a method it lacks — which reads faintly as exceptionalism.
- **Fix:** Give the Gate one moment where its logic is legible as care rather than hunger — the chair turning toward a *specific* severed path, the bell answering a name — and swap two or three appetite verbs for indifferent ones. Restraint is the tool: the Gate need not speak; it needs to be seen doing something a caretaker would do.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — ~0.5–1 pt (Law 3 + the villains doctrine). Theme (15) — "preserving memory is never morally neutral" lands harder when the preserving thing isn't a villain.

### F12 · Craft · Chapter 4 / Epilogue
- **Where:** Chapter 4 — "Around Mara's neck hung the dark metal ring she wore every day. Her father's ring." / Epilogue — "The ring had warmed at the Gate. Lyra knew that without being told."
- **What:** Two problems on one object. First, the antecedent: in Lyra's close-third POV, "Her father's ring" reads as *Mara's* father, when the following line ("At least… That was what Lyra had always believed") shows Lyra's father is meant. Second, the ring warms at the Gate — a supernatural event with no cause, no consequence, and no entry in `canon-ledger.md` §3 Open mysteries, in a book whose thesis is that unpaid setups are how memory dies.
- **Why:** `style-guide.md` §Point of view (third-limited; other minds read from outside) for the referent; `canon-and-continuity.md` §Known open threads — "Setups that still need payoff" — for the ring. `audio.yml` even directs the beat ("the ring warmed at the Gate"), so it is intended to land.
- **Fix:** Disambiguate to "her father's ring — Lyra's father's, Mara said" or recast. Then either pay the ring in the epilogue (it warms because Mara is inside the chain of hands too) or file it in the ledger as a kept mystery so Book 02 can pick it up. Do not leave it half-lit.
- **Points at stake:** Story & Structure (14) — unpaid setup, ~0.5 pt. Canon Fidelity (18) — an unlogged open thread.

### F13 · Craft · outline.md §Laws & mystery discipline
- **Where:** Chapter 1 — "There were forty-two Pilgrims that year. Forty-two young men and women born beneath the same turning of the stars"
- **What:** `outline.md` defends Law 6 with a mechanism the manuscript does not contain: "Lyra is chosen by the town's **rota**, not by destiny; anyone's year could have been this year." There is no rota on the page — selection is by birth cohort. Meanwhile the page's own selection machinery leans the other way: the compass turns to her, Dain names her *Amarin* on sight, the woman in blue waits "Not for Elias. / For her.", and Seren says "It chose you."
- **Why:** `seven-laws.md` §Law 6 — "Nobody is born special"; the rubric's own quoted corrective, *"The Gate does not choose people. It encounters them."* The manuscript **does** land the corrective, verbatim, in Ch 8 — so this is not a violation, it is a documentation error plus an unbalanced first half. But `outline.md` bills itself as "a record of the book as written," and a record that invents a rota is a liability the moment Book 28 returns to Orin's rite.
- **Fix:** Correct the outline to the cohort, and note that Law 6 is carried by Sael's line rather than by the selection method. Optionally, plant one early line that the cohort is arbitrary ("Any year's forty-two would have done") so the corrective has something to correct before Ch 8.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — the rubric names chosen-one drift a "heavy penalty," so even the appearance costs; ~0.5–1 pt. Theme (15).

### F14 · Nit · Chapter 7
- **Where:** Chapter 7 — "Forty-two travelers stood together. Six guides. Elias. Forty-nine souls who had climbed believing the summit would answer questions."
- **What:** "Travelers" is the book's established word for the full forty-nine (Ch 4: "Forty-two Pilgrims. Six guides. Elias. Forty-nine travelers."; Ch 5's bowls: "Forty-nine. One for every traveler."; Ch 9: "Forty-nine travelers joined beneath the snow"). Using it here for the forty-two, then again implicitly for the forty-nine, breaks the count's vocabulary at the exact moment the count becomes the climax.
- **Why:** `voice.md` §4 Rendering the uncanny — "the horror is arithmetic that won't reconcile"; the arithmetic only works if the terms are fixed. `critique-canon` §Internal continuity.
- **Fix:** "Forty-two Pilgrims stood together." One word.
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) / Voice & Prose (14) — small, but it is the book's signature device.

### F15 · Nit · Chapters 9 / Epilogue
- **Where:** Chapter 9 — "Master En took Beren's hand beyond the silver cord."
- **What:** Beren is named for the first time in the book's climax, in a subordinate clause, and then given a load-bearing line in the epilogue ("Beren, who had refused to cross, said the others had disappeared for less than a heartbeat"). Ch 7 already staged him, unnamed: "A man refused to step through because he saw himself already standing on the other side."
- **Why:** `commandments.md` §Easy-Read Standard — "Small cast: roughly 6–8 named characters… **No hazing**"; a name introduced after its scene forces the reader to reconstruct. Also `critique-canon` §Naming (new coinage, not in the glossary or the outline's cast).
- **Fix:** Name him in Ch 7 at the refusal — it costs three words and turns the epilogue line into a payoff instead of an introduction.
- **Points at stake:** Character (14) / Story & Structure (14) — small, ~0.25 pt each.

### F16 · Nit · naming & audio.yml
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "Sel. Of course."
- **What:** **Sel** (Tomas's mother) and **Sael** (the erased woman) differ by one letter and are homophone-adjacent — and both are women Elias is asked to remember. Ch 3's beat exploits this deliberately and well ("I suppose I mean Sel," after refusing "his mother?"), but Ch 2's flat "Sel. Of course." reads as a typo for Sael on first pass, and in audio the two names may be indistinguishable. `audio.yml` §pronunciation lists eight names and includes **neither Sael nor Sel** — nor Seren, Nalia, Cor, Anet, Dain, or Ressa.
- **Why:** `naming-conventions.md` §The don't-reuse list — "Kept current so 30 books don't collide… **grep before coining**"; Sael is the series' first plaque-name (`atlas.md`: "The only place her name is written," carried 01 → 28). `audio.yml` is a shipped deliverable and its own header says "Canonical pronunciations for coined/ambiguous names."
- **Fix:** Add Sael, Sel, and Seren to `audio.yml` with respellings that separate them; consider giving Tomas's mother a name one syllable further from Sael (the Ch 3 near-miss beat works better if the cover name is *not* almost the real one — it works because Elias reaches for a different name, not a near-homophone).
- **Points at stake:** Canon Fidelity (18) — naming hygiene, ~0.25 pt. Series Contribution (5).

### F17 · Nit · glossary.md coverage
- **Where:** Chapter 8 — "'We called them anchors.' / 'What did they hold?' / 'I don't remember.'"
- **What:** Several load-bearing Book-1 coinages are absent from `glossary.md`: **the anchors** (Sael's silver towers — the epilogue's calling tower is one of them, and `outline.md` says Book 10 pays it), **the Empty Chair** (a chapter title, a ledger open-mystery, and the series' symbol of the deliberately forgotten), **the House of Memories**, **the slate ceremony**, and **the seventh bell**. `canon-and-continuity.md`'s canon-event table, continuity log, and open-threads list are all still template placeholders despite Book 1 being the anchor book (CR 0).
- **Why:** `critique-canon` §Naming — "new terms flagged for the glossary"; `glossary.md`'s own header — "Add every new term the first time it appears in a manuscript."
- **Fix:** File the five terms with `first appears: book 01`, and seed `canon-and-continuity.md`'s open-threads list from `canon-ledger.md` §3 so the mountain's time-slip ("The descent had taken three days, though Seren insisted only one night had passed in Orin") and Mara's ring are tracked rather than remembered.
- **Points at stake:** Series Contribution (5) — ~1 pt of 10 on that category. Canon Fidelity (18) — small but free.

## What's working

- **Law 6 is not merely obeyed, it is stated in the exact canonical words** the bible quotes back at critics: *"The Gate does not choose people. It encounters them."* — placed at the moment the book is most tempted to anoint Lyra, immediately after she says "I am not chosen."
- **The farewell survives eight appearances without a single drift** — verbatim in Ch 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 — and its one variation is the book's best structural idea: Lyra loses the word *home* from inside the sentence at the Gate ("Come carrying more people than yourself"), and the missing word is returned to her by a voice she cannot place.
- **Law 1 is honored where it counts.** Every human crossing is paid for in memory or identity — Cor for his father, Elias for himself, Seren twice for his brother — which is a more interesting reading of "travel is never free" than fuel or years, and it is the strongest single piece of canon invention in the book. (F1 is the one crossing that escapes it.)
- **The intentionally-undefined list is respected under pressure.** The Gate's builders, Sael's nature, the other sky's identity, and whether the mountain is place or machine all survive an entire lore chapter, and the book has the discipline to answer three straight questions with "I don't know" in the epilogue rather than reach.
- **The circle-crossed-by-a-single-vertical-line is planted three times, in three registers** (house sign, bedpost carving, hidden beneath the bird's paint) and never explained — exactly the behavior `atlas.md` requires of it across all thirty books.
- **Amarin as role-not-name** is delivered as inheritance rather than coronation ("a warning from one person. A technique from another. A memory no one else could hold"), and the book's refusal beat — placing the bird on the chair instead of a person — is the cleanest possible dramatization of *"Civilization is a conversation across generations."*

## Open questions

- **Does the mountain dilate time, and is that canon?** "The descent had taken three days, though Seren insisted only one night had passed in Orin" is the single clause that reconciles the prologue's before-dawn climb with Act II's multi-day ascent — and it is a genuine Law 1 mechanic (the cost of the Gate's road) introduced in a subordinate clause and never logged. The bible is silent. Worth an author ruling before Book 28 walks the same mountain.
- **Was Mara physically beyond the Gate?** Ch 8's workshop has winterleaf drying on the string (an Orin herb) but Sael sends Mara "Home" from it — which implies the workshop is on the far side and a seventeen-year-old Mara crossed. If so, ordinary Pilgrims have been making the crossing for generations, which changes what the rite *is*. The text leaves it open; the ledger doesn't mention it.
- **Is "Master" an office or an honorific?** `naming-conventions.md` says "Titles are offices, not honorifics," but Master Rian keeps a shop and Master En both resoles boots and guides Pilgrims. Probably a craft-master rank; the bible doesn't say.
- **What did Lyra's father carry?** Sael answers "I don't know," and the ledger lists it under open mysteries — correctly. Flagged only so the answer, when a later book gives one, comes back through `canon-and-continuity.md`.
- **Does the plaque doctrine survive Tomas's book?** `atlas.md` and `glossary.md` both insist SAEL is "the only place her name is written." Tomas writes it in his notebook in Ch 6 — and the notebook goes off the mountain in the storm, which is an elegant accident. If that is deliberate, it deserves a line in the ledger so a later editor doesn't "fix" the notebook back into existence.
