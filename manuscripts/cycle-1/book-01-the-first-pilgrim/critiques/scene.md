# Critique — scene — The First Pilgrim (full manuscript, Prologue–Epilogue, 28,550w)
_run: 2026-08-03 · standards: writing-guide/voice.md §3 (choreography + conversation layers), §6 (emotion in the body, banned templates), §1, §8, Do/Avoid · .claude/skills/critique-scene/SKILL.md rubric · book audio.yml (per-chapter mood) · outline.md · .claude/skills/score/SKILL.md (points at stake)_

**Verdict:** The bodies are here but they are all doing the same three things — the show-don't-name revision replaced named emotion with a *single* gaze template repeated eleven times, left 45 dialogue runs of five-plus lines with no body at all, and let the book's one honest confession of ignorance ("I don't know") leak into every mouth in the cast; the climax is also physically unstageable.

## Findings

### F1 · Blocker · Chapter 9
- **Where:** Chapter 9 — "She turned toward the Pilgrims. “Take someone’s hand.”"
- **What:** Lyra's position at the climax is physically impossible: she walks to the empty chair — which is inside the white chamber *beyond* the threshold — places the bird on it, then is simultaneously the anchor end of a 49-person hand-chain standing on the plateau, and later still has a hand on the carving ("Lyra placed her hand on the carving") while "Tomas squeezed her hand."
- **Why:** voice.md §3, *the blocking test* — "could two actors stage the scene from the prose alone?" Two cannot; forty-nine certainly cannot. The chamber's location is fixed by Ch 8 ("The Gate appeared between the chairs") and by Ch 9 itself, where Elias must separately "reach the threshold" — which places Lyra *not* at the threshold. Crossing is also the book's most costly act (Ch 7–8); Lyra apparently performs its equivalent and the text never notices.
- **Fix:** Fix the geometry, not the drama — either the chair comes to the threshold (the Gate has been reshaping itself all chapter, so let the reader *see* it advance), or Lyra reaches through and the text pays for the reach with a body-cost beat. Then hold her position constant: one place for her hand, one place for Tomas's.
- **Points at stake:** Story & Structure (14) — a climax the reader cannot see costs ~1 pt; drags Emotional Resonance (10) with it.

### F2 · Craft · Chapters 1–9, Epilogue (book-wide)
- **Where:** Epilogue — "“I saw another world.”" (eleven consecutive bare exchanges, ending "“Is it still there?”")
- **What:** 45 separate runs of five or more clipped exchanges carry no body, object, or attribution at all; the worst are 11 lines (Epilogue), 8 lines (Ch 5, twice), and fourteen runs of 7.
- **Why:** voice.md §3 — "Never run more than ~3 clipped exchanges without a body. A long volley with no hands, no objects, no attribution reads as a list and loses the speakers — the reader tracks who is talking by counting lines." The Epilogue run is also the book's *last* two-hander and closes on the flattest possible ladder.
- **Fix:** Do not add attribution — add *bodies*, one per four lines, drawn from repertoire already established (Elias's knees, the bird turning under lamplight, Lyra's head against the chair seat). Prioritize the 8+ runs at Ch 5 lines "“What are you writing?”" and "“You kicked me.”", the Ch 3 Mara volleys, and the Epilogue floor scene.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) — this is the single largest deduction on the page-level rubric, worth ~1.5 pts; Character (14) ~0.5.

### F3 · Craft · Chapters 1–9, Epilogue (book-wide)
- **Where:** Chapter 4 — "Lyra watched the old caretaker take his place among them. “I don’t know.”"
- **What:** "I don't know" is spoken 30 times by Lyra, Tomas, Mara, Elias, Seren, and Sael — six different people using one identical sentence as their deflection.
- **Why:** voice.md §3, *cover-the-names* — the guide names this exact defect by name: "Book 1 hands *'I don't know'* to nearly every speaker 29 times; a phrase that belongs to everyone characterizes no one." It also spends Elias's canonical refrain (§5: "I don't know." / "You keep saying that." / "I keep meaning it.") — by the time Elias says it in Ch 4, four other characters have worn it in.
- **Fix:** Ration it to Elias (refrain) plus one genuine Tomas instance (Ch 2's "the rarest sentence in the Tomas language" is built on its rarity — protect that joke by making it true). Everyone else deflects through their own engine: Tomas by admitting a data gap, Mara by putting her hands back on something, Seren by declining rather than confessing, Sael by answering a different question.
- **Points at stake:** Character (14) — ~1–1.5 pts; also protects the litany under Canon Fidelity (18).

### F4 · Craft · Chapters 1–2, 4–7, 9, Epilogue
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "Then his eyes rose to hers, and waited. “May I?”"
- **What:** The revision's gaze-vector fix hardened into one template — *eyes go to the object, then come back/up to the other person's eyes* — used at least eleven times across the book (Ch 1 "Then they came up and held hers"; Ch 3 "Her mother’s eyes came up from the knot and held hers"; Ch 4 "His eyes went to the pocket, not the coat, and came back to the road"; Ch 5 "His eyes went to her hand and stayed there"; Ch 6 "His eyes came back from the wind" and "His eyes returned to hers"; Ch 8 "Elias’s eyes came back from the dark tower"; Ch 9 "His eyes went to her wrist, then came back up"; Epilogue "Her eyes went to it before they went to him").
- **Why:** voice.md §3 — "Ration it like salt… Never the same gesture twice in a scene… Tic-spam — perpetual nodding, eyebrow theater — is worse than flatness." One vector shape repeated eleven times is eyebrow theater with better grammar.
- **Fix:** Keep three — the strongest are Ch 1's bird ("Then they came up and held hers") and Ch 3's knot. Convert the rest to non-ocular turns from each character's repertoire: Tomas aligns/counts, Mara's hands find work, Elias goes still, Seren's hands stop on the rope (already used well once, Ch 6).
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) — ~0.75 pt.

### F5 · Craft · Chapters 1–9, Epilogue (book-wide)
- **Where:** Chapter 5 — "Seren looked at her. “No, we didn’t.”"
- **What:** ~40 dialogue beats are the bare formula *X looked at Y.* attached to a line, and "looked toward" appears 51 times as the default blocking move; "looked" totals 175.
- **Why:** voice.md Do/Avoid — "beats that only attribute (blink-and-nod wallpaper — speakers who never *see* each other)"; §3 blocking test — "If every look is 'at each other' and every beat is attribution, revise." Also the sense-variation rule: "a scene rendered only through the eyes goes flat — Book 1 says *looked* 176 times; rotate in sound, weight, temperature, smell."
- **Fix:** Triage: a bare *looked at* survives only where the look is the reply. Elsewhere either cut it (the line stands) or replace with a non-visual channel — the cold of the cup, the smell of winterleaf, the weight of the pack, the sound of the bell — the book already owns all four.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) — ~0.75 pt.

### F6 · Craft · Chapter 5
- **Where:** Chapter 5 — "Mara looked down at the bird. For one impossible instant she found herself holding it the way you hold something that belongs to someone you love."
- **What:** In the Mara dream — the chapter's emotional centerpiece per audio.yml — the POV evaporates. The prose opens correctly ("Lyra tried to speak. The body whose eyes she occupied answered"), then for ~40 lines simply calls the speaker "Mara" and reports *Mara's* interiority, which Lyra cannot access.
- **Why:** voice.md, *truthful to the narrator* — "What gets noticed, what it gets compared to, and which words are available all filter through the mind on the page… An image the narrator couldn't have made is dishonest even when it's beautiful." The whole horror of the scene is Lyra riding inside her mother; the moment the camera becomes Mara's, the reader loses the body they were promised.
- **Fix:** Keep Lyra inside the borrowed body for every line: what Mara's mouth says, Lyra hears from the wrong side; what Mara feels, Lyra can only infer from the body's behaviour (the throat tightening, the hand closing before she decides to close it). Cut "she found herself holding it" — render the grip and let Lyra fail to name it.
- **Points at stake:** Emotional Resonance (10) — ~0.75 pt; Voice & Prose (14) ~0.25.

### F7 · Craft · Chapter 2
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "Tomas opened his mouth. Closed it again. Even he seemed unsure how to respond."
- **What:** The chapter's highest-stakes beat — a boy handing a stranger the only object left of his dead mother, and being told it still works "but not in the way it was made to" — closes on narrator gloss rather than a body, and Lyra, the POV, is a camera: she neither reacts, intervenes, nor is looked at throughout the exchange.
- **Why:** voice.md §6 — "Put the feeling in the body… give it a physical carrier: hands, breath, voice, an object"; the guide's own exemplar for exactly this beat is *"his shoulders came down half an inch"*, spent thirty lines earlier on Elias. Compounded by the Chapter-Two Rule (memory/chapter-two-hook-rule): this is the chapter the reader is hooked in, and its detonation is summarized.
- **Fix:** Give the beat two bodies — Tomas's hands on the compass when it comes back (the later beat "Tomas put the compass away with both hands" is the right instinct, arriving three scenes too late), and Lyra's reaction, which is currently missing from her own POV chapter. The narrator should say nothing.
- **Points at stake:** Story & Structure (14) ~0.5 (Chapter-Two Rule); Emotional Resonance (10) ~0.5.

### F8 · Craft · Chapter 2
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "“For saying it like you believe it.”"
- **What:** The western-wall closing scene has no collision: Lyra wants to be told she is coming back, Tomas wants to tell her, and he does. Both parties want the same thing and get it.
- **Why:** voice.md §3, *two agendas* — "Every conversation is two wants colliding… a scene where both speakers want the same thing and have it is a report, not a conversation."
- **Fix:** Give Tomas a second want that costs him — he believes she is coming back *and* he has evidence he has not shown her (the compass just moved, and he will not say what he thinks it means). Let the reassurance be the thing he offers *instead* of the truth (Gerwig's said-instead), and the scene acquires a floor.
- **Points at stake:** Story & Structure (14) ~0.5; Character (14) ~0.25.

### F9 · Craft · Chapters 1, 3, 5
- **Where:** Chapter 3 — "And all through the night, Lyra had the strange feeling that someone was dancing with them whom no one else could see."
- **What:** Three of the book's major hooks are planted by the narrator announcing that Lyra has a *feeling*: "the unreasonable feeling that one name was missing" (Ch 1), the dancer (Ch 3), "Lyra could not escape the feeling that someone had just left" (Ch 5).
- **Why:** voice.md §3 — "Hooks go in the hands. Foreshadowing obeys the same law: a hook planted by narrator emphasis reads blunt and flat at once… The camera must not know it is foreshadowing; only the hands may know." All three land on chapter/scene closers, which doubles the underlining.
- **Fix:** Convert each to behaviour: Ch 1's missing name into her finger stopping on a blank stretch of carving and moving on; Ch 3's dancer into a partner's hand arriving a half-beat before someone reaches for it, or the count of dancers in a ring not matching; Ch 5's departure into the door upstairs that closes (already there — let it carry the beat alone).
- **Points at stake:** Wonder & Mystery (10) ~0.75 — this is the axis most cheaply raised.

### F10 · Craft · Chapters 8–9, Epilogue
- **Where:** Chapter 9 — "Pain entered her."
- **What:** Emotion is repeatedly the grammatical agent doing something to the character: "Pain entered her" · "The promise pierced her" · "The distinction struck through her" · "Pain moved through her" · plus five instances of the abstraction-travel template "X moved through Lyra" ("The word moved through Lyra", "Mara's farewell moved through Lyra", "The phrase moved through Lyra", "The distinction moved through the chamber").
- **Why:** voice.md §6, *banned templates* — "*'[Emotion] rose / arrived / came'* with the feeling as agent." *Entered*, *pierced*, *struck through*, *moved through* are the same construction wearing different verbs, and Ch 9 spends three of them inside forty lines of the climax.
- **Fix:** Give each a carrier that already exists in the scene: the bird's heat in the palm, the cord on the wrist, the cold of the chair's wood, the breath she cannot finish. "Pain entered her" in particular should be the hand on the carving doing something the hand does.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) ~0.5; Emotional Resonance (10) ~0.25.

### F11 · Craft · Chapters 3, 4, 7
- **Where:** Chapter 3 — "Lyra felt no different. She had expected something. Relief. Grief. A sudden understanding that childhood was over."
- **What:** Labeled-abstraction lists used as emotional shorthand: the slate triplet above, and Ch 4's "Not surprise. Recognition."
- **Why:** voice.md §6 — the banned "labeled triplet: *'Recognition. Affection. Loss.'*" Negating the triplet ("she felt no different… Relief. Grief.") does not exempt it; the reader is still handed nouns instead of a body. Ch 4's pair also lands on Mara at her most important unspoken moment.
- **Fix:** Ch 3 already has the true line one paragraph down — "she felt only the absence of the slate in her hand." Cut the triplet and let the hand carry it. Ch 4: render what Mara's face does when a locked door swings open; the simile is doing the work already, the labels undo it.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) ~0.4.

### F12 · Craft · Chapters 5, 6, 8
- **Where:** Chapter 8 — "“I remember building them.”"
- **What:** Sael and Seren run on one engine and share Elias's signature construction. Elias's *I remember [gerund]-ing it* — the book's most characterful syntax ("I recognized remembering it", "I remember remembering it", "I remember seeing it") — is handed to Sael twice ("I remember building them", "I remember giving it to Mara"); and Sael's flat one-word refusals are indistinguishable from Seren's. Cover the names in Ch 8's plain sequence and Sael could be Seren throughout.
- **Why:** voice.md §3 — "Every major character gets a speech engine… when a line could belong to anyone, run it through the engine until it can't"; the cover-the-names test. voice.md gives Seren's engine ("a door held almost shut") but the book never differentiates Sael's from it.
- **Fix:** Name Sael's engine and hold it — she is an architect who divided herself, so everything she says could run as *a specification with one clause deliberately missing* (she states scope, then withholds the referent), which is audibly different from Seren's refusal-at-the-door. Return the *I remember X-ing* construction to Elias exclusively; it is his tragedy's grammar.
- **Points at stake:** Character (14) ~0.75; Wonder & Mystery (10) ~0.25 (Sael reads as mystery-shaped rather than person-shaped).

### F13 · Craft · Chapters 1–9 (book-wide)
- **Where:** Chapter 4 — "Elias stopped. He went entirely still, as though the answer stood directly in front of him."
- **What:** Gesture economy fails on three repeats: *went still* appears seven times (five of them Elias, twice with a banned absolute-modifier — "entirely still", "utterly still", "perfectly still"); Seren's *hand/grip tightened* runs twice within two chapters ("Seren's hand tightened", "Seren's grip tightened"); and the *smile held one beat too long* appears twice, both Elias (Ch 7, Ch 9).
- **Why:** voice.md §3 — "Never the same gesture twice in a scene; gestures from each character's established repertoire; a gesture must change the meaning of its line." Also §Do/Avoid, absolutes: "*completely still* and *utterly silent* prop what cannot be propped."
- **Fix:** Keep one *went still* per act, at most, and strip every modifier from it. Give Elias a second repertoire move that is his (the staff, the loosened hair cord, the hand that reaches and stops), and Seren a second that is his (the rope coil, the knife — Nalia's Ch 9 line establishes it, so plant it earlier). Keep only the Ch 9 held smile, which is doing real work.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) ~0.4.

### F14 · Craft · Chapters 1–3, 5, 7
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "Then he took the compass with both hands, the way you take something that is still warm."
- **What:** One comparison shape — *the way you [verb]* — carries seven of the book's key gestures ("the way you set down something heavy", "the way you glance at someone to share a thought too small for words", "the way you watch a locked door swing open", "the way you hold something that belongs to someone you love", "the way you correct a stranger", "the way a man looks at a face he has spent years grieving"). Each time, the gesture is rendered and then *glossed* for the reader.
- **Why:** voice.md, sentence music — "vary… **structures** (the same grammatical shape repeated… drones even when lengths differ)"; and §3's economy law: if the gesture needs the gloss, the gesture is wrong; if it doesn't, the gloss is padding.
- **Fix:** Keep the two that are unarguably load-bearing ("the way you correct a stranger", "the way you take something that is still warm"). For the rest, cut the gloss and make the physical half do the whole job.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) ~0.4.

### F15 · Craft · Chapters 7, 9
- **Where:** Chapter 9 — "“He became more complete.”"
- **What:** Forty-nine people are onstage for the book's two biggest sequences and behave as scenery. Seren's confession runs ~35 lines with four crowd beats total ("Nalia closed her eyes", "No one moved", "Silence covered the summit", "The wind sharpened"); Ch 7's crossings compress every Pilgrim who isn't named into one summary paragraph ("A woman emerged remembering children she had never borne…").
- **Why:** voice.md §3 blocking test — a scene the reader cannot stage. The inertness costs Ch 9 specifically: the hand-chain payoff requires the crowd to have been *people* for two chapters, and they haven't been.
- **Fix:** You do not need more characters — you need three recurring extras with one body each (the woman who remembers children she never bore is already the best of them, and she reappears in the chain and the Epilogue; give her a hand or a breath in Ch 7 and Ch 9's confession). One reaction per movement of the confession is enough.
- **Points at stake:** Emotional Resonance (10) ~0.5; Character (14) ~0.25.

### F16 · Craft · Epilogue
- **Where:** Epilogue — "One by one, people entered the Hall. Not to add names. To add details."
- **What:** The book's civic payoff — a town learning to keep each other's small memories, the literal thesis in action — is delivered as a summary list of who wrote what, then a count ("thirty-seven entries… one hundred and twelve"). No hands hold a pen, no one hesitates, no one is watched writing.
- **Why:** voice.md §6 and §3 — the sentiment is stated rather than staged; the guide's rule is "Earn sentiment, then undercut or exit fast", and the earning is the part that has been skipped. The Ch 9 chain earned its equivalent moment by giving each memory a mouth and a reply.
- **Fix:** Stage one entry fully — a person who cannot decide what to write, an argument over a detail, a crossing-out (Ressa's "crossed out *correct* and replaced it with *new*" is exactly right and is the only one that lands) — then let the count carry the rest.
- **Points at stake:** Emotional Resonance (10) ~0.5; Theme & Core Question (15) ~0.25.

### F17 · Craft · Chapter 3
- **Where:** Chapter 3 — "He moved beneath the table. The bell vanished. The children scattered. Mara continued counting."
- **What:** Prop realism: a brass bell hanging from the corner of a market stall — established two paragraphs earlier as the thing Mara rings to be heard across the market — is unhooked and carried off in total silence, then rings freely the instant it is away ("The stolen bell rang somewhere in the crowd").
- **Why:** critique-scene rubric, prop realism — the founding instance ("do you wind a compass?"). A bell announces its own theft; that is what bells are for.
- **Fix:** Let it ring as it comes off the hook — one note, wrong, in the middle of the market — and Mara's *not looking up* becomes a much better joke than her not hearing.
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) ~0.2; small, cheap, and the fix improves the gag.

### F18 · Nit · Chapter 2
- **Where:** Chapter 2 — "turning it until the dial’s north faced north"
- **What:** Prop realism: the compass has been established as broken — "The needle wandered lazily instead of settling north" — yet Tomas orients its dial to true north, which requires a north reference the instrument can no longer give him.
- **Why:** critique-scene rubric, prop realism — "would the object actually behave/be operated that way?"
- **Fix:** One clause: he lines the dial up against something he can see — the eastern tower, the setting sun, the road out of Orin — which is also more Tomas (he would use a landmark and say so).
- **Points at stake:** Voice & Prose (14) ~0.1; protects the beat's credibility at the chapter's hook.

### F19 · Nit · Chapter 6
- **Where:** Chapter 6 — "Its leather cover had caught on a shard of stone below the edge. He held it with three fingers."
- **What:** The notebook is simultaneously snagged on rock and held in his fingers, so "His fingers opened. The notebook fell" does not follow from the staging the reader was given.
- **Why:** voice.md §3, the blocking test — the beat is the emotional peak of Tomas's arc and its physics are contradictory for two sentences.
- **Fix:** Pick one. The stronger is the snag: the book is *held* by the mountain, and what Tomas has to open is not his grip on the book but his grip on the rock — which makes Lyra's "Your mother is not in that book!" the line that unclenches a hand that was killing him.
- **Points at stake:** Story & Structure (14) ~0.2; Emotional Resonance (10) ~0.2.

### F20 · Nit · Chapter 4
- **Where:** Chapter 4 — "“You haven’t joined a Pilgrimage in thirty-two years.”"
- **What:** Seren tells Elias a fact about Elias that both men have known for thirty-two years, in a public square, for the reader's benefit.
- **Why:** voice.md §3, *the as-you-know ban* — "No character may tell another what both already know so the reader can overhear it… If the reader needs it, route it through the person who genuinely doesn't know (Book 1 has Pilgrims for this)."
- **Fix:** The square is full of the exact people the guide names. Put the number in a Pilgrim's mouth as a whisper behind Lyra, or in the old woman who already says "No" — she has the years and the disbelief; Seren keeps only "You intend to climb."
- **Points at stake:** Character (14) ~0.2.

## What's working
- **Elias and the compass, Ch 2** — the hook obeys "hooks go in the hands" exactly: the tap on cracked glass, the needle's tremble toward the mountain, and then "Twice, Tomas's hand went to the satchel flap and came away without opening it." That last beat is the best piece of choreography in the book — the camera never says what it means.
- **The Cor/Anet crossing, Ch 7 and its return in Ch 9** — "He said it gently, the way you correct a stranger" and "He took her hand. 'But I believe you.'" Belief without memory, carried entirely by hands and a politeness that wounds. It is also the only beat that pays off twice and gets funnier and sadder both times ("That sounds like a sister.").
- **The Ch 9 chain of small memories** — Nalia's knife line, Ressa's verses, the honey in the tea: each memory is a *reply* to a person, not a statement to the room, which is the thesis argued in dialogue form rather than asserted.
- **Mara's farewell, Chs 3–4** — "Mara's hand went back to the knot she had already tied, checking it again" and the collar that "did not need adjusting." Repertoire used with precision: her hands work while her eyes refuse to.
- **Tomas's notebook drop, Ch 6** — the said-instead at its best: "Your mother is not in that book!" is the sentence that is *not* about the notebook, and Lyra's earlier deflection ("That your left boot is leaking") is the same law played for mercy.

## Open questions
- **Sael's speech engine is undefined.** voice.md §3 assigns engines to Tomas, Mara, Elias, and Seren; Sael — who speaks for most of Ch 8 and delivers the book's thesis — has none, in voice.md or in a character sheet (`series-bible/characters/` holds only `lyra-ash.md`). Until one exists, F12's collision with Seren is a guide gap, not an authorial error.
- **The gesture repertoire for Lyra as POV.** The guide names what Tomas, Mara, Lyra, and Elias *do* in a conversation, but Lyra is the narrator for the whole book and therefore rarely gets a body of her own. Whether a first-among-equals POV should carry a visible repertoire, or should be the one character rendered from inside only, is not settled anywhere in the guide.
- **Motif gestures and the economy rule.** "Two fingers to the heart" appears 16 times and is canonically fixed (§5, verbatim reproduction). Whether a canonical motif is exempt from "never the same gesture twice in a scene" — it appears three times inside the Ch 9 finale — is not stated. I have treated it as exempt.
- **Whether the missing traveler's touch should ever have a body.** "Lyra felt another hand close around her empty left hand. Warm. Familiar." is the book's best unrendered gesture, deliberately. If a later book renders it, the restraint here becomes a setup; if not, it is the one hook the scene layer intentionally leaves cold.
