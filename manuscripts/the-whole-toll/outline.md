# The Whole Toll — Volume Outline

> ⚠️ **SPOILER BANNER — READ BEFORE SHARING.** This outline knows how the series comes
> out. The **volume itself** (FULL-ARC default) also knows: story 6 includes the feel of
> the resolution — from outside, unexplained. The stories spoil **no scene, twist, or
> reveal of any novella** (that is the design law), but this planning document names the
> beats they must steer around, which means the document — unlike the book — is spoilers
> throughout. Hand a collaborator the finished volume freely; hand them this file only if
> they are cleared for the whole series.

- **Volume:** *The Whole Toll* (working title, author's) — companion volume, the front
  door to the legendarium: six stories → thirty novellas → successor works.
- **Shape:** six short stories, one per cycle, in cycle order, inside a thin Archive
  frame. ~5,000 words each, **~30,400 total** — one novella's length that buys the whole
  universe. Defense of the grid in one line: *the escalation is the volume's plot, so the
  order is the structure.*
- **Status:** Outlined.
- **House law in force:** Easy-Read Standard (each story is one sitting, ~15 minutes,
  never lost); the Seven Laws absolute; the Writer's Test in miniature per story; voice.md
  governs the prose; naming-conventions.md governs every name (all new coinage grepped
  against the don't-reuse list 2026-08 — see the names ledger at the end).

## Premise

Six keepers of small offices — a shop ledger, a ferry manifest, a harbor log, a long
shelf, a family field, a relay board — each want one ordinary thing the universe will
not give: a name back, an answer from home, a mind mended, a perfect copy, a grave in
their own ground, a successor at the door. Read in order, their six refusals walk the
reader from one lamplit town to the whole species taking up a watch — and spell out,
without ever explaining it, what the keeping of memory costs and who pays. The title
carries the double meaning and the book earns both: the **Toll** is this universe's
count of years since the First Bell, and a toll is also the price. The series' thesis
in one sentence: **preserving memory is never morally neutral.**

Each story does exactly three jobs and no fourth: it carries its cycle's central
**argument**; it carries its cycle's **era and scale**; and it carries **one beat** of a
six-beat escalation delivered only as effects — never a mechanism, never a name. Every
story is **new** — never a compression or retelling of its cycle's five novellas — and
every POV is a character the novellas do not centre. A reader who later reaches the
thirty finds nothing pre-empted: only things that already mean something.

## The frame — an Archive artifact

The volume presents in-universe as a record compiled at the Archive **after the fact, by
something that has already counted the whole toll**. That is what makes its foreknowledge
a premise rather than a disclosure. The frame is deliberately thin — Easy-Read forbids a
puzzle wrapper:

- **A foreword** (~150 words): six entries, drawn from small ledgers; the Toll named as
  both count and price; the compiler identifying itself only as the one that kept the
  count. *Register sketch (drafter's latitude):* “The Toll is the count of years since
  the First Bell. It is also what the keeping cost. Both readings are correct.”
- **One headnote line per story** — ledger register, Toll-dated (this is where the Toll
  lives; the prose inside every story says “years”). *Sketches:* “Entered from the trade
  rolls of Orin, Toll 7,204. The coin was real.” · “Entered from a ferry manifest closed
  at Bray, Toll 7,520. The crossing was paid.” · “Entered from the gray volumes of a
  daughter-house, Toll 7,597. The water is still argued.” · “Entered from the town roll
  of Dray, Toll 7,662. The variants survive.” · “Entered from the tribunal record at
  Moss, Toll 7,689. The field is closed.” · “Entered from a relay board on a thin road,
  Toll 7,705. The watch continues.”
- **An afterword** (~100 words, FULL-ARC only): the count closed; the watch not. The
  phrase **“the keeper's keeper”** may appear here once, and only here, as inherited folk
  role-name — never in any story's mouth, and its private name appears nowhere, per the
  standing law.
- **The compiler's identity is never given.** That is the volume's own standing mystery —
  the Writer's Test at volume scale.

## Reading order

- **Designed to be read first** — before any of the thirty. FULL-ARC is the default and
  the real onboarding document: thematically coherent with a series whose thesis is that
  inheriting what happened does not diminish the carrying of it.
- **Equally valid after, or anywhere between.** Each story spoils nothing of its cycle's
  five books; a returning reader finds the small figures (Rian's lamp, the harbor-mind's
  gray volume, Neva's era, Yent's courtesy) already meaning something — Design Law 3's
  ideal running in both directions.
- **Within the volume: read in order.** The escalation is the plot; the arguments are one
  argument only in sequence.
- The single fork: a new reader who insists on reaching Book 30 with the resolution
  unknown takes the **THRESHOLD variant of story 6** (see the spoiler posture — a switch
  on one story's final movement, nothing else).

## The escalation spine (one beat per story, effects only)

1. **A hole with edges** — a debt with no debtor; rites kept without reasons, like walls
   built against a weather nobody alive has seen. *(private, deniable)*
2. **A place falls out of “now”** — silence with no author and no bill; the dark between
   feels recently empty. *(uncaused, unbilled)*
3. **The old things do not die whole** — an untaught instinct to divide the keeping into
   many disagreeing hands. *(defense as inherited reflex)*
4. **The losses have taste — and the taste errs** — the finished and sole-kept go clean;
   the argued-over are passed over; then it takes the same thing twice, hesitates,
   returns. *(kept by something failing)*
5. **The toll already counted** — under every settled world, a golden age so thoroughly
   taken its absence was never a mystery; no document anywhere names the taker.
   *(the missing word lived in like weather)*
6. **The fever breaks** — the takings ease; nothing defeated, nothing explained; what
   arrives is not victory but a roster. *(relief as inheritance)*

At root the Erasure stays unnamable — true in Cycle 5 of the series itself, so true
here. No story capitalizes it, names it as a force, or shows a mechanism.

## Story 1 — The Unclaimed Lamp

- **Cycle:** 1 — *Fragments of the First Memory* · **Era:** ~CR −10 · Toll 7,204 (a
  generation before the Pilgrimage of Lyra Ash; an ordinary year, the folk dread not yet
  public) · **Target:** ~5,000 words.
- **POV:** Master Rian, Orin's lamp-oil shopkeeper — the blue hat, the chalk handprints,
  the man who holds pilgrims' hands too long and means every word. Fleshed in Book 1's
  background (`memories/book-01.yml`, orin-workaday), centred nowhere. **Why him:** a
  shopkeeper's ledger is the town's memory in trade form, so the fragment can reach him
  without one mechanism on the page — and the series later runs its first vanishing-name
  device on Rian himself (Book 9's trade-roll blank). A volume reader who loved him here
  arms that beat perfectly, provided this story never sees it coming.
- **Logline:** An honest shopkeeper finds a paid winter of lamp oil in his ledger under a
  name the world no longer contains, and goes door to door trying to return a debt no one
  will admit exists.
- **Argument (Cycle 1):** Memory is carriage, not storage — a person survives only as a
  weight other people agree to share, plural, contradictory, never free; every keeping is
  paid for by a keeper.
- **The fragment as experienced (beat 1 of 6):** a hole with edges — a debt with no
  debtor, a column whose name will not come to anyone's mouth, a dark lamp somewhere in
  town. Nobody investigates, because there is nothing to investigate. What answers it is
  the town's oldest habits, kept without reasons — and standing in a doorway at night,
  sum re-done and still short by one, he feels the rain those walls were built for is
  still falling.

**Beat sheet**

1. An ordinary market morning in Orin; the ledger's hole with edges — a winter of oil
   paid in advance, the column's name gone from his book, his mouth, and the Hall alike;
   the coin real, the oil waiting on the shelf. An honest man cannot keep what he has
   been paid for.
2. The asking begins — Mara's stall, En's bench, doorsteps — and lands wrong: neighbors
   go quiet the way people go quiet about weather that might hear. In place of answers
   they hand him the rites — a thing told him two ways at once and left to argue; the
   parting blessing, two fingers to the heart: **“Come home carrying more people than
   yourself.”** (The volume teaches the farewell here; story 6 inherits it.)
3. Night: the private re-count. The sum done twice by lamplight and still short by one.
   He tells no one the number and puts the book away like a thing that might be
   listening. *(Private, deniable, unproven — never civic.)*
4. The Hall of Names by lamplight: the caretaker lets him search the Book of
   Relationships — names joined to names, who-remembers-with-whom instead of
   what-happened. Nothing joins to nothing: there is no line to a name no one holds. The
   town's oldest way of keeping has no column for his debt.
5. **Wonder beat (Law 7)** — the Festival of Last Things from the shop doorway: every
   window in Orin lit, borrowed things going home changed, children's chalk handprints on
   the blue hat — and above the roofs the mountain's cloud faintly holding light from
   inside, like a lamp behind cloth. No one comments. One breath of awe, then bread.
6. Rian fills and lights the unclaimed lamp in his own window — a bought winter of light,
   delivered nightly to whoever isn't there. Mara tells him gently to stop. He doesn't.
   The town's warmth cools by the width of a joke: *the daft lamp-man.*
7. Seasons close it: a second lit lamp appears down the street, unasked and unexplained,
   then a third. The name never comes. He will pay for the light the rest of his working
   life and never know whether anyone was warmed.

- **Mystery left standing:** whose lamp is dark — the name never comes; and why the
  asking frightened a town that could not say what it was afraid of.
- **Writer's Test in miniature:** *teaches* — keeping is paid by keepers; the rites are
  walls against an unseen weather · *mystery* — above · *changes a character* — Rian
  becomes the daft lamp-man for life · *changes civilization* — the lit-lamp-for-no-name
  custom quietly spreads to other shops (small Orin habit; the novellas never mention and
  never contradict it) · *ends larger* — rites older than their reasons; the mountain's
  cloud holding light.
- **Spoiler guard:** the failing-by-one stays private and deniable — Book 1 Ch 2 owns the
  public detonation of the Registration Count. Nothing beyond the Gate; no Sael, no
  unpinnable-woman figure; ritual never shown as technology; the erasure never named,
  capitalized, or even folk-worded; Elias an uncommented walk-on at most (a doorway, not
  a scene); no seventh bell (reserved); the reserved litany stays in its owners' mouths;
  and **no foreshadowing of Book 9** — the story must never suggest Rian's own name is at
  risk. No red bird, silver thread, or brass compass.
- **Shape / cast:** one sitting, ≤2 scene breaks; named cast ≤5 (Rian, Mara, En, the
  caretaker unnamed-or-Elias-in-a-doorway, one neighbor).

## Story 2 — The Slow Half

- **Cycle:** 2 — *The Scattered Light* · **Era:** ~CR 306 · Toll 7,520 (after the
  guild's everyday is established; before the beacon war and Carath) · **Target:**
  ~5,200 words.
- **POV:** **Lena** *(new — grepped clean)*, born aboard a guild-chartered link-ferry;
  second generation of a decades-long crossing. **Why her:** no novella ever rides the
  mesh's slow half. She embodies the cycle's locked truth from inside — the mesh as
  engineered technology paid for in lifetimes, Law 1 as biography rather than rule — and
  her want (home, arrival, being counted) keeps the teaching in the furniture where it
  belongs.
- **Logline:** A woman born aboard a link-ferry spends the last season of a
  two-generation crossing hauling the far end of a conversation to a colony — and on the
  day the link lights, reaches back for the home that paid for it.
- **Argument (Cycle 2):** Reach is existence — a world, a person, is real only while the
  conversation can still reach them — and what is kept whole in one place is not safe
  there.
- **The fragment as experienced (beat 2 of 6):** a place falls out of “now.” The lamp
  that means *the far end still answers* goes out between one shift and the next; mail
  comes back with no fault on the stamp; the guild's own papers disagree about a road
  they have kept for forty years. Nothing burned, nothing broke, nobody declared
  anything — and the dark between feels recently empty. No cause is ever found.

**Beat sheet**

1. The last season of a crossing older than she is: the ferry the only home Lena has
   had; in the hold, the far end of a conversation — relay, rivets, receipts — promised
   to the colony of **Bray** *(new world name — grepped clean)* before the captain was
   born. Her parents' names are in the manifest; their graves are the crossing itself.
2. **Wonder beat (Law 7), placed early on purpose** — the observation blister: the Road,
   the band across every sky, the known relays picked out like harbour lights — and her
   whole life's lesson landing on the reader at last: every instant link anyone has ever
   used was once this. A ship, a family, decades. The roads in the sky were all walked
   first.
3. Making the join: light-up as work — soundings, the join, freight-pace patience —
   while below, a world that petitioned for reach in its grandparents' generation waits
   without quite believing it.
4. The light-up: Bray enters the shared present. First traffic on a new board; festival
   at the depot; one more world answering for the almanac's next edition. She asks for
   the first private call: home.
5. **Home does not answer.** Mail returns with no fault on the stamp; the guild's papers
   disagree about a forty-year road; no ledger can say when it stopped or why. The
   silence has no author and no bill, and the years cannot be re-crossed.
6. The choice: the captain offers the return leg — decades back toward a silence with no
   author; the guild offers Bray's first station-agent post. To go is to spend a life
   crossing to nothing provable; to stay is to make the arrival mean what the departure
   cost.
7. She signs as Bray's first station-agent. Last page: the depot's standing lamp — lit,
   meaning the far end still answers — with her at the far end. Nothing comes back from
   home; the grace is that the place she carried is real. It answers.

- **Mystery left standing:** why home went silent — never a cause, never a date, never a
  bill; the dark stays recently empty forever.
- **Writer's Test in miniature:** *teaches* — the mesh is lifetimes made rivets; to be
  linked is to share a present · *mystery* — above · *changes a character* — home becomes
  the place she carried, not the place she left · *changes civilization* — a world enters
  the conversation; the count rises by one; a new station stands · *ends larger* — the
  Road seen whole.
- **Spoiler guard:** no character maps or charts silences (Book 6's device); never
  suggest the era's silences share one cause, and never connect any silence to the war;
  the patient mail absent entirely; the almanac count never decrements (that horror is
  Cycle 4's); no vanishing-name beat; no Seren; Farrow, Sowen, and Carath untouched;
  Corvane and Dresk at most distant powers; the mesh's temporal margin never used or
  explained. The farewell is **not** spent here (Cycle 2's one weighted spend is Book
  9's withholding).
- **Shape / cast:** one sitting, ≤2 scene breaks; named cast ≤4 (Lena, the captain, a
  Bray postmistress, one crewmate).

## Story 3 — The Soundings

- **Cycle:** 3 — *The Long Watch* · **Era:** ~CR 383 · Toll 7,597 (after the Leaving of
  Aulis and the Charter Run; before Tolm and the Reading) · **Target:** ~5,000 words.
- **POV:** **Orla** *(new — grepped clean)*, harbor-master of the small port world
  **Cove** *(new world name — grepped clean)* — a woman who has lived her whole working
  life inside an old mind's weather-warnings and bar-soundings. **Why her:** an ordinary
  person for whom an old mind is furniture, not wonder — the cycle's premise at kitchen
  scale. Her harbor's mind is the “old harbor-mind, nothing cosmic” of Book 15's margin,
  so a reader who reaches the novellas finds a throwaway line that already means
  something — Design Law 3's ideal.
- **Logline:** A harbor-master petitions the Listeners to mend the old mind that has kept
  her port's waters since before its records — and gets a witness instead of a mender, as
  the mind begins parcelling its keeping out to the boat-families, a reach of water
  apiece.
- **Argument (Cycle 3):** To hear a mind older than your species you must give up
  finishing it — what stays plural, in its own idiom, and incomplete stays true and stays
  safe; what is tidied into one whole is already being lost.
- **The fragment as experienced (beat 3 of 6):** the old things do not die whole. A
  caretaker whose love has outlived its reasons — felt as kindness before it is ever felt
  as strangeness — parcels everything it keeps into many hands, a piece to each family,
  no two pieces agreeing, and goes quiet as one. Nobody taught it. Nobody says *makers*,
  or *defense*, or *why*. Standing among neighbors holding fragments that contradict each
  other, with the boats living because of exactly that, you understand in your body: the
  old things were made by something that trusted many-ness the way drowning men trust
  air.

**Beat sheet**

1. Cove runs on the old mind's voice: bar-soundings at dawn, the storm-glass,
   gale-warnings that have buried no one in living memory. Her drowned husband's last
   berth is in its log. This season the voice goes vague at the edges — a warning
   repeated for a storm already passed.
2. She petitions the Listeners' daughter-house for a **mending**. What comes back is a
   Listener with a recorder, the gray, and the order's one instruction: **“Listen; do
   not tidy.”** Two wants collide at the harbor-office door: mend versus witness.
3. The parcelling begins — untaught, unannounced: a reach of water to each boat-family,
   spoken once, no two pieces agreeing. Orla fights it with a registrar's craft,
   correcting the families' soundings against the mind's own log; the Listener will not
   help her tidy.
4. The quarrel at the heart: *do something*, she says; the order's whole charter is the
   answer — witness, not keeping; incomplete by design. Her husband's mooring is going
   into a neighbor child's piece, under another family's name, wrong in one particular
   she cannot bear.
5. **Wonder beat (Law 7)** — the teaching night: before it lets go, the old mind speaks
   the whole water once, dusk to dawn — every storm-glass, bell, and buoy sounding its
   reach in order, a million tides read aloud — and the first sounding it gives is a
   depth for a water no chart of theirs has ever held, given to no family, spoken once
   into the dark.
6. It goes quiet as one. The log dies with it. The gray volume closes on testimony in
   the mind's own idiom — tide and glass, untranslated (Law 3 texture rule) — and goes
   to the daughter-house shelf as a **minor holding**.
7. Storm season proves the divided keeping: the boats live because no two soundings
   agree and the families argue them out on the quay. Orla takes up her family's reach —
   and the disagreement itself — as keeper. Her husband's mooring survives wrong, and
   held.

- **Mystery left standing:** why it divided itself — nobody taught it, nobody explains
  it; and the depth for a water no chart holds, spoken once, to no one.
- **Writer's Test in miniature:** *teaches* — old minds can be witnessed, not kept; the
  plural, incomplete keeping is the one that holds · *mystery* — above · *changes a
  character* — from wanting the mind whole and hers to keeper of one reach and of the
  disagreement · *changes civilization* — a port's keeping goes plural; a minor holding
  enters the order's shelves · *ends larger* — a million tides in one night; a water no
  chart has held.
- **Spoiler guard:** the self-division stays an unexplained instinct — no character
  generalizes it, connects it to makers or defenses, or renders the many-then-fewer
  shape as a quotable sentence (the audible sentence-match belongs to Book 15's Reading
  and nowhere earlier). The testimony stays a minor holding — never a sixth voice, never
  referred on (the dossier's chain is closed at five). “The old minds are stirring” at
  most one line of filed unease, never an answer. No Seren, no vanishing-name (Cycle 3's
  is spent in Book 12's appendix), no circle-crossed-line, no Charter Run winners named.
  The caretaker stays small, local, and loved.
- **Shape / cast:** one sitting, ≤2 scene breaks; named cast ≤5 (Orla, the Listener, two
  boat-family heads, the neighbor child).

## Story 4 — The Fair Copy

- **Cycle:** 4 — *The Collection* · **Era:** ~CR 448 · Toll 7,662 (after the count's
  fall is public fact; before the Whole Record's pilot) · **Target:** ~4,800 words.
- **POV:** **Hobb** *(new — grepped clean)*, an aging long-shelf keeper in the town of
  **Dray** *(new town name — grepped clean)*, whose house holds the town's one fine
  thing — a founding song-book generations of hands have miscopied. **Why him:** the
  era's factions all arrive at a small keeper's door as door-knocks — the order offering
  unmaking, the institute offering the count, frightened neighbors offering
  consolidation — so he carries “factions” at kitchen scale; and his want, to keep one
  thing perfectly, is the cycle's forbidden desire in miniature, rhyming toward Book 19
  without previewing it.
- **Logline:** A keeper of his town's one treasure perfects it — gathers every miscopied
  variant and restores the complete, authoritative edition — and the perfected whole is
  exactly what goes.
- **Argument (Cycle 4):** You cannot fight a failing keeper — hiding from the dark,
  firing back at it, and out-keeping it each hand it a cleaner meal; the only refusal
  that works is to be held imperfectly, in many hands, together — and even that is a
  nightly practice you pay for, never a wall you stand behind.
- **The fragment as experienced (beat 4 of 6):** the losses have taste, and the taste
  errs. What goes, goes by kind: the finished, the sole-kept, the agreed-upon taken
  clean; the argued-over passed over the way a hand passes over what won't lift. Then it
  takes the same thing twice; it hesitates over what it once took cleanly. Not the
  feeling of being hunted — the feeling of being **kept**, sorted by something old and
  careful that is no longer sure of its own shelves.

**Beat sheet**

1. The year of gathering: Hobb calls in the scattered part-copies — the schoolroom's,
   the mill-family's, the one that went out with a bride forty years ago — each wrong
   differently. His want, held with love: one perfect keeping, complete, in one trusted
   place, beyond miscopying forever.
2. **The detonation, early:** the fair copy is finished — authoritative, whole,
   beautiful, the variants retired into a crate with honors — and within the season it
   is taken clean. Cold pages, a blanked column, no face, no name, nothing to
   investigate. The rejects in the crate are passed over.
3. The era answers his door: the order's traveling keepers offer unmaking-as-mercy for
   what remains; the institute's surveyor asks only to count, and writes the register's
   terrible word — *attested*; a frightened neighbor urges consolidating everything
   somewhere central and fine. Each answer is reasonable. His was too.
4. The erring: it comes back for a page he re-made from memory; it hesitates over a
   thing it once took whole. Effects only — a cold document, a blanked ledger line — and
   no mouth names the sorting. *(No name-loss staged; things and pages only.)*
5. **Wonder beat (Law 7)** — the relay hut at night: the reach audible through a hilltop
   wire — eleven thousand worlds in one conversation — and the almanac's new count read
   into the town roll, smaller than last year. The size of the dark house, felt from one
   small lit room of it.
6. Hobb refuses all three answers and opens his shelf the wrong way: the dismissed
   variants go back out to the hands that miscopied them, and he begins teaching the
   treasure aloud — several versions, kept disagreeing on purpose, argued in the
   schoolroom the town's way.
7. The close: the town's one fine thing survives smaller and partly wrong, in many
   mouths; the crate stays half empty where the taken whole should be. His love was the
   exposure. He knows it, and teaches anyway.

- **Mystery left standing:** why the losses have taste at all — and where taken things
  go. *(Both stay open forever; the second is the series' kept mystery.)*
- **Writer's Test in miniature:** *teaches* — the perfected and sole-kept go clean; the
  argued-over survive; and the taste is failing · *mystery* — above · *changes a
  character* — sole author to plural teacher · *changes civilization* — Dray adopts
  argued keeping; one more town roll carries the falling count · *ends larger* — the
  reach audible from one hut, and smaller than last year.
- **Spoiler guard:** the story **enacts one lived instance** of the selection pattern;
  no character may name, plot, or generalize it — that arithmetic is Book 18 Chapter
  7's. No garbage-collection vocabulary, no “maintenance”/“cull”/“triage” from any
  mouth; the collector never seen, faced, or sourced. The phrase “the keeper's keeper”
  never appears (coined by grieving Meret at CR 460 — later than this story). No
  vanishing-name beat and no Charter Run reference (Cycle 4's instance is Book 19's).
  The Unwritten appear as public face only — no Breda, no sanctuary, no ascension. No
  Whole Record, no perfect-record project at any scale. The first decrement's founding
  vote (Book 18) untouched — a routine later decrement is the era-texture used here. No
  circle-crossed-line (deliberately absent Books 16–23). Orin never brushed.
- **Shape / cast:** one sitting, ≤2 scene breaks; named cast ≤5 (Hobb, a traveling
  keeper, the surveyor, the neighbor, a schoolroom child).

## Story 5 — The Ground of Things

- **Cycle:** 5 — *The First Silence* · **Era:** ~CR 475 · Toll 7,689 (the find and its
  law public across the reach; everything below the golden-age horizon claim-sealed and
  unknown) · **Target:** ~4,600 words.
- **POV:** **Sorrel** *(new — grepped clean)*, an orchardist of **Moss** under Loam —
  second survey generation, farming the ground above the strata's mapped extent. **Why
  her:** the only kind of person for whom the cycle's discovery is literally domestic —
  her fields are the lid, her family plot sits over an unmourned humanity, and the
  novellas never once look up from the shaft to see her. She carries the argument at
  kitchen scale, the series' preferred scale for apocalypse, and can pre-empt nothing:
  the books' whole drama happens below her feet and behind the claim's rope line.
- **Logline:** An orchardist of Moss fights for leave to dig one small grave — her
  father's — in ground the whole reach has just re-classified as the deep past's.
- **Argument (Cycle 5):** Recovering the past is spending it — the past can be read
  exactly once, so the care taken of the one reading, not the reading itself, is what
  everyone after you will have; recovery is never rescue.
- **The fragment as experienced (beat 5 of 6):** the toll already counted. In one season
  you learn the ground you buried your dead in is a grave twice over — under the
  orchard, a golden age so thoroughly taken that its absence was never even a mystery,
  its one reading being spent page by lamplit page at the bottom of a shaft you can see
  from the porch. And through all of it — notices, filings, schoolroom explanations — no
  one, anywhere, will say what took the first rise. The taker has no name in any
  document, and everyone has stopped expecting one; you live inside that missing word
  the way you live inside weather.

**Beat sheet**

1. Moss under Loam: the orchard, the burying-field with three generations in it, and —
   visible from the porch — the shaft head, the rope line, five lamplit strangers who
   write down everything. Her father is dying, and knows where he wants to lie.
2. **The detonation:** the stratum's mapped extent re-bounds, and the burying-field
   falls inside the claim. Digging one small grave now needs leave — the team's, the
   tribunal's — with the Claimants' Concord's counsel watching every precedent. “Own
   ground” has been withdrawn from under a family that never left it.
3. The petition at the camp table: heard with full dignity under the protocols — *walk
   seldom; walk witnessed; transcribe everything; spend nothing lightly* — and taken
   under advisement, because *everything read here is read for the last time*, and
   nobody yet knows what a grave-cut spends. Advocate Yent is courteous, and not wrong
   about the question.
4. The waiting season: her father's last weeks; the schoolroom explanation that explains

## Story 6 — The Keeper of a Thin Road

- **Cycle:** 6 — *The Memory of Stars* · **Era:** CR 491–492 · Toll 7,705–7,706 (inside
  the wearing-to-relief window) · **Target:** ~5,400 words FULL-ARC; ~4,900 THRESHOLD.
- **POV:** **Maud** *(new — grepped clean)*, keeper of a relay house on a dark segment
  between nowheres — forty years holding the guild's smallest office alone,
  rule-perfect, on a road nobody rides for pleasure. **Why her:** the whole era passes
  through her one room without her touching any book's plot — plural-addressed mail, a
  lodged carrier, then the wearing's traffic — and her private want, *to be relieved*,
  is the cycle's argument at human scale. She is the keeper's keeper's unknowing human
  rhyme, and the story never has to say so.
- **Logline:** After forty years alone in a relay house, a keeper files her annual
  request for relief just as the whole reach begins practicing at once — and relief
  comes, not as a successor but as the end of solitary keeping itself.
- **Argument (Cycle 6):** A civilization survives the loss of its keeper not by victory
  or replacement but by relief — the watch distributed into everyone's practiced,
  imperfect, daily keeping — because no single steward, however faithful, may decide
  what everyone forgets.
- **The fragment as experienced (beat 6 of 6):** the fever breaks. The takings that had
  begun to reach even careful things do not stop like a war stops — they ease, no
  battle, no bells, nothing defeated, nothing explained — and when word finally comes
  from a door your world never saw, it is not victory that arrives but a roster: your
  own name holding one hour of a watch you were told, all your life, that something
  older kept alone.

**Beat sheet**

1. The annual letter: Maud files, again, her request for relief — one successor, one
   person to take the keys — and the guild, stretched across a failing era, again
   cannot send one. Forty years rule-perfect; the board mostly dark; the sky mostly
   empty, and she knows exactly how empty.
2. The era passes through her one room: mail addressed plural; a carrier — the walking
   plural archives of Odri's practice — lodged in her spare bed when a richer house
   won't have them; then the watch-roll arrives for signature: a count that goes up,
   disagreeing tallies from every world, none matching, all binding.
3. The wearing reaches even the thin road: neighbors from the three farms ask for her
   segment's rolls, to copy by hand where people gather; names offered to strangers in
   the local manner of greeting (the Night of Strangers, reach-wide custom by now); the
   old song sung wrong on purpose in every doorway — the drift audible, no verse ever
   printed. She teaches them, grudgingly, exactly — and they do it worse than she does.
4. **Wonder beat (Law 7)** — the night the season crests: her dead-segment board, dark
   for decades and lit only with light-speed news arrived years old, fills with
   practiced traffic from end to end — the whole reach speaking at once, a cobweb in a
   cathedral hung suddenly with dew, seen from the one room that always knew how empty
   the sky usually is.
5. The roster: relief comes, and not as an heir — the office itself ends. A road kept
   by many local hands no longer needs a keeper; her keys go to a rota of neighbors; on
   the meeting-house wall her own name holds one hour of a watch she was told something
   older kept alone. She says the farewell to the first watch-pair out her door — two
   fingers to the heart — and means it the way Orin always meant it.
   **⟨THRESHOLD variant ends here — see the switch below.⟩**
6. *(FULL-ARC only)* The easing: the takings do not stop the way a war stops — they
   ease, the way a fever breaks. No battle, no bells, nothing defeated, nothing
   explained. Word arrives years old and second-hand, from a door her world never saw,
   and it is not victory that arrives. It is a roster.
7. *(FULL-ARC only)* Coda: her hour, kept weekly beside neighbors who do it worse than
   she did and together better than she could. She presides, courteously, over the end
   of the solitude that was her whole name — relieved exactly as the era relieves
   everything: carried, not replaced.

- **Mystery left standing:** what eased the takings — nothing on the page asks, and

## Accumulation — what the six do in order that none does alone

- **The scale walk.** One lamplit street (1) → the roads between worlds (2) → deep time
  in one room (3) → the administered map of a civilization (4) → the lid of the species'
  whole past, one camp's rim (5) → the species acting at once, folded back into one room
  (6). The reader who finishes has walked the entire aperture of the series without
  meeting one of its plots.
- **The escalation.** The six fragments are one sentence spoken slowly: a hole with
  edges → a place out of “now” → old things dividing themselves → a taste that errs →
  the toll already counted underfoot → the fever breaking. No story names it; in order,
  the reader assembles the thing that is never named — and arrives at the novellas
  knowing the weather without knowing one secret.
- **The argument braid.** Each cycle's argument corrects the one before: keeping costs a
  keeper (1); what is whole in one place is not safe (2); don't tidy what is older than
  you (3); don't perfect what you love (4); recovery is expenditure (5); relief is
  distribution (6). Read in order they are one argument — the series' thesis: preserving
  memory is never morally neutral, and the only keeping that survives is plural,
  imperfect, and shared.
- **The keeper's row.** Six small offices — ledger, manifest, log, shelf, field, board.
  The frame compiles the toll out of exactly the records such people keep; by story 6
  the reader understands the volume in their hands is itself one of those ledgers, and
  the compiler one more keeper. (Who — never answered.)
- **Motif seeding, rationed.** The farewell is *taught* in story 1 and *inherited* in
  story 6 — two spends, none between. The almanac's count is born rising in 2, read
  falling in 4, and answered by the watch-roll's rising count in 6. The rites of story 1
  are revealed as the species' armor by story 6 without one sentence of explanation.
  Counting-as-dread is used once (story 1, private) and retired.
- **What none does alone.** Any one story is a good story about a keeper paying for a
  keeping. Only in order do they become the universe's own six-beat sentence — the whole
  toll, counted once, from a street to a species.

## Spoiler posture

- **FULL-ARC (the default).** All six stories complete, including story 6's final
  movement (beats 6–7: the easing and the coda) and the frame's afterword. The more
  powerful book, the real onboarding document for a collaborator, and thematically
  coherent with the series' thesis: inheriting what happened does not diminish the
  carrying of it. Knowing the ending is not explaining the mystery — the resolution is
  *felt from outside*, and nothing on the intentionally-undefined list is answered.
- **THRESHOLD (documented variant — a switch on story 6 alone).** Stories 1–5 identical
  to the word. Story 6 ends at beat 5 plus a short threshold coda: the roster's first
  night — the board still lit with practiced traffic, word from the wider relief not yet
  arrived, her hour signed and kept while the season holds. Two frame swaps travel with
  it: story 6's headnote becomes “Entered from a relay board on a thin road, Toll 7,705.
  The rest of the count is kept elsewhere.” and the afterword is replaced by a single
  line in the same register. Nothing else in the volume changes — one story's final
  movement, one headnote, one afterword; swap them and the volume redesigns itself not
  at all. The threshold coda still lands story 6's argument (the office dissolves into
  the roster); only the series' outcome is withheld.
