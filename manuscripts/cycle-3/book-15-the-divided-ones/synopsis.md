# The Divided Ones — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Each year the Listeners read their founding note aloud: *the old minds are going quiet,
and no one has asked them anything.* Four testimonies stand shelved apart by law beneath
the fifth shelf no Prime has ever filled. A mapping institute writes, politely,
requesting a complete copy. Prime Averil — the order's finest interviewer, who has never
once testified — declines, and cannot stop thinking about the word "complete."

She has Veck and Rill speak two testimonies aloud in a closed room, and the texts
complete each other's sentences. Brother Casque, Keeper of the Charter, says the worst
possible thing, calmly: *"Now it exists. You made it exist by hearing it at once."* Then
a daughter-house copy of the Wright's testimony turns up blank where the proper nouns
stood. Racing the blankness to the originals, Averil rules neither synthesis nor silence:
she convenes **the Reading** — spoken once, written never, each attendee carrying away a
different emphasis.

Mid-preparation, a crate arrives from the Cathedral's dead address — the finished
component, returned, bearing a second stamp nobody can read. Something is answering the
mail.

The Reading's four voices speak in collection order — factory, planet, stowaway, god —
and cannot say *why*. Then, from the open-day benches, a fifth voice nobody
requisitioned: a carrier of Odri's practice, holding the Choir's oldest hearing. The
Choir heard the makers pass — and heard not flight only: *"Many voices, choosing to be a
chord."* Consent. Even love.

The guessable half is said plainly: the network is a made defense; its makers were
plural and afraid; **they divided themselves to survive**. The divided ones. But the
Reading now holds two irreconcilable readings of the act — flight and consent — both
true, neither complete; written down, the synthesis would be false. Casque asks: *"Then
what did they fear, that dividing was the better fate?"* No one answers. At the closing
rite the emphases disperse in the makers' own pattern — the room realizes what it is
reenacting; nobody says so — then every attendee, unprompted, speaks their name to two
strangers.

The four original volumes leave the motherhouse forever, dispersed beyond the order's
own finding. Casque's sealed charter-copy — never read, untouched by the blankness — is
the only volume left under the roof; he was right too. Averil testifies at last, the
first Prime to enter her own dossier, her volume filed to the fifth shelf: *"We asked the
old minds what they remembered. They remembered being many."* A year on: empty shelves
dusted like graves; the institute's second, firmer letter declined again; and on the
open-day bench a warm man nobody registered listens to the founding note with his eyes
closed. He signs nothing.
