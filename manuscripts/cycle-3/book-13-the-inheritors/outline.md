# The Inheritors — Outline

**Memory of Stars — Novella #13** · **Target length:** ~31,500 words · **Structure:**
**No prologue — cold open.** 10 chapters in four movements (The Rite · The Run · The
Pull · The Price) + Epilogue — chosen: romps open cold in this series (Book 9 set the
custom), and an adventure wants its bill late.

- **Facet:** memory as **inherited memory**.
- **Central question:** *When your ancestors live in you, which life is yours?*
- **Register — the cycle's romp (FIRM, confirmed at this charter):** shipboard
  adventure, warm and quick — a crew that is its own ancestry, bickering across
  generations inside one nineteen-year-old's head. The cycle's one fast book among
  four slow old minds. The cost lands in the fourth movement and stays paid.
- **The book's job:** carry the cycle's midpoint fragment in the liveliest possible
  vessel: among the ship's inherited captains is one memory that predates the ship —
  a builder's — and the book smuggles the series' oldest grief inside its brightest
  caper.
- **POV:** Nell — nineteen, newest of the Marigold's line. Her deflection: she jokes
  with the dead — has since she was six, when the dead first started answering.
- **The spine in one line:** a junk-run family ship must win the reach's most famous
  contract race to keep its own inherited memories out of a creditor's vault — while
  the newest captain carries a thirty-second memory that is older than the ship,
  doesn't belong to any captain, and keeps pulling the course toward a dim star
  nobody's charts want to name.

## The threads
- **New truth:** whole-line inheritance exists — a ship whose every captain receives
  all prior captains, memory-to-memory (the Bearer and keeper institutions, made a
  family). And memory has *legal standing*: inheritances are attachable assets; the
  dead can be collateral.
- **The witness (the cycle's rule, kept slant):** this book's nonhuman witness rides
  *inside* the human listener — the **32nd memory**: older than the hull, no
  captain's, afraid, and *of dividing* — being many, choosing fewer. It does not
  match the Wright's feeling or the Observer's stratum in any particular. It matches
  them entirely in shape.
- **The caper:** the Charter Run — the reach's oldest contract race (first hull home
  with the season's bonded freight holds its charter another generation). The
  Marigold's line is mortgaged to the vault-house of **Brace & Sons**; lose the Run,
  and the creditor attaches the line's inheritance — thirty-one captains, sold as an
  asset lot.
- **The pull:** the 32nd keeps surfacing at the worst moments, hauling Nell's course
  sense toward a dim star off every chart — fear and yearning in one vector. (→ the
  god's world, Book 14; never named here.)
- **Chapter-Two Rule:** chapter 2 is the rite going beautifully, then wrong:
  thirty-one captains arrive on schedule — and then something else does.

## Characters in play
- **Nell** — newest inheritor. Jokes with the dead; the book teaches her which
  silences to keep.
- **The captains (in Nell)** — thirty-one voices of consult, led by **Old Bax**
  (loudest, wrongest, most beloved) and **Prue** (first of the line, quietest, right
  twice). Comedy engine and chorus — never possession; always inheritance (she
  outranks them all, which infuriates Bax).
- **The 32nd** — the stowaway memory. Not a captain. Not human. Afraid. The witness.
- **Pol** — ship's engineer, Nell's uncle by affection; alive, exasperated,
  the one who says "your gran said that too" at exactly the wrong moments.
- **Dree** — cousin and pilot; flies like a rumor; wants the Run for glory, learns
  what it's actually for.
- **Marsh, of Brace & Sons** — the creditor's rider aboard (contract clause): polite,
  precise, keeps the lot-inventory of the dead in a locked case. Decent, lawful, the
  arithmetic again — and even he doesn't like the vault.

## The Writer's Test (must pass)
- [ ] New truth — whole-line inheritance; memory as attachable legal asset; a living
      crew can plural-hold a mind-fragment. *(Ch 2, 3, 9.)*
- [ ] A mystery left — what the 32nd fled; what it yearns toward; how it got into
      the line's inheritance at all. *(Ch 6, 10.)*
- [ ] A character permanently changed — Nell: from joking with the dead to chairing
      them; from inheritor to the first captain who *gave* memory back. *(Ch 7, 9.)*
- [ ] Civilization changed — the plural-attachment precedent (what is divided cannot
      be seized) enters salvage law; the Run's charter now recognizes it. *(Ch 9–10.)*
- [ ] Larger universe — a memory older than the ship rides a junk-line's rite; the
      dim star has a shepherd. *(Ch 2, 10.)*

## Laws & mystery discipline
- **Romp discipline:** the wit never mocks the dead or the debt; the Selt rule
  applies (nobody's grief is comic relief); the cost is real and stays paid — the
  32nd's sequence does not come back.
- **Law 3:** the 32nd is not a ghost and not a person-in-miniature; it renders as
  vector, fear, and craft — other, even inside a human head.
- **Canon-spend guard:** the 32nd's origin, makers, and destination stay unnamed;
  "of dividing" is a feeling, not an explanation. The fragment accumulates; Book 15
  reads it.
- **Drafting guards (the rhyme, and the fragments):** the 32nd's shape renders as
  vector, fear, and craft — never a quotable sentence matching Books 11–12; the
  audible match belongs to Book 15's Reading. And Ch 10's "all five fragments point
  one direction" is a *per-fragment* property, like iron filings — the fragments
  never re-agree into narrative; the sequence stays dead.
- **Plural custody, stress-tested (the series' own rule):** division *saves and
  costs* — the sequence dies on the page. The armor is never free here.
- **No chosen one:** Nell is newest because the calendar says so; the 32nd chose the
  *line* (nine generations of practiced inheritance), not the girl.
