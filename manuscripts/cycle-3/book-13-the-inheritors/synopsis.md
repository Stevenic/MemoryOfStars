# The Inheritors — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The family junk-ship *Marigold* works a debris field with a creditor's rider aboard —
Marsh of Brace & Sons, polite, precise, his lot-inventory of the dead in a locked case.
Then the old captain dies planetside: the rite is due, the Charter Run is in forty days,
and a mourning line races anyway. The rite goes beautifully — thirty-one
captains walking into nineteen-year-old Nell, oldest first — and then a thirty-second
arrives: older than the hull, no name, no rank, *afraid* — a memory of being many and
choosing fewer. The consult goes silent for the first time in nine generations. Nell
tells no one. Badly.

Lose the Run and Brace & Sons attaches the line's inheritance — thirty-one captains sold
as an asset lot; Marsh's one lawyerly kindness states the trap nobody hears click:
*inheritances vest under existing liens.* Across the legs Nell learns to chair her
ancestors — Old Bax loudest and wrongest, Prue quietest and right twice — while the
**32nd** keeps surfacing, hauling her course sense toward a dim star off every chart. Not
sabotage; yearning.

Shown at last to the full consult, the stowaway gets the verdict in one line:
*it isn't cargo, it's crew.* It is running from something and toward something, and both
directions are old. On the dead third leg — soundings stale, Dagny's Roan turning back
with a salute — the 32nd offers what no chart holds: live fear-craft. Prue names it:
*"It isn't remembering the road. It's remembering how to be afraid of the right things."*
Nell gives it the conn.

First hull home — and the bill in the same hour: the win voids the attachment, but the
crossing-craft they flew is now the reach's most valuable navigational asset, vested
under the lien, and its only holder is the 32nd. Legally theirs. The line loses the
decent defense, then answers from older salvage law: what is plural cannot be attached.
In the rite reversed, Nell divides the 32nd among five living keepers — and the loss is
on the page: the memory's sequence dies; the fear, the yearning, the choosing survive as
five true pieces that no longer agree. Nell grieves a passenger no one else ever fully
met.

The charter renews; Marsh departs with an empty case and, for the first time, an opinion.
At rest, all five fragments point one direction — homeward, but not *their* home: *"Where
the shepherd stands."* In the line's book, beneath Nell's page, sits a second entry,
unsigned: five short lines in five crew hands, one shared page for a passenger. When a
motherhouse letter finds them at dock, the dead vote unanimous for once, and the
dossier's third testimony arrives as a family's ledger page — given, not taken.
