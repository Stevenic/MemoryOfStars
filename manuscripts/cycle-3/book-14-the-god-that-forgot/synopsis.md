# The God That Forgot — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Tolm at dawn: the Shepherd's weather laid over the valleys like a made bed, and Tavel —
the Shepherd's Voice, forty years of performed certainty — noting, at matins, lapse three
hundred and one in the ledger that is the most faithful and most heretical document on
Tolm.

At the harvest oracle, the Shepherd stops — loses an antiphon it has spoken for a
thousand years — and asks through the Voice, in front of everyone: *"What comes next?"*
Tavel answers from memory; only he and the god know. Privately, it asks him again: it is
losing something it cannot name. Sister Rill of the Listeners arrives on the dossier's
chain — five fragments pointing *where the shepherd stands* — and Tavel permits the
heresy of deposing a god, in secret: better a Listener than the crowds to come.

The interview answers in weather and harvest-signs; Rill must learn to transcribe a sky.
The Shepherd *wants* to answer. It remembers the flock utterly, and nothing before it.
The purpose surfaces broken: it was set here — it did not choose Tolm — and it was set to
**protect against** — and the sentence ends. Its tending is the purpose's residue:
protect, worn down to tend.

Once, for one hour, the Shepherd remembers everything — the **Clearing**, the flock will
coin it, like weather. It spends the hour on the year's whole tending, perfectly — then
begins the sentence, sees what it would put into two mortal heads, and chooses not to
finish: *"You are not made to hold this singly."* It forgets again, knows it has, and
thanks them for witnessing.

Tavel ends the performance and tells Willa, then the congregation, plainly: our god is
old, and made, and failing, and it loves us past its own reasons. Petch, the porter the
truth vindicates, is the one it un-homes; others follow him out. Willa asks the saving
question: *"Then who minds the weather while it rests?"* The congregation learns the
liturgy's working half — the god's own care, plural-held by a thousand ordinary keepers —
and the Shepherd steadies: not restored, accompanied.

Tavel's ledger becomes a public maintenance log; he retires the Voice for the smaller
title of sexton — gladly, he says, and the book lets the reader doubt it. The god's
blessing is a small rain on his garden only; Petch does not come back for it. A year on,
the whole valley sings the office imperfectly, in plural. In the temple's oldest stone
Rill finds a circle crossed by a single line, and beneath the Clearing's words writes her
own inference, signed as inference: *if no one may hold it singly, then what we have
gathered must be held together.*
