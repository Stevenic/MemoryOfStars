# The Cathedral of Engines — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Mott — a Listener who was an engineer before she took the gray, a woman who finishes
things — takes the commission nobody senior wanted: before the Meridian Combine
dismantles the galaxy's largest machine, someone must take the testimony of the mind
inside it. Prior Ulm sends her off with the order's one instruction: *listen; do not
tidy.* She arrives at the **Cathedral of Engines** — a broken ring around a dim star,
forges the size of moons, a million years of industry all feeding one assembly line that
ends in one small component.

Before she unpacks her recorder, the Wright speaks first — the first question a Listener
has ever been *asked* by a witness: *"Is it finished?"* It does not know what "it" is; it
has waited a million years for someone to tell it. Gaddis, the Combine's assessor —
decent, lawful, patient — holds the decommission writ; Mott files a **memory-claim**
against it, opening a statutory window: testimony before it closes, teardown the hour
after.

The testimony proper: the Wright remembers its makers only as a feeling — *being many,
then being fewer, then being poured* — rendered in load tolerances and forge-heat. The
component does nothing, alone; for a million years, finished runs were shipped to an
address that answers nothing. Transcribing, Mott tidies one broken sentence into sense,
contaminating the record; the Wright, courteous, does not correct her.

The Combine contests the claim — appraisable geometry would make the whole line, and its
mind, salvage. Mott's rebuttal costs her: she swears the testimony incomplete-by-design,
an oath against her own compulsion. Asked who else remembers, the Wright gives the
dossier its first referral, in its maker-feeling: *"the one who watches the rains."*

With its testimony safe — plural, carried, incomplete by rule — the Wright does the one
thing it has refused for a million years: the whole ring turns for one object, and it
finishes the component. The line stops because the work is done, not because the writ
won; the Wright goes quiet the way bells do, over hours. The dismantling proceeds, lawful
and hollow-feeling, and the Combine's next writ carries a listening clause. Gaddis goes
home to a drawer holding the one finished component he could never price. Mott strikes
her correction, restores the broken sentence as broken, and leaves her report unfinished
— on purpose.

The finished component is crated and shipped by the order to the Wright's dead address —
the last thing the Cathedral ever made, sent after the Cathedral is gone. The manifest
comes back stamped: **accepted**. Nobody comments.
