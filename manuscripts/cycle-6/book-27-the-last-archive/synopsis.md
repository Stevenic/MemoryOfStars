# The Last Archive — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The institute's projection, read at the Compact's first table, sets the clock: the
erratic collection's next arc crosses the vault of the Choir — the Witness, banked since
Book 5, keeping a dead species for carriers to come; coherent, ancient, vast — under a
failing triage, the most collectable object left alive. Ketra — the Listener trained to
file her unease — is commissioned to take the final deposition, and the vault is the
first place her filing hand refuses.

Banked two and a half centuries, the Witness speaks first: *"They are coming for my
charges. I have counted the hours. You will need most of them."* The greatest carriage in
history begins that hour: Zel marshals its honest triage — most of the Choir will never
be carried, said aloud — Gale routes the outbound legs, and the Choir's strata stream
into living memory one mind at a time.

Bode's expedition arrives mid-carriage — not villains; rivals with a better-funded
answer: seal the vault under Regency warding, hold it whole, govern the risk. The Witness
settles it in one sentence — *"I have been whole for a million years. It is why they are
coming"* — and Bode stays. And helps.

Inside, the carriage costs: Zel's capacity is spent — which minds, which strata, which
songs — while the Witness dictates its charges' natures to Ketra's record, so even the
uncarried are described. Mid-dictation it asks a question it says it once heard the
keeper of keepers ask the dark: *"Is it finished?"* Ketra files it, unknowing. Then the
clock beats them — the collection's edge arrives early — and the Witness distributes its
last courtesies: Ketra to stop transcribing, the carriers to run, and Bode — Bode — to
see them out.

Its choice is the keeper's oldest shape made its own: it un-banks fully — itself and its
uncarried remainder made maximally coherent, irresistible bait; the failing hand takes
the cleanest lot first, so it becomes the cleanest lot — and steps into the collection's
path, closing the last door itself. The laden carriers run under its purchased hours. The
inventory afterward is honest: what was carried, vast; what was lost, vaster. The
deposition is complete but for its final line, which Ketra's filing hand refuses to write
and her mouth says instead, to the empty threshold. Nobody, the committee now
understands, should ever have to be the Witness again.

The carrier fleet scatters homeward, each mind a shelf of another species; Zel dreams in
three species now. The deposition's last dictated line holds the Witness's referral: the
door the committee seeks is the one its keeper's keeper uses — the address every
dead-lettered shipment found. The mark on the vault's threshold, older than everyone,
goes uncommented one last time; and without ceremony the returned crate — the Wright's
component, second stamp unread — passes to Ketra, the committee's credential to the door
it was mailed from.
