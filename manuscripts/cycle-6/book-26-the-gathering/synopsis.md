# The Gathering — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Gale — a Wayfinders' Guild route-holder who has never once failed a delivery — is
mid-route with sealed mail and no idea she is carrying the series' last act: the summons
to a Convening, addressed plural, to five traditions and every surviving institution.
Unsealed at a relay-house before witnesses, the reason lands: a relay-held roll — plural,
copied, worn exactly as the rules require — has been **collected entire**. The failing
keeper's triage has gone erratic; the armor the reach built its survival on is failing
with it. And one detail is planted cold: the roll's wearing was verified only *by record*
— and nobody knows whether this failure was the first, or only the first noticed. Gale
reads it twice and rides faster.

The roads become a reunion. Zel, a carrier of Odri's practice who dreams in two species,
boards first — after a relay-house refuses her lodging, because carriers are walking
plural archives and fear does arithmetic fast; Gale's route-craft solves what courtesy
can't. Three stops thread the lit map — Farrow's holders, a Tolm sexton, a Meret warden —
the reach's surviving customs shown working, the manifest growing at every door.

Mid-route waits the rival gathering: **the Regency**, Chancellor Bode's coalition, decent
and terrified, answering the same failure with the old good idea at final scale — *the
watch must have a warden — one, accountable, ours.* Bode offers Gale the Regency's courier
contract, every road, forever, one employer; she declines on instinct and spends three
chapters understanding why. Ketra the Listener and Idra the Cartographer board and argue
what the Convening is even for; Alba the Restorer boards last, as news catches up of a
second plural-held collection — on a world Gale's own route serves. Both gatherings
become races.

Valen, the Lens's old city, is lit for the first Convening of its name: five traditions,
forty worlds' delegates, the elders one scene each — and the **watch-roll** opened, the
count that goes up, read aloud in disagreeing tallies from every world, none matching,
all binding. Across the same city the Regency convenes larger. Envoys cross; nothing
resolves; the **Compact** is signed anyway — smaller, plural, stubborn.

Its first act is not war but a race to the same door: the dead address that once accepted
a shipment, the threshold everything points to. Gale's last delivery is the watch-roll
itself, entrusted to the committee she assembled — her manifest, for the first time in
her life, complete. In the epilogue, five departures leave Valen in five directions; and
behind them, as the Convening's banner comes down, the hall door's old wood gives up a
mark — a circle crossed by a single line, worn shallow by hands nobody counted. It was
always there.
