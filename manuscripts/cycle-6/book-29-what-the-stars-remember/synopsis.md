# What the Stars Remember — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The committee rides out of Orin's wreckage with the proof and the town's loaf, the
watch-roll's count racing the failure curve. At the Compact's table the correlation
becomes policy: worn holds, assumed fails — therefore *wear everything* — while the
Regency counter-broadcasts that its warden is ready today.

The **great wearing** is designed in one night, every armor of the series scheduled into
one season, world by world — and the spark is Orin: the blank column filled at last,
Gate-silent and undeterred, the first place volunteering first. Worlds take the season up
in their own idioms, and the failure curve slows where the wearing is thick. Proof, live.

The door is hunted from the Witness's referral, the Wright's manifest, and the letters'
margin — the crossing paid for on the page. At the midpoint, the series' gentlest scene:
Zel — the carrier who dreams in three species and introduces herself plurally, *"Zel, and
those I carry"* — asks Seren at last, and he answers by showing what he can do: nothing,
except witness. A thread of the poured, warm and powerless, a million years of watching
other people keep each other. He cannot open the door. He can witness them in — and for
the first time the watcher weeps, because someone finally asked him to be useful in the
only way he is.

The season crests as the committee crosses: the drifted song sung everywhere at once, ten
thousand variants, none matching, all recognizable — the drift itself the proof that
plural custody works; nothing that various can be lifted whole. The curve bends.

At the threshold the Regency waits: Bode, his fleet in warding formation, his case at its
honest strongest — *the keeper dies now; your cure takes a season; my warden takes an
hour* — and neither argument defeats the other; both clocks are real. First, the consent
the practice owes: Zel convenes her carried — the Choir's minds, taken by this thing's
kin — and asks whether they will enter the keeper's house; their answer, a chord, not a
vote, costs her a night and buys the right to knock. The dead address is small, worn,
ordinary, its mark drawn by the first hands. Seren witnesses them in — attendance, never
authorization; the door still chooses. It answers neither faction's credentials, only
*"Zel, and those I carry."* Plurality is the key; the Regency's warden, singular by
design, cannot enter.

Bode gives the order his whole coalition has argued against: the warding fleet stands
down, formation by formation — the series' last armada unmaking itself in silence while
its chancellor watches the committee cross a threshold he cannot. The last approach
begins, the keeper's presence rising like weather in a house. Behind them the watch-roll's
tallies are read aloud on forty worlds — disagreeing, binding, rising — as the season
holds the reach together under one long note of the drifted song, waiting for word from a
door it cannot see.
