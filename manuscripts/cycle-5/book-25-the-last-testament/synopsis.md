# The Last Testament — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

The store is empty, the ledger all strike-throughs, fourteen years of walks alive only
above ground. Standing where the past used to be, Isla says: *"Now we owe it interest."*
The **testament** is commissioned by nobody and all of them. The ruling: it must be
*testimony*, not compilation — one coherent whole would be collectable by anything — so
each survivor writes as *I*, and the frame falls to Amel, the scribe who has never once
written that word. Amel accepts the assignment the way people accept surgery. And the
clock: on lapse day the Ash claim's shield over the walk-products ends and Yent's
counter-claim matures — publish plural before lapse, or lose the past a second time, to
custody.

The testimonies are drafted — Kova's twelve thresholds at length, the rest received in
Amel's hand — and the scribe discovers the difference: walks were the past speaking;
this is the *team*, and there is no notation for colleagues weeping at their own pages.
The deliberate holes are cut: the mercy-horror plural-held, the curator's fingerprints
unwritten, the mark drawn-only. Ondine, back with the decent version of custody, is
shown the holes and converts at price — resigning the provostship to withdraw her
institution's bid; her institution joins the counter-claim instead. The clock tightens.

The final section — *how to end it* — has nothing to hold: the store never held it; the
builders poured mid-question. Hask refuses the despair-reading — *an unanswered
question, held properly, is an instruction to keep holding.* Blocked, Amel returns to
the first deep-walk transcript and reads its first page's rendered words aloud, hearing
them at last as what they are. **The instruction survived as the farewell**: not in the
strata but in doorways, in every mouth, longer than any record runs — a homecoming, not
a twist.

The frame is written at last — the scribe's *I* spent like a stratum, once, entirely —
and the blank section, titled honestly *How to End It*, holds one line: the farewell,
attested plainly as the first defense's teaching — how to keep, not how to end. The
phrase crossed total erasure by being plural, oral, coherent nowhere: the instruction
survived by obeying itself. The testament publishes hours before the lapse — five
documents printed apart, holes and all, on the long shelf's pattern — and Yent arrives
to seize what is now everywhere and owned by no one. Isla lets the Ash claim die — the
first of her line in five centuries to open the hand — and the team unbinds: five people
going home carrying each other.

A year on, the testament is read everywhere, argued everywhere, incomplete everywhere —
working; the mark on schoolhouse walls, its plain reading beneath. On the last page of
the team's ledger, in Amel's hand, first person, one line closes the cycle's account and
opens the next: *We spent the past. What we owe now is the ending.*
