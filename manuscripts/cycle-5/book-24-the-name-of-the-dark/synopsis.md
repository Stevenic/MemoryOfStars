# The Name of the Dark — Synopsis

**Spoiler-complete.** Keep in sync with the [series bible](../../../series-bible/) and the
[canon ledger](canon-ledger.md).

Two strata left, both dark-reading. Hask — the Restorers' skeptic, whose numbered doubts
are the litany the team will not walk without — ends objections one through nine where
they always end: *and yet we will walk it.* The first dark walk opens the builders'
instrument hall: instruments the size of weather, data accumulating on the dying of
meaning — and, worn shallow on the threshold by the era's own hands, a circle
crossed by a single line. Nobody comments. The reframe turns the cycle over: *they were
investigating too.*

Hask tests inside the recording, replicating the investigators' own controls — two
skeptics' methods shaking hands across a million years. In a drift-check, the Hall's
account of the First Pilgrimage is read aloud — and where the old climber's name should
stand, the line is blank, matching in every copy. No character notices; none of them ever
knew it. (The reader knows: Elias.)

The investigation's substance: the dying mapped by the first minds — meaning thinning at
the edges first, the selection rule derived for the first time, their dread procedural.
Their conclusion is not a word. It is **the mark**: the circle (the whole, the
singly-kept) crossed by a single line (the cut that both takes and saves) — warning and
instruction in one figure, drawn because words are collectable and shapes survive.
Twenty-four books of trail resolve in one held silence. Amel does not transcribe. Amel
*copies*. What causes the dying stays unnamed: the mark is its shape, not its source.

Underneath, the mercy-horror surfaces: the dying may be structural — a finite medium
holding infinite meaning — and the cull, gently run, may be the mercy form of something
worse. His litany fails for the first time: the finding stands through all nine
objections. Isla's steward-ruling: **the shape travels; the horror is held** — plural,
unwritten, in the team. Hask dissents, and is asked to hold the horror anyway, because a
doubter's custody is the safest kind.

The last stratum holds the investigators' own ending: they reach the mercy-possibility,
refuse to write it, pour it into the mark, and go to the pouring. Hask, walking it,
does what the first skeptics did — carries what cannot safely be said — and the store's
final page burns behind him. Yent's appeal matures into an injunction against
publication; the team publishes the mark and its plain reading plural and everywhere at
once — schoolhouses, chapter-houses, the long shelf — before the order can be served.
Yent files his own defeat with something close to admiration.

At the empty cold face Hask says the litany aloud — objections one through nine — and
then, for the first time with witnesses, the unnumbered tenth: *some certainties are to
be carried, not resolved.* Amel does not transcribe it; that is the point. The testament
lacks one book now.
