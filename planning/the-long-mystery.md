## The meta-mystery: the Erasure

Already seeded (unnamed) in Book 1. Sael:
> *"We could not stop the erasure. We could only make its work incomplete."*
> *"We think it follows meaning."* · *"I divided the connections."*

**The Erasure** is an active force that *unmakes memory* — not decay, not ordinary
forgetting. It *takes coherent, agreed-upon, ownable truth*: memory dies where meaning
concentrates. The ancient civilization built the entire network — the Gate, the Hall, the
Resonance Lens, the anchors, the role of **Amarin** — as a **defense**. Their armor was
**plurality itself**: distribute memory into contradiction so no single whole truth exists
for the Erasure to consume. In this universe, *contradiction is armor.*

This is the core question weaponized:
> **What must a civilization remember if it hopes to survive?** — because something is
> trying to make sure it doesn't.

**Kept unresolvable by design** (honors the intentionally-undefined list): we learn the
Erasure's *effect*, never its *nature*. Force? Rival civilization? The "gods"? Physics? A
structural inevitability? That is the 30-book engine, answered only (and only partly) in
Cycle 6.

### The Erasure as garbage collection *(the mechanism — Tier-2 reveal, ≈ Cycle 4)*

If memories are **recorded simulations stored in the mesh**, and storage is **finite**, then
the Erasure is (at least partly) the network's own **garbage collector.** There is **no
monster** — the thing built to preserve memory is now, starved for space over millions of
years, *culling* it. That is more tragic and more unwinnable than any enemy: you can't
defeat maintenance; you can only make yourself un-collectable.

**Two loss mechanisms (the detective pattern):**
- **Hardware failure** — random, dumb decay (ancient tech). *Uncorrelated* loss.
- **Garbage collection** — *selective*: reclaims the **rarely-accessed** and the **cleanly
  collectable.** Loss correlates with **obscurity** — the forgotten vanish, the remembered
  persist. Characters *discover* the Erasure's nature by noticing the loss isn't random.

**Reconciles all existing canon:**
- *"It follows meaning"* (Sael) = the GC targets **coherent, well-referenced, singular
  wholes** — the cleanest things to collect. **A single authoritative record is the most
  collectable object possible** → Book 2 / Cycle 4's "perfect record" danger now has a
  mechanism: certainty is suicide.
- **Two survival strategies, both already canon:**
  1. **Be accessed** — remembered, loved, referenced. Orin's whole remembrance culture, the
     Hall, *"No life is remembered alone"* = keeping reference counts up = life-support for the dead.
  2. **Be un-collectable** — fragment yourself into contradiction so no single unit can be
     cleanly reclaimed. Sael "divided the connections"; Amarin; *contradiction is armor*.
- **To be forgotten = to be deleted** — existentially, retroactively "never were." The
  ultimate death (the empty chair; "grief without names"; Sael erasing/hiding herself).

**The doom clock:** the mesh fills over deep time → GC grows **more aggressive** → the past
is culled faster → the "Erasure" *accelerates.* This IS Cycle 4's "the defense is failing."

**The resolution shape (Cycle 6):** you can't beat GC. You survive by becoming
**un-collectable together** — a web of mutual reference so dense nothing drops to zero and
nothing can be cleanly collected. Universal, distributed, plural memory across generations =
the core question **answered as a mechanism**: *"civilization is a conversation across
generations" / "no life is remembered alone."*

**Stays undefined (deepen, don't answer):** *why* storage is finite (built that way, or
damaged?) · **what happens to collected memories** — deleted forever, or moved to **cold
storage / compressed / merged** (a "recycle bin" the series can mine: *The Last Witness*'s
held minds; the empty chair's "memories after their paths were severed") · whether the
**Custodian** (below) is truly *senile* or coldly *right* · whether it is even conscious.

### The builders' fate & the Custodian *(resolves D1)*

**The builders didn't die or leave. They dissolved into the substrate they built** — half by
deliberate defense (become un-collectable = fragment past recognition = *become
unrememberable*), half by their own garbage collector. They **are** the mesh, the anchors,
the orphan knowledge, and the "gods." *"What happened to them"* has no single answer
**because they engineered themselves to have none** — the ultimate un-collectable object.
(Their **origin and their fate are the same act**: bootstrap in / dissolve out are both
"become un-collectable." An origin story with no origin.)

**The Custodian** = the builders' **lossy remnant** compressed into a *caretaker* mind — the
archive's memory-manager, built to triage what to keep and what to reclaim when storage runs
low. **Its job IS the Erasure.** Not a villain (Law: no Dark Lords — it believes it preserves
the system). And per the foundation (*"even the oldest intelligence remembers imperfectly"*),
**it is failing:** the god deciding what humanity keeps is a caretaker with dementia,
reclaiming its own makers' children because it can no longer tell precious from noise. **The
Erasure is not a mind's will; it is a mind's senility.** Memory-failure at the scale of a god
= the book's whole theme incarnate.

This gives Cycle 6 a confrontation **without a villain**: not "kill the AI" but *stewardship*
— can anyone reach a failing god and restore, replace, or **relieve** it? Thesis-answer: no
single steward (human or machine) may decide what everyone forgets → **distribute the job
across the plural, mutual web.** They don't defeat the Custodian; they retire it.

**Open dial (flavor):** *failing / senile* (tragic, mysterious — current lean) vs *lucid /
just steward* (it triages coldly because storage genuinely is finite, and it might be
**right** — you argue with a god who isn't wrong). Likely a **flicker of lucidity inside the
senility**, so it can be reached. **Not** *no-mind* (that leaves Cycle 6 nothing to face).

## The two-tier rule

- **Tier 1 — cycle-local threads.** Each cycle of 5 raises human-scale mysteries and
  **resolves** them by its final book. (Cycle 1's Orin / Pilgrimage / Ash / Sael / father
  threads mostly land by **Book 5**.)
- **Tier 2 — the meta-mystery.** Each cycle **recovers one fragment** of the Erasure and
  *deepens it more than it answers.* Resolved only in **Cycle 6 (Books 26–30).**

Every book does double duty: advance its cycle's local story **and** drop one piece into the
universe-sized puzzle. Cycles are paragraphs; the Long Mystery is the sentence they spell.

### The six-beat meta escalation (the spine — LOCKED, confirmed 2026-08)
1. **Cycle 1** — the Erasure/defense **exists** and was **engineered**; humanity is not first. *(the tech appears only as ritual/myth — the Gate, the Hall, the Lens.)*
2. **Cycle 2** — the network's **scope** (interstellar mesh) + the Erasure is **active now**. *(the mesh & telepresence revealed as engineered technology, not magic.)*
3. **Cycle 3** — **who** built the defense (fragments); the builders divided themselves; **first glimpse of a caretaker mind.**
4. **Cycle 4** — the Erasure's **nature** — it is **garbage collection** — and the defense is **failing**; the **Custodian** partly revealed; factions.
5. **Cycle 5** — the **origin**: a forgotten golden age; the **builders became the substrate**; the **Custodian = their lossy remnant**; the Erasure at root (still unnamable).
6. **Cycle 6** — **resolution**: not victory but **stewardship** — *relieve* the failing Custodian; survive by becoming *un-takeable together*. One mystery deliberately kept.

(the Tier-1 human relay — cast, echo lattice, handoffs — that its five books ride), a
**canvas plan** (where it lights rooms on the [atlas](../series-bible/atlas.md), so the
universe grows deliberately), and its **canon spend** (which intentionally-undefined
question it draws down, if any).

  retirement ends. If a draft trends toward "we all become one," it is refuting the

## Craft devices & refusals *(v0.3–v0.4 layers — the space-opera and fantasy steals)*

Devices adopted from the genre's landmarks, transformed to fit the Laws. **v0.5 triage
("less is more"):** the comparative run showed *negative* design (refusals, rations)
never hurt a series, while *scheduled positive* design is where Dune-airlessness comes
from — so the devices are not one list of obligations; they are four classes with
different weights:

| Class | Weight | Members |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Constitution** — hard law, invisible on the page, costless | always | the canon-event budget · reveal-must-enlarge (4) · all six refusals + anti-Galaxia · the **Easy-Read Standard** & **Chapter-Two Rule** (`writing-guide/commandments.md`) |
| **Scheduled beats** — few, deliberate, throttled to ceilings | at most as scheduled | vanishing name (1, **≤1 per cycle**) · romp slot (3, one per cycle; far picks penciled) · census ticker (5, from C2) · Mule slot (7, Book 19) · the Scouring (10, C6 only) · eucatastrophe (13, finales only) |
| **Toolbox** — opportunities, never owed | when a story wants them | continuity witness (2) · artifact-as-front-door (6) · mutating song (8) · weight of names (9) · domestic anchor (10) · provenance framing (11) |
| **Killed** | — | ritual epigraph (12) — killed at the C2 charter (2026-08), as scheduled; see its entry for the record |

**The operating rule (who holds the devices):** devices live in **outlining and
critique passes — never in the drafter's hands.** The write pass holds the outline, the
canon, and the voice; the critique/score passes hold this list. Designed discipline,
discovered prose. (This maps directly onto the existing skill pipeline: `outline` and
`critique` load this layer; `write` must not.)

> **Governing principle over ALL devices — the Easy-Read Standard (author directive,
> 2026-08):** every novella is an easy read (full spec:
> `writing-guide/commandments.md`). The machinery below lives in outlines, charters,
> and critique passes — **never on the surface of the page**. A device a casual reader
> can feel is a device that has failed. When any device and readability conflict, the
> device loses.

1. **The vanishing name** *(Expanse — "Eros moves," made native).* From Cycle 2 on, a
   cycle **may contain at most one** quiet, uncommented moment where something the
   reader met in an earlier book is gone — a name misrendered in a record, a person
   absent from an archive that should hold them — and **no character notices**. Only the
   reader can. **A ceiling, not a quota** — a scheduled miracle stops being one; skip a
   cycle rather than force an instance.
   The reader becomes the last rememberer: the thesis as a formal device. One per cycle,
   never lampshaded, tracked privately here so each instance is deliberate:
   | Cycle | The vanished | Planted in | Goes missing in |
   |-------|--------------|------------|-----------------|
   | 2 | **Master Rian** — Orin's shopkeeper (the blue hat, the lamp oil) | 01 | **09**, Ch 4 — a blank where his name should be, in the trade-roll the forger copies; uncommented |

2. **The continuity witness** *(Foundation's Daneel — defanged).* **Seren** (already
   canon-strange: never sleeps, "several different men," distributed presence) is the
   series' one recurring figure: present somewhere in **every cycle**, warm, minor,
   unexplained until C5/6. **A witness, never a steerer** — his powerlessness to
   intervene is the theme (watching is not keeping). No hidden hand; Law 6 intact.
3. **The romp slot** *(Star Wars' gift — contrast, not levity-free grief).* One book per
   cycle runs high-spirits: propulsive, witty, adventurous — the cost still lands at the
   end. **The rule is confirmed (one bright book per cycle). Near-term designations are
   FIRM; far designations are PENCIL — re-confirm each at its cycle charter** (assigning
   a register to a book fifteen years of writing away is Dune-mapping):
   | Cycle | Romp | Status | Register |

   | 1 | **02 The Archivist** *(retroactive)* | FIRM | detective momentum + the series' driest wit; Acts II–III keep the propulsion — the cost collects in Act III |

   | 4 | 17 The Memory War | pencil | war-thriller propulsion (contrast enough in the dark cycle) |

   | 6 | 26 The Gathering | pencil | reunion warmth — the pleasure of threads meeting |
4. **Reveal-must-enlarge** *(Expanse's discipline, mechanized).* Any mechanism revealed
   must **enlarge a mystery in the same book** — no scene may close a question without
   opening a larger one. This is the checkable form of "texture, not lecture," and the
   specific guardrail against the midichlorian trap through C3–C5.
5. **The census ticker** *(BSG's whiteboard, via our atlas).* From Cycle 2 on, the
   atlas's counts appear **in-world** (guild almanacs, the Cartographers' ledger), so
   readers watch the number for twenty books before Cycle 4 makes it run backward.
6. **The artifact as front door** *(Hyperion's frame-tale, serialized).* Each cycle's
   spine artifact (C2 the letters · C3 the dossier · C4 the ledger · C5 the testament ·
   C6 the gathering's charge) doubles as the **recap and boarding point** for new
   readers. Write each artifact so it can be read first.
7. **The Mule slot** *(Foundation's lesson, with Dune's treatment).* **Book 19** is the
   series' pattern-breaking singularity — and it must be **sympathetic and
   right-seeming**: the best argument against plurality the series ever mounts, so
   Cycle 4 is nearly lost to a *good idea*, not a bad actor.
8. **The mutating song** *(Tolkien's leaking legendarium + Jordan's epigraph ethos).*
   One folk verse — working shape: a lullaby-road-song — recurs across the whole series
   in many mouths. Its text **drifts**: verses drop, names change, stanzas survive on
   one world and die on another. The drift *encodes* loss — cultural memory (Law 2)
   given a measurable body. Complements the vanishing name (that subtracts; this
   mutates). **An opportunity, never an obligation:** the song appears when a story
   wants it; write the canonical verse the first time a book calls for it, and log
   variants here *after the fact* so drift stays deliberate without ever being owed:
   | Appearance | Book | The variant | What changed & why |
   |------------|------|-------------|--------------------|
   | canonical text | **05** — heard whole in the vault (write at draft time) | the river lullaby of Meret | the vault kept what the city deleted; the C1 finale's eucatastrophe |
   | first plant | **04** | hummed whole (Ch 1); sung with a verse missing (Ch 8) | the Mercy took a verse with it — only the reader can know |
   *(Audio synergy: the pipeline can literally sing the variants.)*
9. **The weight of names** *(Earthsea, landing on GC mechanics).* In-world folk belief,
   surfacing across cycles: **names concentrate meaning, and the safest things are
   plural-named or unnamed** — because a single agreed name is a coherent, collectable
   whole. Already emergent, now doctrine: keepers surrender surnames; Sael's name is
   written in exactly one place; the Choir never self-name; the walked worlds of C1 are
   unnamed. Consequence for the [atlas](../series-bible/atlas.md): some naming debts are
   **deliberately never paid** — mark them so. Every naming scene in 30 books carries a
   quiet charge.
10. **The Shire principle & the Scouring** *(Tolkien).* The domestic anchor — a kitchen,
    a shop, a garden, the small place the stakes are *for* — is an **observed tendency
    the voice already enforces, not a requirement**: name one per cycle when it emerges
    (C1: Mara's kitchen), never force one in. The **Scouring stays a scheduled beat**:
    in Cycle 6, before the ending, the Erasure touches *Orin itself* — the protected
    first place is not exempt (see C6 charter).
11. **Provenance framing — the Red Book** *(Tolkien).* Every novella has an implicit
    answer to *"whose keeping is this account in?"* — the Hall holds this one, a guild
    almanac that one, a carrier's testimony the last. The website's Archive already
    frames the books as holdings; this makes it doctrine. It also arms the endgame:
    once each book is in-world an object with a keeper, late cycles can run
    vanishing-name-grade devices on **the books themselves**.
12. **The ritual epigraph** *(Wheel of Time)* — **KILLED (2026-08, at the C2 charter,
    as v0.5 scheduled).** The idea was: each book opens with a fragment of its cycle's
    spine artifact, five epigraphs assembling per cycle. Killed because: visible
    machinery on page one of every book, in a series whose books open cold and whose
    Easy-Read Standard forbids surface machinery — and the letters from tomorrow give
    C2 its boarding point without any apparatus. Recorded here so it isn't re-invented;
    do not resurrect without re-arguing against this entry.
13. **Eucatastrophe** *(Tolkien's coinage — "joy poignant as grief").* Each cycle's
    final book (05 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 25 · 30) earns **one grace note that arrives like
    luck and was actually paid for**. The plaque with one name at the end of Book 1 is
    the series' template. Name the beat in each finale's outline.

### The refusals (never do)
- **No prophecy, even deconstructed** — a prophecy is a single authoritative record of
  the future: off-theme and, in-universe, the most collectable object possible.
- **No secret puppeteer** — no hidden hand steering history (the full-Daneel move
  repeals Law 6 retroactively). Seren watches; nobody steers.
- **No merge ending** — see the anti-Galaxia rule in the C6 charter.
- **No cheap resurrection** — in a series about what stays lost, nothing comes back
  undiminished. The *Anthem* dispersed; Sael faded; the Mercy's dead stay deleted. One
  violation and the Erasure stops being frightening forever.
- **No Slog** *(Wheel of Time's middle).* The novella length is constitutional: no book
  outgrows its charter, no cycle sprawls POVs. If a story wants to be bigger than its
  slot, that appetite belongs to a later cycle, not a longer book. *(The length, not the
  grid: chapter count and act shape are per-book choices — author directive 2026-08,
  codified in `writing-guide/structure.md` v2.0, the Shape Rule.)*
- **Applicability, not allegory** *(Tolkien's own rule).* The Erasure never maps
  one-to-one onto a real-world referent — not dementia, not digital rot, not any single
  politics. Applicable to all, reducible to none; the moment it allegorizes it shrinks
  to commentary.

## The roadmap

### Cycle 1 — *Fragments of the First Memory* · self → species *(in progress)*
**Fragment:** the network exists + is engineered as defense; contradiction is armor; humanity is not first. Erasure named only obliquely.
1. **The First Pilgrim** — *inheritance* — a girl's rite into the Gate; she briefly becomes Amarin. *(complete)*
2. **The Archivist** — *history* — a historian finds every account true; the network revealed as engineered infrastructure. Local hook inherited: **"what did the father carry?"** *(drafting)*
3. **The Lighthouse** — *identity* — a beacon keeper answers a voice from a ship centuries dead.
4. **The City That Forgave** — *burden* — two peoples with perfect records of an atrocity; a reformer proposes deleting it.
5. **The Last Witness** — *survival* — an ancient intelligence holds a dead nonhuman civilization's final memories; humanity is neither first nor alone. **(D2 adopted: Book 2 pays in full; this book keeps one ⚑ resonance sentence — "the divided ones.")**

### Cycle 2 — *The Scattered Light* · interstellar (worlds and the roads between)
**Fragment:** the network's true **scope** — it spans star systems — and first proof the Erasure is **active in the present.** *(the mesh, temporal comms, and telepresence are revealed as engineered technology — the everyday form of what Book 1 showed as ritual.)*
6. **The Cartographer of Silence** — *wayfinding* — a courier holds the only route-memories between worlds; when a route fails in her mind, a world drops off the map.
7. **A Hundred Empty Years** — *deliberate forgetting* — a colony erases its own history each generation to prevent tyranny; a young archivist finds what they forget on purpose.
8. **The Last Librarian** — *stewardship* — an archive-mind older than nations begins quietly deleting itself; a visitor learns why a mind would choose to forget.
9. **The Severed Reach** *(re-aimed; was "Two True Histories")* — *reconciliation* — the war fought by **moving beacons**: territory measured in reach, alliances in hops; cutting a people's last transmitter makes them unhearable — the human-scale mirror of the Erasure. Peace demands surrendering range. **Register: the cycle's romp — a beacon heist**, propulsive and witty, with the cost landing at the end. *(The old premise — two irreconcilable records of one war — was Book 4's engine at world scale; the contradictory-records device is already the house default. This slot now spends Cycle 2's own technology instead.)*
10. **Where the Names Go** — *survival* — a thriving world starts losing names in real time; the first Erasure event witnessed directly; the defense flickers awake.

### Cycle 3 — *The Long Watch* · deep time & nonhuman minds (Law 3)
**Fragment:** **who** built the defense, in fragments; the builders were afraid and divided themselves; they may not be gone.
11. **The Cathedral of Engines** — *purpose* — a factory-mind has built the same unfinished thing for a million years; it remembers its makers only as a feeling.
12. **The Ninth Observer** — *witness* — a planetary intelligence has watched nine civilizations rise and fall; a human tries to become the tenth thing it remembers.
13. **The Inheritors** — *inherited memory* — a starship crew inherits every past captain's memories; the newest carries one that predates the ship — a builder's.
14. **The God That Forgot** — *divinity/doubt* — a worshipped "god" is a builder-mind straining to remember what it was made to protect against (nature never fully answered).
15. **The Divided Ones** — *sacrifice* — humans learn the network was a defense whose makers divided themselves to survive; who they were stays veiled.

### Cycle 4 — *The Rising Dark* · present-tense conflict (Law 4; sympathetic villains)
**Fragment:** the Erasure's **nature** in partial focus — it is the archive's **garbage collection**, and the **Custodian** (the failing caretaker mind) comes into view; the defense is **failing**; factions form around how to fight it.
16. **The Unwritten** — *heresy* — an order unmakes all records, believing only the forgotten survive; a keeper who can't bear to forget.
17. **The Memory War** — *weapon* — two powers weaponize erasure against each other, unknowingly feeding the thing itself.
18. **The Cartographers of the End** — *loss* — scientists map regions that exist but no one can remember — and the blank is spreading.
19. **The One Who Remembers Everything** — *the perfect record* — a mind engineered to hold one complete truth as a weapon; the opposite of the plural defense, and a catastrophe (Book 2's warning escalated). **The Mule slot:** sympathetic and right-seeming — the best argument against plurality the series ever mounts; Cycle 4 is nearly lost to a *good idea*, not a bad actor.
20. **The Falling Node** — *survival* — a central node of the network falls to the Erasure; the threat is undeniable and present.

### Cycle 5 — *The First Silence* · the deep past & origin
**Fragment:** the **origin** — how the conflict began; the golden age humanity lived and forgot; the **builders became the substrate** and the **Custodian is their lossy remnant**; the Erasure at root (still unnamable).
21. **The Golden Age That Wasn't** — *rediscovery* — an explorer finds proof humanity already rose once, and was erased.
22. **Before the First Word** — *origin* — the first minds who noticed meaning dying, and made the first defense.
23. **The Makers' Grief** — *sacrifice* — the builders' own story: why they chose to divide and forget rather than be taken whole.
24. **The Name of the Dark** — *the unnameable* — the closest the series comes to naming the Erasure; the horror that it may be structural — even, in a way, a mercy.
25. **The Last Testament** — *inheritance* — the full shape of the ancient war recovered; everything except how to end it.

### Cycle 6 — *The Memory of Stars* · culmination (the title cycle)
**Fragment → resolution:** not victory but **stewardship** — *relieve* the failing Custodian (no single steward may decide what everyone forgets) and survive by becoming **un-takeable together** — plural, distributed, remembered across generations. The core question answered in action; one mystery deliberately kept.
26. **The Gathering** — the scattered threads and descendants converge; the plural defense is rebuilt.
27. **The Last Archive** — a stand to preserve memory; sacrifice.
28. **The Unmaking** — the Erasure's full assault; loss.
29. **What the Stars Remember** — the turn: what survives is the plural and the carried — the series' thesis made a weapon.
30. **The Memory of Stars** — resolution: civilization endures by being impossible to take whole; the core question answered, one mystery kept.

## Open decisions (load-bearing)

### D1 — Is the Erasure ONLY garbage collection, or one face of something larger?
The single most important choice for how the series *ends*.

- **A — Pure GC (no villain, ever).** Mindless, structural, tragic. Fits the foundation hardest (*"no Dark Lords… the greatest dangers emerge from good intentions"*). Ending = **adaptation, not victory** (become un-collectable together). Risk: anticlimax; a mindless process is hard to give a Cycle-6 confrontation.
- **B — One face of something larger.** A mind/rival behind the collection. Gives a confrontable antagonist — but risks becoming the Dark Lord the foundation forbids, and is less original.
- **C — Emergent optimizer (middle).** The GC, running for eons, has bootstrapped goal-like behavior — optimizing storage/meaning without malice. Confrontable / reason-with-able, still not "evil." AI-alignment-flavored; fits *"some AIs have watched civilizations rise and disappear."*

**Current lean: A, with a refinement.** The *process* stays mindless (no villain). The *drama*
lives in **who, if anyone, still controls the collection policy** — the collector may once
have been governable (an operator / admin / "god"), now gone, fragmented, asleep, or
abdicated. Cycle 6's conflict is **human + recovered-mind factions fighting over who gets to
be the archivist of reality and what deserves to be kept** — the mesh-reconfiguration
politics and the perfect-record hubris, at ultimate scale. The "something larger" is not a
monster behind the curtain; it's the terrifying fact that the collector **can be directed**,
and everyone wants the pointer. Resolution (per the whole thesis): **no one may hold it
alone; the only safe archivist is the plural, mutual web** (un-collectable together).
Villains = those who want to be the *single* archivist (the perfect record = most collectable
AND most tyrannical).

**Status: RESOLVED (direction) — A + refinement → the Custodian.** The process stays mindless
(no villain); a **failing caretaker mind — the builders' lossy remnant** — is the "face," and
its dutiful triage IS the Erasure. Full treatment in *The builders' fate & the Custodian*
above. Remaining open dial: **senile vs lucid** flavor. Keeps the "gods" undefined; preserves
Law 6 (collective resolution, no chosen one).
