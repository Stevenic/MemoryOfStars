## 0. Discipline (hold this)
- **Hard SF: the universe obeys known physics.** No FTL *matter*, no violating relativity. Wormholes are GR-permitted (exotic matter is the honest caveat). "Instant comms" is treated honestly as a *channel to the past* (FTL signalling = time travel), governed by self-consistency (Novikov).
- **Texture, not lecture.** Physics is the *vocabulary of wonder and plausibility*, never the load-bearing explanation. Show a Dyson shell; never explain who built it or how it's powered. The deepest mechanics stay **undefined** (see the intentionally-undefined list).
- **Author's provenance:** the two cores (temporal mesh + telepresence) are the author's, conceived 1985 at age 16.

## 1. The keystone
**A "memory" is a *volumetric recording of an experience*, stored in the mesh, that can be
RE-ENTERED (walked through) — not merely recalled.** This is the atom the whole system runs
on, and *what the Erasure garbage-collects.*

## 2. The core primitive — temporal micro-wormholes
- Carry **information, not matter.** Once created, a wormhole is a channel that can send data back in time — **only as far as its own creation** (real physics: Morris–Thorne–Yurtsever).
- **Created in pairs** (entanglement-*flavored*); one is **physically ferried (sublight) to the destination** → a **beacon.** One transmitter is one-directional; bidirectional = a pair.
- **Self-consistency (Novikov):** the channel can only carry what was always going to be true. **You can hear, but not change.**
- **Exotic matter is scarce** ⇒ transmitters are precious; **cannot be made** by the current age, only **moved.** Total count only **decreases** (a finite, dwindling inheritance).

## 3. The system layers (all one inherited system)
- **Temporal Transmitter → the mesh.** Beacons form a routed, multi-hop graph (~10–20 per node). **Range = age**; a route's reach is capped by its *newest* beacon (oldest = deepest, most fought-over). **Reconfiguration:** can't make, only *move* — civilizations are surgeons of the mesh; cutting a rival's beacon erases them from the conversation (human-scale mirror of the Erasure).
- **Temporal Processor.** A wormhole looped in *time* locally → "instant" computation, but **self-consistent problems only** (Deutsch/Aaronson: CTCs ⇒ PSPACE). Paradox-problems have no solution → the machine hangs/fails/destroys itself. Yields **bootstrap/orphan knowledge** (answers with no origin — maybe the network itself).
- **Telepresence.** Two modes on the same tech:
  - **Real-time** — inhabit a *live* body (android, or a person) across the mesh. Presence, not travel.
  - **Simulation mode** — inhabit a *recorded memory* (a volumetric recording): free to explore, not to change (the fixed past = self-consistency again).
- **Reality capture ("portal, not screen").** Ambient volumetric scanning; the near-future *root* of the whole tree. This is *what makes memories* (and what fills storage).

## 4. The gradient (us → the ancients) — the reader's on-ramp
1. **Near-future:** local volumetric telepresence + ambient capture; screens obsolesce. (Propulsion register for this rung: the 2026→3026 on-ramp — reuse, torches, gram-probes at 0.1c, first seed-ferries; see `science-and-concepts.md`.)
2. **Mid:** android inhabitation + interstellar telepresence over the mesh.
3. **Ancient/mythic:** the **Gate** — telepresence across space, *time, and death.*
→ *"portal to the game"* and *"portal to another life"* are the **same technology at different scales.**

## 5. Canon retro-fits (Books 1–2, consistent — do not contradict the text)
- **The Gate** = an ancient, extreme telepresence/inhabitation node. **The Pilgrimage** = that system decayed into ritual/myth. (Lyra "fell forward into another life" = inhabitation.)
- **The anchors** (Sael built; "no longer remembered what they held") = ancient temporal-mesh **beacons.**
- **The House of Memories** = a place running **sim-mode** (Lyra piloted Mara's recorded memory).
- **The Resonance Lens** (Book 2) = a **sim-mode viewer** — walk the recorded memories in an object ("objects remember people" = objects as capture points). The **Hall of Names** = an archive/index node of the mesh.
- **Book 1's uncanny "knowing"** (the woman in blue; the *seventh* bell; "someone should have been there") = **temporal leakage / future observers** bleeding into the past (the "crowded past").
- **Seren** (never sleeps, appears/vanishes, "several different men") = **distributed / telepresent** presence.
- **"Cities losing sequence"** = **consistency collapse** — GC deleting a load-bearing *looped* memory unstitches local cause & effect.

## 6. Constraints that keep it from breaking the universe
- **Matter still can't move.** Telepresence = *presence without reach*: you can visit a far world through a body but never *go*, never bring anything back. Preserves Law 1's loneliness & Law 7's wonder.
- **Mesh-only + bandwidth-limited + latency-sensitive** ⇒ interstellar presence is precious; the frontier is dark.
- **Beacon-transport is sublight** ⇒ new links need slow voyages; the mesh is *a map of every journey ever made.* (This is what *mechanically preserves Law 1.*)
- **Self-consistency** ⇒ no paradox-heroics; you receive the past/future like a memory you can't alter.

## 7. Mesh geography — where the web falls in the galaxy (2026-08)

**The mesh is the boundary of the knowable, and it is small.** ~300 billion stars;
the mesh has ever touched on the order of 10⁵ systems — one star in three million,
on one spur of one arm. Everything else is night: visible by light, outside the
conversation. Structure, inside-out:

- **The deep core:** the works-shell, 23–53 ly around the old zero (Hearth) — the
  makers' original beacons. Range = age makes them the deepest channels in existence
  (~1M years of reach) and therefore the most fought-over objects in history.
- **The living web:** humanity's regrowth — a filament network running ~2,000 ly
  along the spur (the settled reach), built over the settled ages by sublight
  ferry, one journey at a time. **11,406 nodes answering** at series present, down
  from its peak (Cycle 4's decrements are mesh contraction, witnessed).
- **The walked pocket:** the ~130 ly the thirty books actually light.
- **The dead trunk lines:** makers'-era long-haul filaments run *out* of the spur
  toward the inter-arm dark — the builders had far more capacity than the current
  age. Where they went is never answered on any page. (Exploration fuel: the roads
  out of the neighborhood exist, and they are dark.)

**"All known civilizations hang off the mesh" — yes, in exactly three ways:**
1. **Answering through it** (alive, in the conversation): humanity; the Selt —
   whose entire Book 9 stake is that their one aging link *is* their existence.
2. **Remembered by it** (dead, held): the Choir via the vault, the makers via the
   works and the strata, the prior human rise via the store, Aulis's nine rises
   via the Observer. The dead are known because the mesh remembers them.
3. **Being it** (the poured): the makers became the substrate — part of the mesh
   *is* a former civilization. Seren hangs off the mesh in the most literal sense
   there is.

**One exception in thirty books:** the far-sky signature (01 → 30) — the only
signal that ever arrives without a relay, from a place with no coordinates in any
frame. The exception is the point: it proves the boundary by crossing it once, at
the very end, as grace.

Silence therefore has two causes — **severed or collected — indistinguishable from
inside the web.** (Book 6's map of silences is this doctrine, dramatized.) And the
Drake shortfall (cosmology: tens of thousands expected, two or three known) is
partly the Erasure's thoroughness, partly the web's smallness — the series never
says in what proportion.

**Scope & totals (quantified 2026-08 — full model in [`mesh-model.yml`](mesh-model.yml)):**
the makers built the mesh over **~400,000 years** (shell → association fill → trunk
lines, the trunks being 100,000-year projects each), finishing ~1.05M years ago; its
total history to series present is **~1.4 million years**, tenanted three times
(makers → the golden age, collected ~200k ago → the current rise). Humanity's
regrowth is the last ~30,000 years at ferry-wave speed (~0.22 ly/yr with plateaus),
consolidated under the Bell for the final ~7,700. **Transmitters created, ever:
~1.1 million — all by the makers.** Disposition now: ~137,000 in living service
(11,406 nodes × ~12), ~560,000 dark but extant, ~400,000 destroyed or collected.
The count only goes down; the beacon war was fought over single-digit percentages
of a dead civilization's estate.

**Generation-algorithm notes (the exploration pass, upcoming):** grow the web as
*accumulated journeys* — the mesh is literally a map of every journey ever made:
1. Seed the works-shell at the zero; makers'-era expansion phase (deep, vast,
   mostly dark now).
2. Human regrowth over the settled ages: preferential attachment along stellar
   density; hop lengths mostly 2–15 ly (sublight ferry practicality), rare long
   hauls; node degree ~5–10 links (10–20 transmitters, one per direction).
3. Overlay history as edits: the beacon war's cuts and treaty caps (09), the
   Accords (17), Cycle 4's decrements, collected subtrees as dark branches.
4. Edges carry **age ⇒ depth**; a route's reach is capped by its newest beacon.
5. Deterministic seed; renders at any Toll epoch; site data stays anonymous until
   read-to-reveal wiring (same discipline as `galaxy.json` / `stars-named.yml`).

## 8. Travel — who could go where, and how (2026-08)

**Nobody has FTL travel. Ever.** The ladder below is entirely sublight, and the
summit civilization traveled *least* — the deepest tech tree in the galaxy belongs
to a culture that decided matter stays home and presence goes instead.

| Civilization | Interplanetary | Interstellar | Primary mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| The nine rises (Aulis) | Partial — the highest rises touched orbit and moons, briefly | Never | Chemical/early-atomic rockets; nine ladders, none tall enough |
| The Selt | Yes — three settlements in one system, small fusion craft | **No ships of their own** — their relay *is* their interstellar existence | In-system hulls + the mesh; the purest case of "the link is the civilization" |
| Humanity (current rise) | Routine — tethers (Sowen's tether-port), tugs, freighters, fusion torch | Rare, costly, slow: 0.3–0.5c ferries and colony hulls along the roads; decades per crossing | **The mesh for presence, hulls for matter.** People visit by wire; cargo and colonists pay the real price |
| The golden age | Same kit, more of it | More roads lit, more ferries running — remembered as easier than it was (B21's asterisk) | As above; their gold was partly the mesh's, not theirs |
| The Choir | Trivial for them | Yes — slow **choral ships**, crossings measured in generations and *sung through*: many-in-one crews made centuries bearable | Plural-mind voyaging; their mind-craft was their drive solution |
| The makers | Dyson-scale industry (the ring at Cinder, moon-sized forges) | Automated ferry fleets on beam corridors; 100,000-year logistics run by made minds | **Telepresence.** Per capita, the least-traveled civilization in history — bodies stayed home a million years; only freight ever really moved |

**Roads, precisely:** a road = a maintained corridor — beacon chain for navigation
and comms, beam stations and depots for boost and braking. Roads make crossings
*survivable and navigable*, not fast: physics still charges years-to-decades
between stars. Off-road is rocket-only (0.01–0.05c) — hence "months of risk" for
short unroaded leaps and generation-bets for real ones. (The C6 season-scale
journey tension stands as logged — presence-vs-travel resolves it at drafting.)

### The propulsion stack — how anything actually moves

**Governing fact (real physics doing theme's work):** a self-contained rocket
cannot reach 0.3–0.5c — the mass ratios are obscene. High speed therefore comes
only from **infrastructure that pushes you**: in this universe, *speed is not a
possession, it is a road.* A hull alone is slow; a hull on a lit corridor is fast.
Nobody owns velocity — you borrow it, which is why routes are polities, why the
guild matters, and why severance strands worlds. Law 1, enforced by engineering.

**The on-ramp (2026 → 3026 — full chart in
[`science-and-concepts.md`](science-and-concepts.md)):** humanity's own first
starflight millennium converges on this same shape — engines plateau at fusion by
~2200; every gain after is beamers, sails, seed-ferries, corridors. One slow ferry
first, then the lit road: **corridor-building is what starflight *is* under real
physics, for anyone, at any level.** Which is why, ages later, settlers recognized
the makers' mesh on sight — it didn't feel alien; it felt like better roads. They
moved in, and never left.

**In-system (interplanetary):**
- **Orbital tethers** — surface-to-orbit freight without rockets (Sowen's
  tether-port, canon B7). The settled ages' quiet workhorse.
- **Chemical / early-atomic rockets** — the nine rises' ceiling; landers and
  emergencies everywhere else.
- **Fusion torches** — the interplanetary standard: tugs, freighters, couriers
  (Madge's tug, Pell's runs). Days-to-weeks between worlds at sustained milli-g.
- **Solar sails** — the humblest rung and the oldest: pushed by nothing but the
  home star's own light. Slow bulk cargo, station-keeping, and sundiver boosts;
  the first sail every rise builds (the later ladders of Aulis's nine plausibly
  flew them), and the last thing still working when every depot is dark. Patient
  freight for a patient universe.
- **In-system sails on depot beams** — the same hardware, fed: bulk cargo, slow
  and nearly free. **Every sail is a solar sail at heart — a road's beam is just
  an artificial sun, aimed at you.** One craft lineage runs unbroken from a
  primitive rise's first foil to the 0.5c corridor liners; only the light source
  changes.

**Interstellar:**
- **Beam-riding (on-road, 0.3–0.5c)** — the corridor's boost stations light your
  sail outbound; the destination's **braking beam** catches you. No reaction mass
  carried for the cruise. The catch is the whole geopolitics: *you need a live
  depot at both ends.* Going fast is easy; **stopping is the road's real gift.**
- **Magsail braking (the off-road half-answer)** — drag against the interstellar
  medium; years of deceleration, no infrastructure needed. This is how you arrive
  where no beam waits: colony ships launch on a home beam and magsail-brake at
  the far end (Reed → Sowen, 8.8 ly in ~20 years = beam boost + long brake, canon-
  consistent). It is also how you survive a **dead road** — ride the corridor's
  line, bring your own stopping (Madge's stunt, B9).
- **Independent fusion cruise (roadless, 0.02–0.08c)** — hero-runs and the
  desperate; "months of risk" is this drive on a short unroaded leap.
- **Choral ships (the Choir)** — sail-and-torch hybrids at ~0.1c; their real
  drive technology was *crew endurance* — many-in-one minds that made
  generation-crossings bearable and sung-through.
- **The makers' freight (the ceiling)** — pellet-stream corridors: launcher works
  firing fuel/momentum streams that automated ferries catch and burn, laid like
  roadbed across thousands of light-years. The beamways humanity maintains are
  the surviving, dumbed-down descendants. **No antimatter, no exotic drives** —
  the makers' exotic-matter budget went into transmitters, and their answer to
  distance was never a better engine: it was *infrastructure, then telepresence,
  then staying home.*

**Page guard:** none of this is ever lectured. Prose shows tethers, sails, torches,
and the fear of a dead road's far end; the words "beam-riding" and "magsail" stay
in the bible.

### Derelicts of the ancients — yes, and they are the best objects in the universe

Vacuum preserves; a million years is nothing to a cold hull. Classes, most
precious first (generator data in [`mesh-model.yml`](mesh-model.yml)):

1. **Lost ferries** (makers' era, 1.0–1.45M yr old) — beacon-ferries that failed
   mid-corridor. Each carries transmitters **aged since creation**: a
   million-year-deep channel *whose partner mouth is still in the mesh, still
   waiting.* Recovering one doesn't just salvage hardware — it **opens an instant
   link to wherever the ferry left from**, at a depth no living route can match.
   The single most valuable movable object this universe can contain.
2. **Silent arrivals** — systems where the ferry arrived and commissioning failed:
   a hull in-system, a transmitter never linked. The mesh's ancient dead-letter
   list — *the addresses that never answered* — is therefore a literal treasure
   map, sitting in the routing tables the whole time.
3. **Works-swarm hulks** — construction and support hulls around the great works;
   the Cathedral's swarm (B11) is canon's exemplar, already an "honest grave."
4. **Choir crossings** — choral hulls on the old lanes near Lull and their home
   sea; their recordings are interwoven voices (carriage-sensitive — a hull full
   of many-in-one memory is not neutral salvage).
5. **Golden-age hulls** (~200k yr) — human-recognizable, degraded, poignant:
   kitchens in the wreckage.
6. **Recent wrecks** (the *Anthem* class) — residue-bearing; B3 already showed
   what residue does.

**Why they persist, and the mechanic that makes them dangerous:** derelicts are
**off-mesh — beneath the maintenance's notice.** The keeper collects what is in
the conversation; a dark hull answers nothing and is therefore invisible. Board
one and walk its recordings (every derelict is a sim-mode site — objects are
capture points; a dead ferry holds a walkable million-year-old journey) and you
are safe. **Link one — light it up, commission its transmitter — and it enters
the conversation, and the conversation is what gets collected.** Salvage dark or
salvage never: the exploration era's first law.

09's graveyard, 11's swarm). The taxonomy above is Archive/exploration-era
