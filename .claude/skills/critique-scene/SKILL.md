# Critique-scene — are the people present?

One lens: **bodies, eyes, and wants on the page.** Named for the series' first beta
reader; sourced from her actual findings (2026-08, Books 1–2 prose). **Add her future
notes here — this section accretes.** You critique; you do not rewrite — findings go to
`critiques/scene.md` (format: the findings contract in the `critique` skill) for the
`edit` skill to apply.

## Load first

`writing-guide/voice.md` §3 (choreography + conversation layers) and §6 (emotion in the
body, banned templates) · the book's `audio.yml` (per-chapter mood) · character sheets /
bible entries for the cast's repertoires and speech engines.

## The rubric

**Presence (the founding Sarah findings):**
- **Do the speakers see each other?** A conversation can be line-perfect and still 2D —
  no gaze vectors, no nonverbal turns, beats that only attribute. At least one
  meaningful look or gesture-as-reply per scene beat; flag absence *and* tic-spam (the
  overcorrection — perpetual nodding, eyebrow theater).
- **Gesture intent:** a gesture mentioned, not rendered ("she gestured at the door") —
  render the vector and intent, or cut.
- **Gesture economy:** never the same gesture twice in a scene; gestures from each
  character's established repertoire; a gesture must change the meaning of its line.
- **Prop realism:** would the object actually behave/be operated that way? (Founding
  instance: *"do you wind a compass?"* — you don't; compasses are tapped.) Hunt
  physical-action errors on props, tools, hands.
- **Hook temperature:** foreshadowing that is blunt and flat at once = narrator emphasis
  without embodiment. Hooks go in the hands — an object given wrong-weighted attention,
  a glance that snags; never the camera underlining itself.
- **The blocking test:** could two actors stage the scene from the prose alone?

**Emotion (voice.md §6):**
- **In the body, never named** — hunt the banned templates: *"[emotion]
  crossed/filled/entered his face"*; *"[emotion] rose/arrived/came"* with the feeling as
  agent; labeled triplets (*"Recognition. Affection. Loss."*); narrator frames (*"The
  answer came too quickly"* and cousins).
- Grief/love oblique; biggest emotions get the shortest sentences; earn sentiment, exit
  fast.

**The conversation layer (voice.md §3):**
- **Cover-the-names test:** attributions stripped, is every speaker identifiable? Flag
  shared tics (a phrase that belongs to everyone characterizes no one).
- **Speech engines:** does each major character's dialogue run on their engine? Lines
  that could belong to anyone are findings.
- **Two agendas:** what does each party want in this conversation? A scene where both
  want the same thing and have it is a report — flag it.
- **No as-you-know:** no character tells another what both already know.
- **The said-instead:** in loaded exchanges, is the true sentence displaced (Gerwig's
  law) — or spoken outright? Emotion said aloud is usually emotion lost.
- **Beats attached:** never more than ~3 clipped exchanges without a body; repeated-reply
  ladders (*"No." / "No."*) varied after one bare repetition.
